Remediation → err-cover-letter-purpose-confusion | Retest → err-cover-letter-purpose-confusion

  1. A. To list all of your work and education history in paragraph form.
    That is the purpose of a resume, not a cover letter. A cover letter tells a targeted story.
  2. B. To introduce yourself and request an interview politely.
    While polite, this is too vague. A cover letter specifically connects your skills to the employer's needs.
  3. ✓ C. To connect your skills and experiences to the specific employer's needs in a professional story.
    Correct! A cover letter is a narrative that shows why you are a great fit for the job.
  4. D. To show off your vocabulary and writing style.
    Writing style matters, but the core purpose is to match your skills to the job, not to impress with language.

补救模块

err-cover-letter-purpose-confusion

The student thinks a cover letter is merely a restatement of the resume or a polite introduction, rather than a targeted narrative that connects their skills to the employer's specific needs.

Explain: A cover letter is a professional story that connects your skills and experiences to the employer's needs. Unlike a resume which lists facts, a cover letter shows why you are a good fit and demonstrates enthusiasm for the specific job. For example, Sam Foster's letter connected his school fundraiser cash handling to retail skills and his basketball experience to an athletic footwear company.

Example: Low-quality: Deirdre's letter with missing info, casual greeting, and irrelevant hobbies. High-quality: Sam Foster's letter with proper header, experience translation, and enthusiasm.

err-cover-letter-greeting-casual

Students may use casual greetings like 'Hello' or generic 'To Whom It May Concern' instead of a specific salutation.

Explain: A cover letter salutation must be specific and professional. Avoid casual greetings like 'Hello' or generic phrases like 'To Whom It May Concern'. Instead, address the hiring manager directly if you know their name, or use 'To the [Role] Hiring Manager at [Company]' if the name is unknown. This shows you have researched the company and are taking the application seriously.

Example: Wrong: 'Hello,' Wrong: 'To Whom It May Concern,' Correct: 'To the Retail Manager Hiring Team at SportChek,' Correct: 'Dear Ms. Johnson,'

err-cover-letter-opening-question

Students think it's acceptable to open with a question like 'Are you hiring?' as a way to engage the reader, not realizing it signals a lack of research and unprofessionalism.

Explain: The opening paragraph of a cover letter must immediately state the exact job title and express genuine enthusiasm for the role. Never open with a question such as 'Are you hiring?' because it shows you haven't researched the company and it sounds unprofessional.

Example: Instead of 'Are you looking for a hardworking employee?', write 'I am excited to apply for the Sales Associate position at your downtown location, and I am confident my customer service experience makes me a great fit.'

err-cover-letter-no-call-to-action

Students think that simply thanking the employer and expressing hope to hear from them is sufficient, forgetting to explicitly and confidently request an interview.

Explain: The closing paragraph of a cover letter must do two things: thank the hiring manager for their time and include a confident call to action requesting an interview. A call to action shows initiative and interest, e.g., 'I look forward to discussing my qualifications further in an interview.' Without it, the letter feels incomplete and passive.

Example: Weak: 'Thank you for your time. I hope to hear from you soon.' Strong: 'Thank you for your time and consideration. I look forward to the opportunity to discuss how my skills align with your team's needs in an interview.'

err-cover-letter-resume-repeat

Students often list their resume experiences in paragraph form without linking them to the employer's needs, making the cover letter a repetitive summary rather than a targeted story.

Explain: A cover letter is not a duplicate of your resume. It is a focused narrative that connects your specific skills and experiences to the requirements of the job. For each experience, explain how it prepared you for the role you're applying for.

Example: For a retail position, instead of saying 'I was team captain of basketball', say: 'As basketball team captain, I developed leadership and communication skills by coordinating schedules and motivating teammates, which I can use to assist customers and support store operations.'

err-cover-letter-file-format

Student thinks Word format is acceptable and does not recognize that PDF is required to preserve formatting.

Explain: Employers require cover letters in PDF format because PDFs keep your layout, fonts, and spacing exactly as you designed them. Word documents can shift formatting when opened on different computers or operating systems, potentially making your letter look unprofessional. Always convert your final cover letter to PDF before submitting.

Example: Compare: A Word document with a table might display incorrectly on a hiring manager's screen, while the same file saved as PDF remains perfect. Also, PDFs cannot be accidentally edited, ensuring your work stays original.

err-cover-letter-missing-header

Students may omit or incorrectly order header information, such as forgetting the phone number or using numeric dates.

Explain: The header of a cover letter must include your full name, street address, phone number, professional email, the full written date (e.g., June 5, 2026), and the hiring manager's name, title, company, and address. Each element is required and must appear in this order. A missing phone number or address prevents employers from contacting you.

Example: Jordan Lee 123 Maple Street, Toronto, Ontario M5V 2T6 (416) 555-0198 jordan.lee@email.com June 5, 2026 Ms. Sarah Chen, Hiring Manager ABC Company 456 King Street West, Toronto, Ontario M5V 1K4

err-cover-letter-irrelevant-hobbies

Students think any personal interest can be included in a cover letter, but only experiences that can be translated into workplace skills relevant to the job should be included.

Explain: Cover letters should only include experiences that can be connected to the job's requirements. Irrelevant hobbies like video gaming, unless directly relevant, weaken your application. Focus on transferable skills from school, clubs, or volunteer work.

Example: Bad: 'I enjoy playing video games.' (no connection to retail). Good: 'Organizing a school bake sale helped me develop cash handling and customer service skills.' (directly relevant).
