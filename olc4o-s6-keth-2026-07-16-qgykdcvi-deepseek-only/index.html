补救模块

err-inference-guessing

Students think inference is just random guessing without using textual clues or background knowledge.

Explain: Making inferences combines textual clues (explicit information) with background knowledge to understand implied meaning. It is not random guessing; it is evidence-based reasoning. For example, if you see a woman with scars and flowers, the scars are a clue that she has survived hardship, and the flowers suggest she appreciates life. You use your knowledge of what scars and flowers typically mean to infer she is resilient and finds beauty despite pain.

Example: Picture of a woman with scars and flowers: scars indicate survival, flowers indicate appreciation of life, not hiding scars shows strength.

err-inference-only-text

Students may neglect background knowledge and rely only on explicit text clues.

Explain: Making inferences is a two-step process: you start with explicit clues in the text (words, images, details) and then add your own background knowledge to understand what is implied. If you only use the text, you miss the deeper meaning because many implications depend on what you already know about the world.

Example: Look at the woman with scars and flowers. The text tells you she has scars and flowers, but to infer resilience and wisdom, you need background knowledge that scars often result from surviving hardship, and flowers can symbolize appreciation of life. Without that knowledge, you only see a woman with marks and plants.

err-inference-prediction

Students think inference and prediction are the same, or they swap their meanings: they believe inference is about future events and prediction is about hidden meanings.

Explain: Inference draws conclusions from current textual clues and background knowledge to understand implied meaning. Prediction, on the other hand, is an educated guess about what will happen next in the text. The key difference is time: inference is about what is implied now; prediction is about what might happen later. Both use evidence, but inference focuses on present implicit meaning, while prediction looks forward.

Example: Consider the childhood narrative: 'One cold evening in autumn, I ran away from the second house from the corner.' An inference from this clue is that the narrator lives in a northern climate (because of the cold evening) and might have a difficult family situation (because they ran away). A prediction would be that the narrator will encounter a neighbor or get lost next. So inference explains what is not directly stated about the current situation; prediction guesses what comes next.

err-translation-acceptable

Students may think that using machine translation tools or directly translating from Chinese to English is acceptable. They need to understand that such practices result in a score of 0 and that they must write in their own words.

Explain: Using machine translation or direct Chinese-to-English translation is prohibited. Any assignment submitted with such translations will receive a score of 0. This policy is in place to ensure you develop your own English writing skills. If you receive a 0 for this reason, you may email the teacher to request a resubmission.

Example: For example, if you copy a sentence from a Chinese article and use Google Translate to convert it to English and submit it, that assignment will receive a 0.
