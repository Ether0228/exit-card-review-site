      补救模块

      err-distance-displacement-scalar-vector

      Students may confuse scalar distance with vector displacement, ignoring direction or treating distance as having direction.

      Explain: Distance is a scalar quantity that measures the total path length traveled, regardless of direction. Displacement is a vector quantity that measures the change in position from a reference point, including direction. For example, walking 500 m east then 1200 m west gives a distance of 1700 m (total path) but a displacement of 700 m west (final position relative to start).

      Example: A student walks 500 m east from home to school, then 1200 m west from school to library. Distance = 500 m + 1200 m = 1700 m. Displacement = (500 m east) + (1200 m west) = -700 m east = 700 m west.

      err-speed-velocity-interchange

      Students may use total distance instead of net displacement when calculating average velocity, or disregard direction entirely.

      Explain: Speed is a scalar: it only measures how fast something is moving, based on total distance traveled. Velocity is a vector: it measures how fast and in what direction, based on displacement (change in position). Average velocity = displacement / time, while average speed = distance / time. For a round trip that returns to start, displacement is zero, so velocity is zero even if speed is positive.

      Example: A dog runs 80 m west, then 27 m east. Total distance = 107 m, so speed depends on time. Displacement = 53 m west. Velocity would be 53 m west divided by time, not 107 m divided by time.

      err-graph-slope-interpretation

      Students often think a negative slope on a position-time graph means the object is slowing down, when actually a constant negative slope indicates constant velocity in the negative direction. They may also confuse changes in slope (curvature) with the slope itself.

      Explain: On a position-time graph, the slope of the line represents the velocity. A straight line means constant velocity; the sign of the slope indicates direction (positive = forward, negative = backward). Curved lines (changing slope) indicate acceleration: increasing slope means speeding up, decreasing slope means slowing down.

      Example: A dog runs 80 m west in 10 seconds. The position-time graph is a straight line from (0,0) to (10,-80). The slope is -8 m/s, meaning constant velocity of 8 m/s westward. A student might think it's slowing down because the position decreases, but constant slope means constant velocity.
