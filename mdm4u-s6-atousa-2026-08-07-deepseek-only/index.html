Q1. A scatter plot of study hours vs test scores yields r = 0.89. What is the most accurate interpretation?

errorId: err-correlation-causation | Bloom: understand | Scaffold: L1

Achievement: Knowledge and Understanding | Action: interpret | OSSD: Analyse, interpret, and draw conclusions from two-variable data using numerical,

Official: D2 | Node: MDM4U-D-OE02 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/math1112currb.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-1

Misconception: High correlation implies causation.

Remediation → err-correlation-causation | Retest → err-correlation-causation

  1. A. Test scores cause study hours to increase.
    Correlation does not imply causation. A high r value indicates a relationship but does not prove one variable causes the other.
  2. ✓ B. There is a strong positive linear relationship between study hours and test scores.
    Correct. r = 0.89 indicates a strong positive linear association.
  3. C. The correlation is weak because 0.89 is less than 1.
    A correlation close to 1 is considered strong, not weak. Remember that |r| > 0.8 generally indicates a strong correlation.
  4. D. 89% of test scores are determined by study hours.
    This confuses r with R². R² is the proportion of variation explained, not r itself.

补救模块

err-correlation-causation

Confusing correlation with causation; believing that a high |r| value proves that changes in one variable cause changes in another.

Explain: The correlation coefficient r measures the strength and direction of a linear relationship between two quantitative variables. It ranges from -1 to 1. A value close to 1 or -1 indicates a strong linear association, but this does not mean that changes in one variable cause changes in the other. There could be third variables or coincidence. Always remember: correlation does not imply causation.

Example: Consider the relationship between femur length and humerus length in vertebrates. The regression line is humerus = -3.6 + 1.2 * femur, with R² = 0.98, indicating a very strong positive linear correlation. However, a longer femur does not cause a longer humerus; both are influenced by overall body size. This shows that even a perfect correlation does not establish causation.

err-rsquared-interpretation

The student thinks R² = 0.85 means 85% of predictions from the regression model are correct, instead of understanding it as '85% of the variation in the dependent variable is explained by the linear model, while 15% is unexplained.'

Explain: The coefficient of determination, R², ranges from 0 to 1 and indicates how well the regression line explains the variability of the dependent variable. A value of 0.85 means 85% of the variation in y is explained by the linear model; the remaining 15% is due to other factors. It does NOT mean the model is correct 85% of the time.

Example: In the bone length regression, humerus = -3.6 + 1.2 * femur, with R² = 0.98. This tells us that 98% of the variation in humerus length is explained by the linear relationship with femur length; only 2% of the variation is left unexplained. It is not a statement about the percentage of correct predictions.

err-extrapolation

Extrapolation is using a regression model to predict values outside the range of the original data. This is risky because the linear relationship may not hold beyond the observed data, and uncertainty increases with distance from the data range.

Explain: A regression equation is built from a set of data. It summarizes the trend only within the range of x-values you observed. When you predict for x-values outside that range, you have no evidence that the linear trend continues. For example, the acid rain model pH = 5.43 – 0.0053·week was based on a limited span of weeks. Predicting pH for week 500 assumes the same rate of decline for a long time, which may not be realistic. Always check the range of your data and avoid extrapolating farther than the data supports.

Example: Suppose pH data were collected for weeks 1 to 50, and the regression equation is pH = 5.43 – 0.0053·week. Predicting the pH at week 45 (inside the range) is reasonable. Predicting the pH at week 500 gives 5.43 – 0.0053(500) = 2.78, but that is extrapolation far beyond the data. We cannot be confident the linear decline continues.

err-residual-pattern

The student thinks that every pattern in a residual plot is a sign that the regression model is completely wrong. In truth, small residuals with no apparent pattern support a good linear fit, while a clear pattern suggests that a non-linear model may be more appropriate.

Explain: Residual = observed y – predicted y. A positive residual means the point lies above the regression line, and a negative residual means it lies below. To assess model fit, we look at all residuals together in a residual plot. If the residuals are small and show no apparent pattern, the linear model fits well. If the residuals show a pattern (for example, a curve), this suggests the relationship may not be linear, so a non-linear model may be better. This does not mean the linear model is 'bad'—it means there may be a better model. In the bone length example, R² = 0.98 and the residuals were small and patternless, confirming a strong linear fit. A curved residual pattern would point toward trying a non-linear model.

Example: In the used car example, the regression line was price = -0.1629*mileage + 38257. For a car with mileage 7583, the predicted price was 26075 but the actual price was 21099, so the residual = 21099 - 26075 = -4765. This negative residual tells us the car sold below the line. One residual alone does not show a pattern; we must examine all residuals together. If they are randomly scattered around zero, a linear model is appropriate; if they form a curve, a non-linear model may be more suitable.
