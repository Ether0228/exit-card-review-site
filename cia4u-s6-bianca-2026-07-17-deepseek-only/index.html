Q1. Which of the following is a key characteristic of a perfectly competitive market?

Achievement: Knowledge and Understanding | Action: interpret | OSSD: Describe various types of market structures and assess their advantages and disa

Official: CC1.2 | Node: ? | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/2015cws11and12.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-1

Misconception: Students may think perfect competition allows product differentiation to gain market power.

Remediation → err-perfect-competition-product-differentiation | Retest → err-perfect-competition-product-differentiation

  1. ✓ A. Firms produce homogeneous (identical) products.
  2. B. Firms differentiate their products to attract customers.
    Perfect competition requires homogeneous products; differentiation is a feature of monopolistic competition.
  3. C. There are high barriers to entry.
    Perfect competition has free entry and exit, not high barriers.
  4. D. Only one firm controls the market.
    That describes a monopoly, not perfect competition.

Q2. A student argues that monopolistic competition is essentially the same as a monopoly because both give firms some control over price. Which response best corrects this misunderstanding?

errorId: err-monopolistic-competition-monopoly | Bloom: analyze | Scaffold: L2

Achievement: Thinking | Action: analyze | OSSD: Describe various types of market structures and assess their advantages and disa

Official: CC1.2 | Node: ? | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/2015cws11and12.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-1

Misconception: Students think monopolistic competition is similar to monopoly because firms have some price control.

Remediation → err-monopolistic-competition-monopoly | Retest → err-monopolistic-competition-monopoly

  1. ✓ A. Monopolistic competition has many competing firms with low entry barriers, while a monopoly has only one firm with extremely high entry barriers.
  2. B. Both have many firms, but monopolistic competition has differentiated products.
    Monopoly does not have many firms; it has one dominant firm.
  3. C. Monopolistic competition has high entry barriers, just like a monopoly.
    Monopolistic competition has low entry barriers; high barriers are a feature of oligopoly and monopoly.
  4. D. In monopolistic competition, firms are price takers; in monopoly, they are price makers.
    In monopolistic competition, firms are price makers (partial control) due to differentiation, not price takers.

Q5. A firm introduces a highly innovative product in a competitive market. Initially, it earns large abnormal profits. According to the competitive market cycle, what is likely to happen to these profits over time?

Remediation → err-competitive-cycle-profit | Retest → err-competitive-cycle-profit

  1. A. The profits will remain abnormally high because the innovation protects the firm from competition.
    In a competitive market, the innovation creates short-term abnormal profits, but new entrants will eventually increase supply and erode these profits.
  2. ✓ B. The profits will be driven down to normal as new firms enter the market, increasing supply and lowering prices.
  3. C. The profits will increase because demand for the product will keep rising.
    Without barriers, increased supply from new entrants will reduce prices, not increase profits.
  4. D. The profits will become zero immediately because competitors copy the innovation.
    Profits decrease gradually as new firms enter, not immediately.

    Q8. Explain how the competitive market cycle ensures that abnormal profits from an innovation are only temporary. Include the role of new entrants and price changes in your answer.

    errorId: err-competitive-cycle-profit | Bloom: analyze | Scaffold: L3

    Achievement: Thinking | Action: analyze | OSSD: Analyse how new technology has affected markets and consumers.

    Official: CC1.3 | Node: ? | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/2015cws11and12.pdf

    Learning Goal: lg-2

    Misconception: Students may think abnormal profits are permanent or that the firm can maintain them without barriers.

    Remediation → err-competitive-cycle-profit | Retest → err-competitive-cycle-profit

      Rubric: Full credit requires: (1) Innovation creates short-term abnormal profits due to first-mover advantage. (2) High profits attract new firms to enter the market. (3) Entry increases market supply, which drives down the market price. (4) As price falls, profits decrease back to normal (zero economic profit) in the long run.

      Sample: When a firm introduces an innovation, it initially earns abnormal profits because it has a temporary monopoly. These high profits signal other entrepreneurs to enter the market. As new firms enter, the total supply of the product increases, which pushes the price down. Eventually, the price falls to the point where firms only earn normal profits (zero economic profit). Thus, abnormal profits are eroded by competition.

      补救模块

      err-monopolistic-competition-monopoly

      Students often confuse monopolistic competition with monopoly, believing that because firms in monopolistic competition have some pricing power, the market structure is similar to a monopoly. They overlook key differences: monopolistic competition has many firms with low entry barriers, while monopoly has a single firm with extremely high entry barriers.

      Explain: Monopolistic competition and monopoly are distinct market structures. In monopolistic competition, there are many firms offering slightly differentiated products, with low barriers to entry and partial pricing control. In contrast, a monopoly is dominated by a single firm with near-total pricing control and extremely high entry barriers. For example, local restaurants operate in monopolistic competition—many restaurants, each with a unique menu, and new ones can open easily. A local water utility is a natural monopoly—only one provider due to high infrastructure costs.

      Example: Compare a local restaurant (monopolistic competition) with a local water utility (monopoly). The restaurant has many competitors and can enter freely; the water utility is the sole provider with high barriers to entry.

      err-perfect-competition-product-differentiation

      学生错误地认为在完美竞争市场中，企业可以通过产品差异化来获得市场力量，而实际上完美竞争要求所有产品都是同质的。

      Explain: 完美竞争市场要求所有产品是同质的（identical），即消费者无法区分不同企业的产品。任何形式的产品差异化都会使市场变成垄断竞争或寡头垄断，而不是完美竞争。例如：在农产品市场上，所有小麦都是相同的，农民无法通过品牌或包装来获取更高价格。

      Example: 以农产品（如玉米、大豆）为例：无论哪个农民种植的玉米，在消费者眼中都是完全相同的，因此价格由市场供求决定，单个农民无法提价。

      err-oligopoly-entry-barriers

      Students think new firms can easily enter an oligopoly market.

      Explain: 寡头垄断市场由少数几家大企业主导，存在很高的进入壁垒。这些壁垒包括规模经济（大企业成本更低）、品牌忠诚度（消费者信任已有品牌）、巨额资本需求、专利或技术壁垒等。新企业很难进入并与现有巨头竞争。

      Example: 银行业、超市行业和全球石油行业都是寡头垄断的例子。比如银行业，新银行需要大量资本、监管批准和客户信任，这些都使得进入非常困难。

      err-monopoly-efficiency

      Assuming monopoly is always efficient due to economies of scale; students may ignore potential inefficiency from lack of competition.

      Explain: While monopolies can benefit from economies of scale and have incentives for R&D, they also face potential inefficiencies because there is no competitive pressure. Without competitors, a monopoly may charge higher prices, produce less output, and reduce product choice for consumers. In contrast, competitive markets drive efficiency through rivalry. Natural monopolies, like local water utilities, may be efficient due to scale, but even they must be regulated to protect consumers.

      Example: Consider a natural monopoly like a local water utility. It can provide water at low cost due to economies of scale. However, without regulation, it could raise prices and reduce service quality because consumers have no alternative. This shows the tradeoff: scale benefit vs. inefficiency from lack of competition.

      err-competitive-cycle-profit

      Students incorrectly believe that innovation creates permanent abnormal profits because they overlook how easy entry in competitive markets allows new firms to increase supply, reducing prices and eroding profits back to normal levels.

      Explain: In competitive markets (perfect competition or monopolistic competition), a firm that innovates may earn short-term abnormal profits because it has a temporary edge. However, these high profits attract new competitors who enter the market, increasing the total supply. With more supply, the market price falls, reducing each firm's profit margin. Eventually, profits return to normal (zero economic profit) as the market reaches long-run equilibrium. This cycle ensures that abnormal profits are only temporary in the absence of barriers to entry.

      Example: A coffee shop introduces a new brewing method that becomes very popular, earning high profits. Seeing this success, other coffee shops quickly adopt similar methods or open new stores. The increased supply of specialty coffee drives down the price, and eventually the original shop's profits fall back to normal.
