Q1. Why did Hades take Persephone to the underworld?

errorId: err-demeter-hades-zeus | Bloom: understand | Scaffold: L1

Achievement: Knowledge and Understanding | Action: interpret | OSSD: Read and demonstrate understanding of a variety of texts for different purposes.

Official: B1 | Node: ESLDO-B-OE01 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/esl912currb.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-1

Misconception: Students may think Hades acted alone without Zeus's consent.

Remediation → err-demeter-hades-zeus | Retest → err-demeter-hades-zeus

  1. A. He wanted revenge on Demeter.
    Hades did not seek revenge; he was attracted to Persephone and had Zeus's permission.
  2. ✓ B. Zeus gave him permission to take Persephone as his wife.
    Correct! Zeus allowed Hades to take Persephone.
  3. C. Persephone ran away and Hades rescued her.
    Persephone was not running away; she was taken by Hades.
  4. D. Hades wanted to start a war with Demeter.
    The myth does not mention a war; Hades wanted a wife.

Q2. What happened when Persephone ate the pomegranate seeds in the underworld?

errorId: err-persephone-pomegranate | Bloom: understand | Scaffold: L1

Achievement: Knowledge and Understanding | Action: interpret | OSSD: Read and demonstrate understanding of a variety of texts for different purposes.

Official: B1 | Node: ESLDO-B-OE01 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/esl912currb.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-1

Misconception: Students may think Persephone was forced to eat; actually she chose to eat, which bound her.

Remediation → err-persephone-pomegranate | Retest → err-persephone-pomegranate

  1. A. She gained immortality and stayed in the underworld forever.
    She didn't gain immortality; the seeds forced her to return each year.
  2. B. She was forced to eat them and became Hades's prisoner.
    She was not forced; she chose to eat, and that choice bound her.
  3. ✓ C. She chose to eat them, which bound her to spend half the year in the underworld.
    Correct! By eating the seeds, she had to spend part of each year with Hades.
  4. D. Nothing happened; the seeds were a trick and she returned to Demeter immediately.
    The seeds had a real effect; they created the cycle of seasons.

    补救模块

    err-demeter-hades-zeus

    Students may think Hades abducted Persephone without Zeus's permission, missing the divine conspiracy.

    Explain: In the myth, Hades did not act alone. Zeus, the king of the gods, gave Hades permission to take Persephone as his wife. This divine agreement is a key part of the story, showing that the abduction was not a random act but a planned event among the gods.

    Example: Zeus agreed to Hades' plan because he wanted to please his brother and settle a matter. Without Zeus's consent, Hades would not have dared to take Persephone from Demeter.

    err-midas-golden-touch-source

    Midas's golden touch came from his own wish, not from a god's gift.

    Explain: In the myth, King Midas helped Dionysus, and as a reward, Dionysus granted him the power to turn everything he touched into gold. Midas did not ask for it; it was given as a gift. This is important because the story shows that even gifts can become curses.

    Example: Midas could not eat or drink because his food and drink turned to gold. He realized the golden touch was a curse, not a blessing.

    err-midas-donkey-ears-reason

    Many students mistakenly believe Midas's donkey ears were a punishment for his greed, but in the myth, greed was connected to the golden touch from Dionysus. The donkey ears were a separate punishment from Apollo for Midas's poor judgment in a music contest.

    Explain: King Midas was once asked to judge a music contest between the god Apollo and the satyr Pan. Midas declared Pan the winner, which offended Apollo. In response, Apollo gave Midas donkey ears as a punishment for his lack of musical judgment. This is different from the golden touch, which was a curse for greed. Remember: the donkey ears are about bad taste in music, not about greed.

    Example: In the story, Midas's greed led to the golden touch—everything he touched turned to gold, including food, so he couldn't eat. Later, his poor decision in judging the music contest led to donkey ears. The two punishments are separate and caused by different actions.

    err-persephone-pomegranate

    Thinking Persephone was forced to eat the pomegranate seeds, whereas she chose to eat them, which bound her to spend half the year in the underworld.

    Explain: In the myth, Persephone was not forced to eat the pomegranate seeds. She chose to eat them. In Greek mythology, eating food from the underworld binds a person to that realm. Because Persephone ate the seeds, she had to spend part of each year with Hades. This creates the cycle of seasons: when she is with her mother Demeter, the earth flourishes (spring/summer); when she returns to the underworld, Demeter grieves and the earth becomes barren (autumn/winter).

    Example: Think of it like accepting a gift from someone you're staying with – once you accept, you have an obligation. Persephone's choice had a permanent consequence.

    err-theme-greed-simplistic

    You might think the Midas myth is only about greed, but it also teaches about consequences and humility.

    Explain: The Midas myth has multiple themes. Greed is prominent, but the story also shows that every action has consequences (the golden touch became a curse) and the importance of humility (Midas's arrogance led to donkey ears). Ignoring these themes gives an incomplete understanding.

    Example: Student Andy noted that Midas's greed is similar to gambling addiction: both crave effortless wealth and often lose everything. This connects greed to real-world consequences. However, the myth also shows Midas's lack of humility when he judged Pan better than Apollo, resulting in a humiliating punishment.
