Q1. Which statement is true about the counterargument in a persuasive essay?

errorId: err-counterargument-optional | Bloom: understand | Scaffold: L1

Achievement: Knowledge and Understanding | Action: interpret | OSSD: Organize ideas coherently in writing.

Official: C2 | Node: ESLDO-C-OE02 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/esl912currb.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-1

Misconception: Students may think the counterargument is optional or only for advanced essays.

Remediation → err-counterargument-optional | Retest → err-counterargument-optional

  1. A. It is optional and should only be used if you have strong opposing points.
    The counterargument is not optional. It is a required part of a persuasive essay to show you have considered the opposing view.
  2. ✓ B. It is mandatory and should be included in the essay structure.
    Correct! The counterargument is a mandatory component of a persuasive essay.
  3. C. It should only appear in the conclusion to summarize opposing views.
    The counterargument typically appears as a separate section before the conclusion, not within the conclusion itself.
  4. D. It is the same as the rebuttal, so you only need one or the other.
    The counterargument states the opposing view, while the rebuttal is your response. Both are needed, but the counterargument is a distinct section.

Q2. What is the purpose of the hook in an introduction?

errorId: err-hook-vs-thesis | Bloom: understand | Scaffold: L1

Achievement: Knowledge and Understanding | Action: interpret | OSSD: Organize ideas coherently in writing.

Official: C2 | Node: ESLDO-C-OE02 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/esl912currb.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-1

Misconception: Students may think the hook states the main argument or summarizes the essay.

Remediation → err-hook-vs-thesis | Retest → err-hook-vs-thesis

  1. A. To state the main argument of the essay.
    That is the job of the thesis statement, not the hook. The hook is meant to grab attention.
  2. ✓ B. To grab the reader's attention and make them want to continue reading.
    Correct! The hook's purpose is to engage the reader from the start.
  3. C. To provide background information about the topic.
    Background context is a separate part of the introduction. The hook comes first and is attention-grabbing.
  4. D. To introduce the counterargument.
    The counterargument is introduced later in the essay, not in the hook.

Q3. Which of the following is a valid thesis statement?

errorId: err-vague-thesis | Bloom: evaluate | Scaffold: L2

Achievement: Thinking | Action: evaluate | OSSD: Organize ideas coherently in writing.

Official: C2 | Node: ESLDO-C-OE02 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/esl912currb.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-2

Misconception: Students may accept vague statements as valid theses.

Remediation → err-vague-thesis | Retest → err-vague-thesis

  1. A. Learning English is important.
    This thesis is too vague. It states an opinion but does not give any specific reasons.
  2. B. I think learning English is good and everyone should learn it.
    This is also vague and uses 'I think', which weakens the statement. A thesis should be a clear declarative sentence with specific reasons.
  3. ✓ C. Learning English is important because it helps you get a job, communicate globally, and access information.
    Correct! This thesis states a clear position and provides three specific reasons.
  4. D. This essay will talk about learning English.
    This is an announcement, not a thesis. A thesis should state the argument, not describe what the essay will do.

Q4. What are the required elements of a body paragraph in a persuasive essay?

errorId: err-rdf-ee-missing-explanation | Bloom: understand | Scaffold: L1

Achievement: Knowledge and Understanding | Action: interpret | OSSD: Organize ideas coherently in writing.

Official: C2 | Node: ESLDO-C-OE02 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/esl912currb.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-3

Misconception: Students may think including a reason and a fact is sufficient, omitting the explanation of how the fact supports the reason.

Remediation → err-rdf-ee-missing-explanation | Retest → err-rdf-ee-missing-explanation

  1. A. A reason and a fact only.
    You are missing the explanation. A body paragraph also needs to explain how the fact supports the reason (the EE part).
  2. ✓ B. A reason, a fact, and an explanation of how the fact supports the reason.
    Correct! This is the RDF+EE framework: Reason, Detail/Fact, and Example/Explanation.
  3. C. A reason and an example only.
    You need a fact or detail as well, not just an example. Also, you need to explain the connection.
  4. D. A fact and an example only.
    You are missing the reason (topic sentence). The reason states the main point of the paragraph.

补救模块

err-counterargument-optional

Students may think the counterargument is optional, but it is a mandatory structural component of a persuasive essay.

Explain: A persuasive essay has four mandatory structural components: introduction, body paragraphs, counterargument section, and conclusion. The counterargument is not optional; it shows you have considered the opposing view and strengthens your argument by addressing it. A valid counterargument states the opposing stance and its logic, then provides a targeted rebuttal.

Example: For the essay topic 'Whether wealth is the core driver of happiness,' a counterargument might be: 'Some argue that money can't buy happiness. While it's true that money alone doesn't guarantee happiness, financial security provides access to basic needs, healthcare, and opportunities, which are fundamental to well-being.'

err-hook-vs-thesis

Students may think the hook states the main argument or that the thesis is just an attention-getter. They confuse the roles: hook grabs attention, thesis states the writer's stance with supporting reasons.

Explain: In a persuasive essay introduction, the hook and thesis have different jobs. The hook is the first sentence(s) designed to grab the reader's attention. There are 6 approved hook types: question, surprising statistic/fact, bold strong statement, personal anecdote, famous quotation, or riddle. The thesis is a single, clear sentence stating your stance and 3 specific supporting reasons. It tells the reader what you will argue. Do not use the hook to state your argument; save that for the thesis.

Example: Hook: 'Did you know that over 1.5 billion people speak English worldwide?' (surprising statistic). Thesis: 'Learning English is important because it enhances job opportunities, enables global communication, and provides access to information.'

err-vague-thesis

A strong thesis needs a clear stance and three specific reasons. The student chose a vague statement without listing supporting points.

Explain: A thesis statement is a single sentence that presents your main argument and previews your three supporting reasons. It must be specific, not vague. For example, 'Learning English is important' is vague because it doesn't say why. A strong thesis would be: 'Learning English is important because it helps you get a job, communicate globally, and access information.'

Example: Invalid: 'Learning English is important.' Valid: 'Learning English is important because it helps you get a job, communicate globally, and access information.'

err-rdf-ee-missing-explanation

Students think that a body paragraph only needs a reason and a fact; they forget to include an explanation of how the fact supports the reason.

Explain: Each body paragraph in a persuasive essay must follow the RDF+EE framework: Reason (topic sentence), Detail or Fact (supporting evidence), and Example or Explanation (how the evidence supports the reason). The explanation is crucial because it connects the evidence to the argument, showing why the evidence matters.

Example: Reason: Learning English can improve job opportunities. Fact: 70% of global jobs require English proficiency. Explanation: This statistic shows that knowing English opens up more career paths, making it a valuable skill for economic success.
