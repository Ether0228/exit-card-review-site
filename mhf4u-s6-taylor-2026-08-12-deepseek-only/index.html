Q1. The Ferris wheel height function is h(t) = -15cos(π/4 (t-1)) + 17. What does the leading negative sign on the 15 represent?

errorId: err-ferris-wheel-amplitude-sign | Bloom: understand | Scaffold: L1

Achievement: Knowledge and Understanding | Action: interpret | OSSD: Make connections between trigonometric ratios and the graphical and algebraic re

Official: B2 | Node: MHF4U-B-OE02 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/math1112currb.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-1

Misconception: Students often treat amplitude as always positive and ignore the vertical reflection's meaning for starting position.

Remediation → err-ferris-wheel-amplitude-sign | Retest → err-ferris-wheel-amplitude-sign

  1. A. The wheel starts at its highest point, so the amplitude is positive 15.
    A negative cosine does not start at a maximum. The negative sign actually flips the graph so it starts at a minimum.
  2. ✓ B. The wheel starts at its lowest point because the cosine is inverted, so the amplitude is -15.
    Correct. The negative sign on the cosine indicates the graph is reflected vertically, starting at the minimum.
  3. C. The wheel starts at the midline moving upward, so the negative sign indicates a sine function.
    The function is still cosine, not sine. A negative sine would also start at midline moving downward, not upward.
  4. D. The wheel has amplitude 15 but the negative sign is a vertical reflection, not related to starting position.
    Vertical reflection is exactly what changes the starting position from maximum to minimum. So it is directly related.

Q2. Given h(t) = -15cos(π/4 (t-1)) + 17, which step correctly standardizes the argument to verify the phase shift?

errorId: err-k-factoring-phase-shift | Bloom: apply | Scaffold: L2

Achievement: Application | Action: apply | OSSD: Make connections between trigonometric ratios and the graphical and algebraic re

Official: B2 | Node: MHF4U-B-OE02 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/math1112currb.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-2

Misconception: Students often read the phase shift directly from the parentheses without factoring k, or misapply the division.

Remediation → err-k-factoring-phase-shift | Retest → err-k-factoring-phase-shift

  1. A. Keep it as -15cos(π/4 t - π/4) + 17 and identify the phase shift as -π/4.
    The constant term must be divided by k (π/4) to get the actual horizontal shift. -π/4 divided by π/4 is -1, not the phase shift itself.
  2. ✓ B. Divide the constant -π/4 by π/4 to get -1, giving the standardized form π/4(t - 1).
    Correct. Factoring out π/4 from π/4(t-1) gives π/4 t - π/4, so dividing the constant by π/4 yields -1, confirming a 1-unit right shift.
  3. C. Multiply the constant -π/4 by π/4 to get -π^2/16, so the phase shift is -π^2/16.
    You should divide, not multiply, by k when factoring. Multiplying changes the value incorrectly.
  4. D. The phase shift is already 1 because it appears in the parentheses, so no extra step is needed.
    While (t-1) does show a right shift of 1, the standardization step is required to prove it when the argument is not already factored. Always factor k explicitly.

Q4. Which statement correctly identifies the vertical asymptotes of tan(x)?

errorId: err-reciprocal-asymptote-denominator | Bloom: understand | Scaffold: L1

Achievement: Knowledge and Understanding | Action: interpret | OSSD: Make connections between trigonometric ratios and the graphical and algebraic re

Official: B2 | Node: MHF4U-B-OE02 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/math1112currb.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-4

Misconception: Students often set the numerator equal to zero, thinking zeros of the function create asymptotes.

Remediation → err-reciprocal-asymptote-denominator | Retest → err-reciprocal-asymptote-denominator

  1. A. tan(x) = sin(x)/cos(x), so asymptotes occur where sin(x) = 0.
    You set the numerator equal to zero. Asymptotes occur from the denominator, not the numerator.
  2. ✓ B. tan(x) = sin(x)/cos(x), so asymptotes occur where cos(x) = 0.
    Correct. The denominator cos(x) cannot be zero, so those x-values are excluded as vertical asymptotes.
  3. C. tan(x) = cos(x)/sin(x), so asymptotes occur where sin(x) = 0.
    That is the definition of cot(x), not tan(x). And for cot, sin(x)=0 does give asymptotes.
  4. D. tan(x) = cos(x)/sin(x), so asymptotes occur where cos(x) = 0.
    That uses the wrong definition of tan. tan is sin/cos, not cos/sin.

补救模块

err-ferris-wheel-amplitude-sign

The student treats amplitude as always positive and ignores the vertical reflection meaning of the leading negative sign, losing the connection between a negative cosine and starting at the minimum.

Explain: In a sinusoidal height model, the midline is y = c. Amplitude is the vertical distance from the midline, but the leading sign of the cosine term is the orientation. Positive cosine starts at the maximum (midline + amplitude). Negative cosine starts at the minimum (midline - amplitude). In h(t) = -15cos(π/4 (t-1)) + 17, the midline is 17, the amplitude magnitude is 15, and the negative sign reflects the cosine so the ride starts at the minimum height.

Example: At the moment the ride begins, t = 1 because of the 1-unit right shift. h(1) = -15cos(π/4 (1-1)) + 17 = -15cos(0) + 17 = -15 + 17, which is the midline minus the amplitude. A positive cosine would give 17 + 15, starting at the maximum.

err-k-factoring-phase-shift

Students think the horizontal shift is simply the constant term shown in the unfactored expression. They forget to divide that constant by k, so they get -π/4 instead of the correct 1-unit right shift.

Explain: To read a phase shift, the trigonometric argument must be written in the form k(t - c). If you see an argument like kt + b, you must first factor out k and divide the constant term by k. In h(t) = -15cos(π/4 (t-1)) + 17, the argument is already factored, so the phase shift is clearly 1 unit to the right. If it were written as -15cos(π/4 t - π/4) + 17, you would rewrite it as π/4(t - 1) by dividing the -π/4 by π/4.

Example: Start with h(t) = -15cos(π/4 t - π/4) + 17. Factor k = π/4 out of the argument: π/4 t - π/4 = π/4(t - 1). Now compare to the standard form k(t - c). Since t - 1 = t - 1, c = 1, so the phase shift is 1 unit right, not -π/4.

err-function-selection-start-point

The student confuses which trigonometric function (sine or cosine, positive or negative) corresponds to a starting point at maximum, minimum, or midline. They may think positive sine starts at maximum, or that cosine always starts at maximum regardless of shift.

Explain: The choice of trig function depends on where the cycle starts at the reference point (usually t=0 or the phase-shifted start). Positive cosine starts at the maximum, negative cosine starts at the minimum, positive sine starts at the midline moving upward, and negative sine starts at the midline moving downward. The sign on the amplitude flips the starting point vertically.

Example: For a Ferris wheel that starts at its lowest point, use negative cosine. This matches h(t) = -15cos(π/4 (t-1)) + 17. If it started at the highest point, use positive cosine. If it started at the midline going up, use positive sine; going down, use negative sine.

err-reciprocal-asymptote-denominator

The student confuses zeros of a function (numerator zero) with points where the function is undefined (denominator zero). For tan(x) = sin(x)/cos(x), they incorrectly use sin(x)=0 to locate asymptotes, but the actual asymptotes occur where cos(x)=0 because the denominator cannot be zero.

Explain: Vertical asymptotes of a reciprocal trigonometric function occur where the function is undefined, meaning the denominator of the fraction equals zero. For tan(x) = sin(x)/cos(x), the denominator is cos(x), so vertical asymptotes occur at all x-values where cos(x)=0. For cot(x) = cos(x)/sin(x), the denominator is sin(x), so vertical asymptotes occur at all x-values where sin(x)=0. Setting the numerator to zero instead gives the x-intercepts of the function, not its asymptotes.

Example: Think about tan(x). The numerator sin(x)=0 at multiples of π, and those are exactly the x-intercepts of tan(x). But a vertical asymptote appears where tan(x) is undefined, which happens when its denominator cos(x)=0. So check the denominator, not the numerator, for asymptotes.

err-arc-length-radian-vs-degree

The student thinks θ in the formula θ = a/r can be measured in degrees, not understanding that this formula is the definition of a radian and therefore requires radian measure. They may also confuse angular velocity units, not recognizing that v = rω needs ω in rad/s.

Explain: The formula θ = a/r is the definition of a radian: one radian is the angle formed when the arc length equals the radius. Because it is a definition, θ must be in radians. If an angle is given in degrees, convert it to radians first before using it. The related formula linear velocity = angular velocity × radius (v = rω) also requires ω to be in radians per unit time to give linear velocity in matching distance units.

Example: A Ferris wheel has radius 17 m. If a seat travels through π/4 radians, the arc length is a = rθ = 17 × (π/4) = 17π/4 m. If someone tried to use a degree measure there, the formula would not work because the ratio a/r is equal to the angle only when that angle is measured in radians.

err-graphing-midline-from-k

The k-value and phase shift control horizontal stretching and shifting, but they do not move the graph up or down. Students often ignore c and draw the midline at y = 0, losing the vertical center of the graph.

Explain: The midline is the horizontal line halfway between the maximum and minimum values of a sinusoidal function. In standard form y = a·sin(k(x-d)) + c or y = a·cos(k(x-d)) + c, the midline is exactly y = c. The vertical shift c moves the whole graph up or down. For example, if c = 17, the graph oscillates around y = 17, not y = 0.

Example: For h(t) = -15cos(π/4(t-1)) + 17, the vertical shift is 17, so the midline is y = 17. The amplitude is |−15| = 15, so the maximum is 17 + 15 = 32 and the minimum is 17 − 15 = 2. The k-value π/4 and the 1-unit right shift do not change the midline.
