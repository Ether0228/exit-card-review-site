补救模块

err-orpheus-lookback-reason

Orpheus looked back out of curiosity or defiance, but the story says it was due to doubt.

Explain: Orpheus looked back because he doubted that Hades would keep his promise. Hades had set a condition: Orpheus must not look back until both had left the underworld. But Orpheus was anxious and unsure, so he turned around too soon. This doubt caused him to lose Eurydice forever.

Example: Imagine you are in a dark cave. Someone promises to lead you out, but says you must not look back until you see the sun. You start to doubt they are still there, so you turn around—and find they disappear. That's what happened to Orpheus.

err-orpheus-eurydice-revival

Some students think Eurydice was revived or saved later, but in the myth she was lost permanently.

Explain: When Orpheus looked back at Eurydice before leaving the underworld, she was immediately taken back to the underworld permanently. This is because he broke the 'no-looking-back' rule given by Hades. Once he looked back, she was lost forever, and he could not bring her back again.

Example: Orpheus doubts and looks back near the exit of the underworld, losing Eurydice permanently. She does not return to life.

err-orpheus-lyre-power

Students may underestimate the lyre's power, thinking it only charms animals, but it also charms stones and underworld gods.

Explain: Orpheus' lyre has extraordinary power. Its music can charm not only living creatures like animals, but also inanimate objects like stones, and even the gods of the underworld, including Hades and Persephone. The lyre can calm storms and tame wild beasts. This power is central to the story, as Orpheus uses it to persuade Hades to release Eurydice.

Example: In the myth, when Orpheus enters the underworld, he plays his lyre and charms the three-headed dog Cerberus, allowing him to pass. Later, he plays for Hades and Persephone, moving them to tears and convincing them to grant his wish for Eurydice's return.

err-version-difference

Students may think the two video versions have different core plots, but they share the same core plot; differences are only in detail focus.

Explain: Both video versions of the Orpheus myth share the exact same core plot: Orpheus marries Eurydice, she dies from a snake bite, Orpheus journeys to the underworld, charms Hades and Persephone, is told not to look back until they leave, looks back due to doubt, and loses Eurydice permanently. The differences are only in which details are emphasized. For example, one version focuses on the eternal reunion after death, while the other adds more background about the characters.

Example: In the first video, the story emphasizes what happens after Orpheus loses Eurydice and their eventual reunion in the afterlife. In the second video, more time is spent on the characters' backgrounds and intermediate plot details. Yet both contain the marriage, snake bite, underworld journey, no-looking-back rule, and loss.
