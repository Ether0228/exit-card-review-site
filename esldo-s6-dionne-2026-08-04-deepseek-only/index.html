    补救模块

    err-act2-plot-misorder

    Student confuses the order of events, e.g., thinking the wedding occurs before the balcony scene.

    Explain: In Act 2 of Romeo and Juliet, the events happen in a clear order. First, Romeo and Juliet confess their love in the balcony scene. Then, Friar Lawrence agrees to marry them in secret, hoping to end the feud between the families. After that, the Nurse acts as a messenger between Romeo and Juliet to help arrange the secret wedding. Finally, Romeo, Juliet, and Friar Lawrence attend the secret wedding.

    Example: In Scene 2, Romeo overhears Juliet's private thoughts and they agree to marry. This happens before Friar Lawrence agrees to help and before the Nurse carries messages.

    err-oxymoron-misidentification

    The student does not recognize that an oxymoron combines two contradictory terms within a single phrase, and may call it a metaphor or think any contradiction is an oxymoron.

    Explain: An oxymoron is a literary device that combines two contradictory terms for effect. In Act 2, Juliet says 'parting is such sweet sorrow.' This is an oxymoron because 'sweet' and 'sorrow' are opposite in meaning, yet they are placed together to express the mixed sadness and joy of parting.

    Example: 'parting is such sweet sorrow' – 'sweet' (pleasant) and 'sorrow' (painful) are contradictory terms.

    err-vocab-misunderstanding

    The student sees 'feuding' as just a small disagreement instead of recognizing it as a deep, long-lasting, and bitter conflict. This prevents them from understanding the family hatred in Romeo and Juliet.

    Explain: A vocabulary word's meaning must include its full weight. 'Feuding' describes a prolonged, bitter conflict between groups or families, not a one-time quarrel. In Act 2, the Montagues and Capulets have been feuding for generations. This is why their hatred is so strong and why Friar Lawrence hopes a secret marriage can end the feud.

    Example: In Romeo and Juliet, the Capulets and Montagues have been feuding for generations. The feud is not a small argument—it is a deep, violent conflict that affects the whole city, and it makes Romeo and Juliet's secret marriage risky.

    err-discussion-shallow-reply

    The student thinks a reply in a discussion forum only needs to show agreement, not engage with the content, add reasons, or ask questions.

    Explain: A good reply to a classmate's post shows thoughtful engagement. Use the A.G.A. strategy: Agree (or respectfully disagree), Give a reason or new idea, and Ask a question. This shows you read the post carefully and want to continue the discussion.

    Example: For the prompt 'Should Romeo and Juliet keep their relationship a secret? Why?' a classmate posts: 'Yes, because their families would be angry.' A strong reply could be: 'I agree that their families would be angry, but I also think secrecy causes more problems. If they had told Friar Lawrence earlier, maybe they could have planned better. What do you think would happen if the families found out?' This acknowledges the classmate's idea, gives a new reason, and asks a question.
