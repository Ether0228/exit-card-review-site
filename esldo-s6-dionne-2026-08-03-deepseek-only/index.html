补救模块

err-esldo-s6-rj-overview

The student does not yet understand that Unit 3 is organized around the five acts of Shakespeare's Romeo and Juliet, and may not connect the lesson structure to the setting and key events of Act 1.

Explain: Unit 3 is a complete study of William Shakespeare's tragedy Romeo and Juliet. The play has five acts, and the unit is divided into five lessons, one for each act. The play is set in Verona, Italy, in the late 1500s during the Renaissance. Two families, the Montagues and the Capulets, have a long-standing feud. Act 1 introduces the main characters and sets the story in motion: a street brawl breaks out, Paris asks to marry Juliet, and at the Capulet banquet Romeo and Juliet meet for the first time, fall in love, and then discover they are from rival families.

Example: In Lesson 1 you studied Act 1 scene by scene. Scene 1 starts with a fight between the two families' servants. Scene 2 shows Paris asking Lord Capulet for Juliet's hand. Scene 3 introduces Juliet and her family's plans. Scene 4 shows Romeo and his friends going to the Capulet banquet in masks, and Romeo has a bad feeling about what will happen. Scene 5 is the key moment: Romeo and Juliet meet, kiss, and fall in love, but then learn they are from enemy families. Each lesson in Unit 3 does the same for Acts 2, 3, 4, and 5, so the whole unit tells the entire tragic story.

err-esldo-s6-rj-language

The student may not realize that the course provides a side-by-side modern English comparison to help decode Shakespeare's archaic language, or may think they are expected to understand the original text without support.

Explain: In this unit, Shakespeare's original play can be hard to understand because it uses archaic English. To help you, the course provides a side-by-side modern English comparison. This means you can see the original Shakespearean text and a simpler, modern English version together. There are also two versions of the Act 1 videos: one in original archaic English and one in simplified modern English. You can use these tools anytime you get stuck.

Example: When you watch the Act 1 video, you might hear a line like 'Wherefore art thou Romeo?' in the original archaic English version. The side-by-side comparison or the modern English version helps you understand that it means 'Why are you Romeo?' in today's language.
