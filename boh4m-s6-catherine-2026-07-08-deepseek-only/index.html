  1. A. 2020, Collins) states that...
    You placed the year before the author. In APA parenthetical citations, the author's last name comes first, followed by a comma and the year: (Collins, 2020).
  2. B. (2020 Collins) explains that...
    This format is missing the comma. The correct format is (Collins, 2020).

    Correct. Parenthetical citations require author last name, comma, year, all inside parentheses.

    补救模块

    err-citation-author-year-order

    The student may think that the year should come first in parentheses, but APA requires the author's last name first, followed by a comma and the year.

    Explain: In APA parenthetical citations, always place the author's last name first, then a comma, then the year. For example: (Collins, 2020). The year never comes before the author's name inside the parentheses. This rule helps readers quickly identify the source by the author's name.

    Example: Correct: (Collins, 2020) argues that... Incorrect: (2020, Collins) argues that... In narrative format, you write: Collins (2020) argues that...

    err-direct-quote-page-missing

    The student thinks that a direct quote citation only requires author and year, and may place the page number incorrectly (e.g., after the ending period) or forget it entirely.

    Explain: When you use a direct quote in APA style, you must include the author, year, and the specific page number where the quote appears. The page number goes inside the parentheses, after the year, and before the closing period. For example: 'quote' (Author, Year, p. XX).

    Example: According to Collins, 'leadership matters' (2020, p. 23).

    err-paraphrase-page-included

    Students may mistakenly include a page number when citing a paraphrase, believing all citations require it, but APA only requires page numbers for direct quotes.

    Explain: In APA style, when you paraphrase (restate someone else's idea in your own words), you only need to include the author's last name and the year of publication. Page numbers are not required because you are not quoting directly. This rule helps distinguish paraphrases from direct quotes, which must include the page number.

    Example: Correct: Anderson (2021) discusses corporate social responsibility. Incorrect: Anderson (2021, p. 45) discusses corporate social responsibility.

    err-narrative-vs-parenthetical

    The student confuses narrative and parenthetical in-text citation formats. They may place the year in the sentence (e.g., 'Collins 2020 argues...') or use a parenthetical citation (e.g., '(Collins, 2020) argues...') when the author's name is already part of the sentence.

    Explain: APA has two formats for in-text citations: narrative and parenthetical. In narrative format, the author's last name is integrated into the sentence, and the year is placed in parentheses immediately after the author's name. In parenthetical format, both the author and year are placed in parentheses at the end of the sentence. Do not put the year in the sentence itself or use parentheses around the author's name in narrative format.

    Example: Correct narrative: Collins (2020) argues that good-to-great companies have disciplined people. Incorrect narrative: Collins, 2020 argues... (year not in parentheses) or (Collins, 2020) argues... (parenthetical used as narrative). Correct parenthetical: Good-to-great companies have disciplined people (Collins, 2020).

    err-reference-tool-misuse

    Students believe that auto-generated references from tools like Google Scholar are always 100% correct and require no further verification.

    Explain: Automatic reference generators (e.g., Google Scholar) are useful starting points, but they can contain errors such as wrong capitalization, missing periods, incorrect author initials, or outdated formatting. Always verify any auto-generated reference against a reliable APA style guide (e.g., the official APA manual or OWL Purdue) or the original source's copyright page.

    Example: If you use Google Scholar to generate a reference for Thaler's 2015 book 'Misbehaving: The Making of Behavioral Economics', it might produce: Thaler, R. (2015). Misbehaving: The making of behavioral economics. W.W. Norton & Company. Check that: (1) the author's first initial is correct (R.), (2) the title is in italics (if formatting allows), (3) the publisher name is exact, and (4) the year matches the copyright date.
