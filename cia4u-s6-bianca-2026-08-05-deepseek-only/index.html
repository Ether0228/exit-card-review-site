Learning Goal: Define fiscal policy and identify who formulates it in Canada through the annual federal budget.

Misconception: Students often think the Bank of Canada or Federal Reserve sets fiscal policy.

  1. A. Decisions by the Bank of Canada about interest rates and the money supply.
    That describes monetary policy, not fiscal policy. Fiscal policy is about taxation and public spending set by the government.
  2. ✓ B. Government decisions on taxation and public spending, presented in the annual federal budget.
    Correct. Fiscal policy is made by the federal government through the budget, not by the central bank.
  3. C. A plan by private banks to increase consumer spending during a recession.
    Fiscal policy is a government responsibility, not a private banking plan.
  4. D. Policy made by the federal government to control the exchange rate of the Canadian dollar.
    Exchange rate intervention is usually associated with monetary or international economic policy, not the core fiscal policy definition.

Q2. Canada enters a recession with high unemployment and weak consumer demand. According to Keynesian theory, which fiscal policy would be most appropriate?

errorId: err-cia4u-fiscal-policy-definition | Bloom: apply | Scaffold: L2

Achievement: Application | Action: apply | OSSD: Demonstrate an understanding of fiscal policy in Canada, including how it is sha

Official: D2 | Node: CIA4U-D-OE02 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/2015cws11and12.pdf

Learning Goal: Distinguish expansionary and contractionary fiscal policies and choose the appropriate policy for recessionary condition

Misconception: Students might confuse contractionary policy (tax increases and spending cuts) with recovery measures, or suggest central bank actions instead.

Remediation → err-cia4u-fiscal-policy-definition | Retest → err-cia4u-fiscal-policy-definition

  1. ✓ A. Cut taxes and increase government spending.
    Correct. This is expansionary fiscal policy, which Keynesians recommend to boost aggregate demand during a recession.
  2. B. Raise taxes and cut government spending.
    That is contractionary policy, used mainly to fight inflation, not a recession with weak demand.
  3. C. Have the Bank of Canada lower interest rates only.
    That is monetary policy, although it can help. The question asks for fiscal policy, and Keynesian theory specifically recommends government tax and spending actions.
  4. D. Reduce government spending and the money supply.
    Both actions would further lower aggregate demand during a recession, making it worse.

Q3. Why do critics argue that deficit spending can crowd out private investment?

errorId: err-cia4u-fiscal-policy-definition | Bloom: analyze | Scaffold: L2

Achievement: Thinking | Action: analyze | OSSD: Demonstrate an understanding of fiscal policy in Canada, including how it is sha

Official: D2 | Node: CIA4U-D-OE02 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/2015cws11and12.pdf

Learning Goal: Explain the crowding-out debate and the Keynesian rationale for deficit spending during recessions.

Misconception: Students may think crowding out means government spending automatically eliminates all private business or that it is caused by tax increases rather than borrowing.

Remediation → err-cia4u-fiscal-policy-definition | Retest → err-cia4u-fiscal-policy-definition

  1. ✓ A. Government borrowing increases demand for credit, raising interest rates and making it more expensive for firms to borrow and invest.
    Correct. Critics argue that higher government borrowing raises interest rates, which reduces private investment.
  2. B. Government spending always eliminates existing private businesses.
    This is too absolute. Crowding out works through interest rates and credit markets; it does not automatically eliminate all private firms.
  3. C. Higher government taxes are used to pay for deficit spending, leaving firms with less after-tax profit.
    Deficit spending is financed by borrowing, not by current tax revenue. Tax-financed spending is not usually what critics mean by crowding out.
  4. D. The central bank reduces the money supply whenever the government borrows.
    The crowding-out critique focuses on interest rates and private borrowing, not on automatic central bank actions.

补救模块

err-cia4u-fiscal-policy-definition

Student confuses fiscal policy with monetary policy and does not recognize that fiscal policy is government decisions on taxation and spending made through the annual federal budget, not by the central bank.

Explain: Fiscal policy is the government's use of taxation and public spending to stabilize the economy. In Canada, the federal government makes these decisions through its annual federal budget, not the central bank (which handles monetary policy). During recessions, expansionary fiscal policy uses tax cuts and increased government spending to boost aggregate demand. To fight inflation, contractionary policy uses spending cuts and tax increases, though it is rarely used because it is politically costly. Keynesians support deficit spending in recessions to offset weak demand. Critics warn that deficits raise interest rates and crowd out private investment, but Keynesians reply that crowding out only occurs at full capacity; during recessions with idle resources, it does not apply. The fiscal multiplier is about 1 during booms and 2 during recessions; welfare and infrastructure spending have higher multipliers.

Example: The 2009 U.S. $800 billion stimulus reduced the severity of the recession, while post-2011 European austerity led to a 1% GDP contraction and 12% unemployment. These outcomes show that fiscal policy choices matter.
