    补救模块

    err-venn-start-single

    The student believes they can fill the single-set (only one sport) regions first, not realizing that the triple intersection must be placed before the two-set or single-set regions to avoid overlapping errors.

    Explain: When constructing a three-set Venn diagram, always begin by filling in the number of elements that belong to all three sets (the triple intersection). Then subtract that number from the given two-set intersections to get the values for exactly two sets. Finally, subtract the overlapping parts from each set total to get the number that belong to exactly one set. This order ensures correct totals.

    Example: In the three-sport example: volleyball=15, basketball=12, swimming=10, triple intersection=3, basketball&swimming=4, volleyball&swimming=4, volleyball&basketball=8. Start by placing 3 in the center. Then for volleyball&basketball but not swimming: 8 - 3 = 5. Similarly, basketball&swimming only: 4 - 3 = 1, volleyball&swimming only: 4 - 3 = 1. Then for only volleyball: 15 - (5+3+1) = 6, only basketball: 12 - (5+3+1) = 3, only swimming: 10 - (1+3+1) = 5. Total: 6+5+3+1+1+3+5 = 24, verifying totals.

    err-prob-and-or-confusion

    The student does not distinguish between the intersection (AND) and union (OR) of events. They may think 'and' means adding probabilities together, or they confuse the two terms in word problems, resulting in incorrect operations.

    Explain: In probability, 'and' refers to the intersection (overlap) of two events, while 'or' refers to the union (all outcomes in either event). For events A and B: P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A and B). This formula subtracts the overlap to avoid double-counting. If A and B are mutually exclusive (disjoint), P(A and B)=0, so P(A or B)=P(A)+P(B).

    Example: In a three-sport example with 24 total students: 15 play volleyball (V), 12 play basketball (B), 8 play both. P(V and B)=8/24=1/3. P(V or B)=(15+12-8)/24=19/24. Notice the union is larger and subtracts the overlap.

    err-union-overcount

    Student thinks that the number of elements in the union is simply the sum of the elements in each set, not realizing that overlapping elements are counted twice.

    Explain: When finding the union of two sets (A ∪ B), you must add the number of elements in each set and then subtract the number of elements that are in both sets (the intersection). This is because those overlapping elements are counted twice if you just add. The formula is |A ∪ B| = |A| + |B| - |A ∩ B|.

    Example: In a sports club, 18 students play volleyball, 20 play basketball, and 13 play both. To find how many play volleyball or basketball, do: 18 + 20 - 13 = 25. Simply adding 18+20=38 would double-count the 13 who play both.

    err-venn-no-verify

    Student thinks that after filling in the numbers from the given data, the region values are automatically correct without needing to sum them back to the original set totals.

    Explain: After filling all regions in a Venn diagram, you must verify that the sum of regions for each set equals the given total for that set. This catches arithmetic or logic errors, such as forgetting to subtract the triple intersection from a two-set overlap.

    Example: Three-sport example: volleyball=15, basketball=12, swimming=10, triple=3, basketball&swimming=4, volleyball&swimming=4, volleyball&basketball=8. Correct filling: triple=3; basketball&swimming only=4-3=1; volleyball&swimming only=4-3=1; volleyball&basketball only=8-3=5; volleyball only=15-(5+3+1)=6; basketball only=12-(5+3+1)=3; swimming only=10-(1+3+1)=5. Verification: volleyball total=6+5+3+1=15; basketball total=3+5+1+3=12; swimming total=5+1+1+3=10. All match. If you skipped verification, you might think the numbers are correct when they aren't.
