Q1. In Unit 3, which sentence best describes the main purpose of a persuasive essay?

errorId: err-persuasive-purpose-audience | Bloom: understand | Scaffold: L1

Achievement: Knowledge and Understanding | Action: interpret | OSSD: Use knowledge of writing forms, and of the connections between form, audience, a

Official: B2 | Node: OLC4O-B-OE02 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/english12curr.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-1

Misconception: Student conflates persuasive writing with storytelling or objective explanation.

Remediation → err-persuasive-purpose-audience | Retest → err-persuasive-purpose-audience

  1. A. To tell a personal story about a meaningful experience
    That is a narrative purpose. Persuasive writing aims to convince the reader, not just to tell a story.
  2. ✓ B. To convince the reader to agree with a particular position
    Exactly. Persuasive essays are opinion pieces meant to convince the audience.
  3. C. To explain a topic fairly without taking a side
    That describes informative or explanatory writing, not persuasive writing.
  4. D. To summarize the main points of another writer's text
    Summarizing is a different task; a persuasive essay argues a position rather than simply summarizing.

Q2. Read this short passage: 'Many students stay after class to finish homework. Longer library hours would also give students a quiet place to read. For these reasons, the school board should keep the library open until 6 p.m.' What is the main idea of this passage?

errorId: err-main-idea-vs-detail | Bloom: analyze | Scaffold: L2

Achievement: Thinking | Action: analyze | OSSD: Demonstrate understanding of the content and meaning of informational, narrative

Official: A3 | Node: OLC4O-A-OE03 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/english12curr.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-2

Misconception: Student mistakes a supporting detail or an example for the main idea.

Remediation → err-main-idea-vs-detail | Retest → err-main-idea-vs-detail

  1. A. Many students stay after class to finish homework.
    This is a supporting detail, not the main idea. It gives one reason for longer hours.
  2. B. The library has a quiet place to read.
    This is another supporting detail, not the central claim.
  3. ✓ C. The school board should keep the library open later.
    Correct. The main idea is the overall claim; the other choices are details that support it.
  4. D. The library is currently open until 6 p.m.
    The passage argues for this change; it does not state the library is currently open until 6 p.m.

补救模块

err-persuasive-purpose-audience

The student thinks a persuasive essay is meant to tell a story or explain a topic fairly, instead of to convince the reader to agree with a particular position. They also may not adjust their message for the specific audience.

Explain: In Unit 3, persuasive writing is an opinion piece. Its purpose is to convince the reader to agree with your position. It is different from narrative writing, which tells a personal story, and informative writing, which explains a topic without taking a side. The OLC4O expectation says writers must understand the connections between form, audience, and purpose, so a persuasive text must be shaped to convince the people reading it.

Example: Unit 3 moves from a basic persuasive essay to a final persuasive essay. For each one, you need a clear opinion and reasons that would convince your reader. If you just tell a story or list both sides without arguing, you are not meeting the persuasive purpose.

err-main-idea-vs-detail

The student mistakes supporting details for the main idea when reading persuasive texts, treating examples or reasons as the central claim.

Explain: The main idea is the writer's central claim in a persuasive text. Supporting details are the specific reasons, examples, or evidence used to prove that claim. When you read, ask: 'What is the author trying to convince me of?' The answer is the main idea. A detail may be true but it is not the main idea if it only supports the claim.

Example: Consider the passage: 'Many students stay after class to finish homework. Longer library hours would also give students a quiet place to read. For these reasons, the school board should keep the library open until 6 p.m.' Here, the main idea is the last sentence: the school board should keep the library open later. The first two sentences are supporting details that give reasons.

err-revision-surface-only

The student thinks revision only means fixing spelling and grammar and does not include adding content or details.

Explain: Revision is more than correcting capitalization and word conjoining. In Unit 2, students had to expand their narrative texts from 242 words to over 300 by adding details about setbacks and shared learning scenes. This means revision must also add meaningful content, not only fix surface errors.

Example: A student's narrative was 242 words and seemed complete. To revise it properly, the student had to add details about a setback and a shared learning scene to reach 300+ words. Fixing capitals and joining words was part of the process, but expanding the story with new content was the main goal.
