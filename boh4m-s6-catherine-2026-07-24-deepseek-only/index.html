Q1. Which statement about Tuckman's team development model is correct?

errorId: err-tuckman-linear | Bloom: understand | Scaffold: L1

Achievement: Knowledge and Understanding | Action: interpret | OSSD: Demonstrate an understanding of group dynamics.

Official: B2 | Node: BOH4M-B-OE02 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/business1112currb.pdf

Learning Goal: lg2

Misconception: Students believe teams always progress through stages in strict sequential order without going back.

Remediation → err-tuckman-linear | Retest → err-tuckman-linear

  1. A. Teams always progress through the five stages in order and never go back to an earlier stage.
    This is a common misconception. Teams can regress to earlier stages when facing challenges like resource cuts or personnel changes.
  2. ✓ B. Teams may regress to earlier stages when facing resource cuts or personnel changes, or may stagnate in storming or norming.
    Correct! Tuckman's model allows for regression and stagnation based on team experiences.
  3. C. Teams only move forward through forming, storming, norming, and performing; adjourning is optional.
    Adjourning is a standard final stage in the model, and teams can regress even from performing.
  4. D. Teams never leave the performing stage once they reach it.
    Teams can leave the performing stage due to changes, such as new members or shifting goals, and may regress.

Q2. In Tuckman's model, which stage is characterized by conflict and disagreement among team members?

errorId: err-tuckman-stages-confusion | Bloom: understand | Scaffold: L1

Achievement: Knowledge and Understanding | Action: interpret | OSSD: Demonstrate an understanding of group dynamics.

Official: B2 | Node: BOH4M-B-OE02 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/business1112currb.pdf

Learning Goal: lg1

Misconception: Students may confuse storming with norming, thinking conflict is normal during norming.

Remediation → err-tuckman-stages-confusion | Retest → err-tuckman-stages-confusion

  1. A. Forming
    Forming is the initial stage where team members are polite and try to understand their roles; conflict is minimal.
  2. ✓ B. Storming
    Correct! Storming is when team members start to express opinions, leading to conflict and disagreement.
  3. C. Norming
    Norming is when the team resolves differences and establishes norms; it is more cooperative than conflictual.
  4. D. Performing
    Performing is a stage of high productivity and collaboration; conflict is generally low.

    Performing is the fourth stage; the final stage is adjourning, where the team disbands.
  2. ✓ B. Adjourning
    Correct! Adjourning is the fifth and final stage in Tuckman's model, when the team completes its work and disbands.

    Storming is the second stage, not the final one.

Q4. A team had been performing well for months. After a key member left, the team started having conflicts and missed deadlines. Using Tuckman's model, explain what might have happened and why.

Achievement: Thinking | Action: analyze | OSSD: Demonstrate an understanding of group dynamics.

Official: B2 | Node: BOH4M-B-OE02 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/business1112currb.pdf

Learning Goal: lg4

Misconception: Students may think the team had to start over from forming or that performing is permanent; they miss the concept of regression.

Remediation → err-tuckman-linear | Retest → err-tuckman-linear

    Rubric: Full credit: mentions regression from performing to storming (or forming) due to the personnel change, explaining that the team lost cohesion and had to re-establish norms. Partial credit: identifies regression but does not specify stage. No credit: says the team is stuck in performing or does not use Tuckman.

    Sample: The team likely regressed from the performing stage to the storming stage. The departure of a key member disrupted established norms and trust, causing conflicts as members struggled to adjust roles. This regression shows that Tuckman's stages are not strictly linear; teams can move backward when facing changes.

    补救模块

    err-tuckman-linear

    Students often think teams always move forward through Tuckman's stages in a strict linear order, never regressing or stagnating.

    Explain: Tuckman's model describes five sequential stages—forming, storming, norming, performing, adjourning—but teams do not always progress linearly. They can regress to earlier stages due to changes like personnel turnover or resource cuts, or stagnate in storming or norming. For example, a team that was performing well may regress to storming after losing a key member, as new conflicts arise. Understanding this non-linearity is crucial for managing team dynamics.

    Example: Consider a team that had reached the performing stage. After a key member left, the team started having conflicts and missed deadlines. According to Tuckman, this team regressed from performing back to storming because the change disrupted established norms and roles.

    err-tuckman-stages-confusion

    Students confuse the characteristics of storming and norming, or mistakenly believe performing is the last stage, overlooking adjourning.

    Explain: Tuckman's model has five sequential stages: forming, storming, norming, performing, and adjourning. Storming is marked by conflict and disagreement, while norming is when the team resolves differences and establishes cooperative norms. Performing is about high productivity, and adjourning is the final stage where the team disbands after completing goals.

    Example: For instance, a new team might argue about roles (storming), then later agree on rules (norming). Many students think performing is the end, but in reality, teams eventually disband (adjourning).
