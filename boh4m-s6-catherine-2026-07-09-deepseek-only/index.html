补救模块

err-managers-vs-leaders

Students often think managers and leaders are the same or interchangeable, ignoring that managers hold formal responsibility for collective outputs and support others' work, while leaders may not have that formal role.

Explain: Managers and leaders are distinct concepts. Managers support others' work and take formal responsibility for collective outputs. Leaders may inspire but do not necessarily have formal authority. The four core management functions are planning, organizing, leading, and controlling. All managers need three skill sets: interpersonal (communication), technical (office software), and conceptual (big-picture problem-solving).

Example: In the lesson, a class poll unanimously considered Steve Jobs a strong manager, yet he was also a visionary leader. A manager like a project manager ensures deadlines are met and reports to executives, while a leader might inspire innovation without that formal accountability. The distinction is about formal responsibility.

err-management-functions

The student may think management functions include tasks like staffing, directing, or budgeting, rather than the core four: planning, organizing, leading, and controlling.

Explain: The four core management functions are planning, organizing, leading, and controlling. Planning involves setting goals and deciding how to achieve them. Organizing arranges resources and tasks. Leading motivates and directs people. Controlling monitors progress and makes corrections.

Example: For a school fundraiser: planning sets the goal and strategy; organizing assigns roles and gathers supplies; leading inspires the team; controlling tracks donations and adjusts if needed.
