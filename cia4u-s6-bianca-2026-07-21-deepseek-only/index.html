补救模块

err-cia4u-s6-ecommerce-definition

Student may believe e-commerce only involves physical goods or retail purchases, or misunderstands that it exclusively refers to online retailing, ignoring services and digital goods. They may also confuse the eight unique features.

Explain: E-commerce is defined as digitally enabled commercial transactions via the internet, web, or mobile apps. It covers all categories of online paid purchases, including goods (e.g., snacks, electronics), services (e.g., flight bookings, streaming subscriptions), and digital goods (e.g., software). It encompasses both individual and organizational transactions. E-commerce also has eight unique features, such as ubiquity and global reach, which distinguish it from traditional commerce.

Example: For instance, ordering a book on Amazon (goods), subscribing to Netflix (service), or purchasing a software license online (digital good) are all examples of e-commerce. Even a business buying office supplies from an online supplier is e-commerce.

err-cia4u-s6-ecommerce-development-timeline

The student may confuse the chronological order or the start/end years of the three e-commerce development phases.

Explain: E-commerce developed through three phases: Invention (1995-2000) featured static banner ads and simple retail sales due to limited bandwidth. Consolidation (2001-2006) saw home broadband enable complex services like flight booking. Reinvention (2006-present) is dominated by social, mobile, and local e-commerce driven by smartphones.

Example: Invention: 1995-2000 with banner ads and simple retail. Consolidation: 2001-2006 with broadband enabling flight bookings. Reinvention: 2006-present with smartphone-driven growth and on-demand delivery.
