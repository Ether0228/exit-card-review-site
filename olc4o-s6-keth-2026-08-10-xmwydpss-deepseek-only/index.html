Misconception: Students often translate 'pathos' as sadness, but it is the broader appeal to emotion.

Remediation → err-rhetoric-pathos-mistranslation | Retest → err-rhetoric-pathos-mistranslation

  1. A. Sadness
    Pathos is a rhetorical term meaning emotional appeal, not sadness. The correct term is broader.
  2. ✓ B. Emotional appeal
    Correct. Pathos refers to the appeal to the audience's emotions.
  3. C. Logical reasoning
    Logical reasoning is logos, not pathos. Pathos targets emotions.
  4. D. Credibility
    Credibility is ethos. Pathos is about emotional connection.

    补救模块

    err-rhetoric-pathos-mistranslation

    The student equates the rhetorical term 'pathos' with sadness, failing to recognize that pathos is the appeal to the audience's emotions, which includes a wide range of feelings beyond sadness.

    Explain: In the rhetorical triangle, pathos is one of the three persuasive appeals. It refers to the use of emotional connection to persuade an audience. This is a standard rhetorical term and should not be translated as 'sadness.' In Super Size Me, for example, Spurlock's raw footage of post-meal vomiting and mood swings is pathos because it creates an emotional connection through disgust, pity, and concern—not only sadness. Remember, pathos can evoke any emotion, such as anger, hope, fear, or sympathy.

    Example: In the documentary, when Morgan Spurlock experiences post-meal vomiting, viewers feel visceral disgust and concern for his health. That emotional response is pathos. The scene is not meant to make you merely sad; it makes you emotionally invested in his condition. Similarly, his mood swings generate frustration and worry, all of which are pathos.

    err-task-single-document

    The student believes that combining the Analytical Audit and the Rhetorical Analysis Essay into one document is acceptable, but the assignment requires two separate files; combining them results in an automatic zero.

    Explain: The Unit 3 assignment on Super Size Me consists of two separate tasks: the Analytical Audit and the Rhetorical Analysis Essay. The Analytical Audit has 8 short-answer questions (2-3 sentences each) covering film facts and personal reflections. The Rhetorical Analysis Essay is a 500-word, 5-paragraph essay. Both tasks must be submitted as separate documents. Missing either component results in an automatic zero.

    Example: Think of the two tasks as separate dishes: the Analytical Audit (8 questions) and the Rhetorical Analysis Essay (500 words). Just as a buffet requires separate plates, these two tasks must be submitted as separate files. If you combine them into one document, it is as if you failed to submit one of them, risking an automatic zero.

    err-essay-structure-omission

    The student thinks any essay with an introduction, body, and conclusion is acceptable, not realizing that the 500-word Rhetorical Analysis Essay requires a specific 5-paragraph structure where each body paragraph has a distinct analytical purpose.

    Explain: The Rhetorical Analysis Essay must be 500 words and follow a 5-paragraph structure: Introduction, Body 1, Body 2, Body 3, Conclusion. Each part has a non-negotiable job. Introduction: name the film (Super Size Me), the director (Morgan Spurlock), and present a thesis on persuasive effectiveness. Body 1: identify scene examples for each appeal (logos, pathos, ethos) with description. Body 2: compare appeals, distinguishing the most frequent from the most effective with justification. Body 3: give an overall judgment of long-term effectiveness, citing the real-world outcome (McDonald's cancelled supersize menu 6 weeks after release). Conclusion: summarize findings and reflect on critical media literacy. Omitting any of these sections means the essay does not meet the assignment structure, so it loses marks in Thinking and Communication.

    Example: Use this skeleton: Intro (Super Size Me + Spurlock + thesis) → Body 1 (Scene examples: 7+ hours of exercise = logos; vomiting and mood swings = pathos; US Surgeon General = ethos) → Body 2 ('Most frequent is pathos, but most effective is logos because...') → Body 3 ('McDonald's cancelled supersize menu 6 weeks after release, proving long-term impact') → Conclusion (summary + media literacy reflection).

    err-evidence-generalization

    The student assumes that general references to evidence are acceptable in analysis. They do not yet understand that a strong analytical argument requires concrete, named facts or scenes from the text to prove the claim and demonstrate close viewing.

    Explain: When you analyze Super Size Me, you must support every statement with a specific, verifiable fact from the film. Instead of writing 'the film uses statistics', say exactly which statistic: for example, it takes over 7 hours of continuous exercise to burn a supersize McDonald's meal. That precise detail is logos because it uses evidence to appeal to reason. Similarly, to show pathos, name Spurlock's on-camera vomiting or mood swings; to show ethos, cite the US Surgeon General or the doctors interviewed. The rubric rewards 'well-selected evidence' and 'nuanced reasoning', so using exact facts improves both your Thinking and Application marks.

    Example: Weak: 'The movie has a lot of scientific data.'\nStrong: 'The film uses logos when it states that a supersize meal requires more than 7 hours of continuous exercise to burn, making the audience realize the high caloric cost of fast food.'

    err-proofreading-reliance

    The student believes that using grammar-check tools or having tutors edit their work is sufficient proofreading, not realizing that independent self-proofreading is a required responsibility in the Communication category.

    Explain: Proofreading is the final step of the writing process and is your own responsibility. In the Unit 3 assignment, the scoring rubric's Communication category explicitly includes proofreading. Independent proofreading means you, the writer, carefully check your own work—reading aloud is an effective method. No tool or tutor can replace this step. The expectation is that you take ownership of your final polished piece.

    Example: For your 500-word, 5-paragraph essay, you should read each paragraph aloud to catch awkward sentences and spelling errors. A grammar checker might flag a comma, but it will not catch a confusing thesis or a weak transition. Just as the documentary used real footage and expert testimony to build credibility, your own careful proofreading builds the credibility of your work.
