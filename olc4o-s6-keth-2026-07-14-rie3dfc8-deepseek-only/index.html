Misconception: Thinks graphic text equals plain image without text

Remediation → err-graphic-text-define-as-only-image | Retest → err-graphic-text-define-as-only-image

  1. A. A text that uses only images to convey information.
    Graphic texts combine images with words, not just images.
  2. ✓ B. A text that integrates visual elements with written words to enhance understanding.
    Correct! Graphic texts use both visuals and text together.
  3. C. A text that is entirely made of bullet points and charts without any sentences.
    Even bullet points and charts are often accompanied by explanatory text.
  4. D. A text that replaces all written words with icons.
    Icons are only one element; graphic texts usually include written words too.

补救模块

err-graphic-text-define-as-only-image

The student thinks a graphic text is just an image without reading the text, missing the integration of visual and written elements.

Explain: A graphic text is not just a picture; it combines visual elements (like charts, icons, diagrams) with written words to convey information clearly. For example, an infographic about forest resources uses both images (trees, arrows) and text (labels, statistics) to explain data. Reading both parts together helps you understand the full message.

Example: Look at this forest resource infographic. It has images of trees and arrows, but also text labels like 'Deforestation rate' and numbers. Without the text, you wouldn't know exactly what the arrows mean. The text and visuals work together to tell you the story.

err-reading-skip-legend

The student thinks they can accurately interpret symbols and colors in a graphic text based on common sense or prior knowledge, without consulting the provided legend or labels.

Explain: Graphic texts often use symbols, colors, or icons to convey information efficiently. The legend is a key that explains what each symbol or color represents. Skipping the legend can lead to serious misinterpretation of the data or message. Always check the legend before drawing conclusions.

Example: In the forest resources infographic, a green arrow might mean 'increase' and a red circle with a slash might mean 'no logging allowed'. Without the legend, you might guess green means 'good' and red means 'bad', but the legend gives the precise meaning.

err-reading-order-scan-first

Student dives into details without first scanning the overall text, missing the main topic and context.

Explain: The first step in reading any graphic text is to scan the entire piece to get the main topic and overall structure. This helps you understand the context before examining details.

Example: For a forest resource infographic, you would first look at the title, major sections, and overall layout to know it's about forest composition.
