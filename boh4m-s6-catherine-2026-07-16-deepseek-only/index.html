Remediation → err-classical-conditioning-voluntary | Retest → err-classical-conditioning-voluntary

  1. A. A voluntary action chosen by the learner
    Classical conditioning involves involuntary reflexes, not voluntary actions. That description better fits operant conditioning.
  2. ✓ B. An involuntary reflex triggered by a previously neutral stimulus
    Correct! Classical conditioning produces automatic, involuntary responses.

  4. D. A random response unrelated to any stimulus

    补救模块

    err-classical-conditioning-voluntary

    The student believes classical conditioning produces voluntary actions rather than involuntary reflexes.

    Explain: Classical conditioning is a learning process where a neutral stimulus (e.g., a bell) is repeatedly paired with an unconditioned stimulus (e.g., food) that naturally triggers an involuntary reflex (e.g., salivation). After pairing, the neutral stimulus becomes a conditioned stimulus that alone triggers the same involuntary response, called a conditioned response. The learner is passive; the response is automatic and not under conscious control. This is different from operant conditioning, where the learner actively chooses voluntary behaviors to get rewards or avoid punishments.

    Example: Consider Pavlov's dog experiment. The dog did not choose to salivate when it heard the bell; it was an automatic reflex learned through association. Similarly, when a student automatically perks up or feels alert upon hearing the school bell, that is a conditioned involuntary response, not a voluntary decision.

    err-operant-conditioning-passive

    The student believes that in operant conditioning the learner is passive, responding automatically to stimuli, similar to classical conditioning.

    Explain: Operant conditioning is a learning process where the learner is active and behavior is voluntary. The learner's actions are shaped by consequences: reinforcement increases behavior, punishment decreases it. Unlike classical conditioning, which involves involuntary reflexes, operant conditioning requires the learner to actively engage in behaviors to receive outcomes.

    Example: A rat pressing a lever to receive a food reward (positive reinforcement) is an example of operant conditioning where the rat actively performs the behavior to get a desired consequence.

    err-negative-reinforcement-punishment

    Student confuses negative reinforcement (removing an unpleasant stimulus to increase a behavior) with punishment (adding an unpleasant stimulus to decrease a behavior).

    Explain: Negative reinforcement removes an unpleasant condition after a behavior to increase its frequency. Punishment adds an unpleasant consequence after a behavior to decrease its frequency. Both involve unpleasant stimuli, but negative reinforcement strengthens behavior while punishment weakens it.

    Example: A rat pressing a lever to remove an electric shock is negative reinforcement (behavior increases). Giving a student detention for talking is punishment (behavior decreases).

    err-classical-extinction-mechanism

    The student believes that extinction in classical conditioning is due to forgetting or memory decay, rather than understanding that the conditioned response disappears because the conditioned stimulus is no longer paired with the unconditioned stimulus, breaking the learned association.

    Explain: Extinction in classical conditioning does not occur because the animal forgets the conditioned stimulus. Instead, it happens when the conditioned stimulus (CS) is repeatedly presented without the unconditioned stimulus (US). The learned association between CS and US weakens and eventually the conditioned response (CR) stops. For example, Pavlov's dogs learned that a bell (CS) predicts food (US), causing salivation (CR). If the bell rings many times without food, the dogs stop salivating—not because they forget the bell, but because the bell no longer signals food.

    Example: Pavlov's dog: after conditioning, the bell (CS) triggered salivation (CR). To produce extinction, Pavlov rang the bell repeatedly without giving food. Initially the dog salivated, but after several trials the salivation decreased and eventually stopped. The bell still sounded familiar, but its predictive value was lost.
