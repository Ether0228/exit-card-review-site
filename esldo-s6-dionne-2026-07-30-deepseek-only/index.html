    补救模块

    err-echo-narcissus-character-confusion

    Confusing the roles and fates of Echo and Narcissus, such as attributing Echo's curse to something else or misunderstanding Narcissus's transformation.

    Explain: In the myth of Echo and Narcissus, Echo is a nymph cursed by Hera to only repeat the last words she hears. Narcissus is a youth doomed by his own reflection, eventually transforming into the narcissus flower. Their fates are distinct and important for understanding the story.

    Example: Echo cannot initiate conversation; she can only echo others. Narcissus falls in love with his own reflection and dies, becoming a flower. Do not confuse Echo's curse with Narcissus's self-obsession.

    err-theme-misidentification

    The student confuses the theme of excessive self-focus with pride. They may think Narcissus's downfall is due to pride in his beauty, but the core problem is his obsessive self-absorption, which leads to self-harm.

    Explain: The class identified 'excessive self-focus leads to self-harm' as the core theme, not 'pride leads to ruin.' Pride is about feeling good about achievements, but excessive self-focus is an unhealthy obsession with oneself. In the myth, Narcissus is not just proud; he is completely absorbed in his own reflection, ignoring everyone else. This obsession directly causes his death. The theme 'pride leads to ruin' is too broad and misses the specific harm caused by self-obsession.

    Example: Narcissus didn't just have pride; he was so in love with his reflection that he couldn't eat, drink, or interact with others. This excessive self-focus led to his death. For contrast, a proud athlete might still have healthy relationships, but a self-focused person like Narcissus isolates themselves and suffers.

    err-vocabulary-misapplication

    Students may confuse the meanings of vocabulary words derived from the myth, e.g., using 'narcissist' to mean a repeater or 'nemesis' to mean self-love.

    Explain: From the myth of Echo and Narcissus, we get several modern English words. A narcissist is someone excessively self-absorbed, like Narcissus. Nemesis means a rival or opponent who brings deserved punishment, like the goddess Nemesis who punished Narcissus. Echo means a repeated sound, like Echo's curse. Understanding these origins helps you use them correctly.

    Example: If someone only talks about themselves, they are a narcissist. Your rival in a sports game is your nemesis. The sound bouncing off a wall is an echo.

    err-assignment-length-requirement

    You may have forgotten the exact word count or structure required for Unit 2 Graded Assignment 2. The assignment requires a minimum of 250 words in 3 paragraphs: a theme introduction, examples from two myths, and a real-world connection.

    Explain: Unit 2 Graded Assignment 2 asks you to write about two myths that share a common theme. Your response must be at least 250 words long and organized into three paragraphs: first paragraph introduces the theme, second provides examples from the two myths, and third connects the theme to real life. Remember to reference your previous posts.

    Example: For instance, if you choose myths about excessive self-focus, your first paragraph states that theme, your second paragraph summarizes how Echo and Narcissus and another myth (e.g., Prometheus or Icarus) show it, and your third paragraph gives a modern example like how people obsessed with social media can harm their relationships.
