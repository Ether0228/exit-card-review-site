补救模块

err-descriptive-writing-definition

Student thinks descriptive writing is just listing facts or telling a story, not creating vivid mental imagery.

Explain: Descriptive writing prioritizes creating clear, vivid mental imagery for readers, rather than only stating basic, generic factual information about a subject. Effective description uses sensory details (sight, sound, smell, taste, touch) to paint a picture in the reader's mind.

Example: Compare: 'Alex entered the classroom' (basic) vs. 'Alex stepped into the warm, sunlit classroom, the faint scent of chalk dust hanging in the air' (descriptive, using sight and smell).

err-five-senses-observation

The student may not recognize all five senses (sight, sound, smell, taste, touch) when reading a description, often missing less obvious senses like smell or touch, or overcounting by including non-sensory details.

Explain: Effective descriptive writing uses intentional, detailed observation through the five senses: sight, sound, smell, taste, and touch. Each sense adds vividness and helps readers imagine the scene. When you describe something, ask yourself: What do I see? Hear? Smell? Taste? Feel?

Example: In the lesson, Alex described the cafeteria: 'The pizza smelled of oregano and cheese, and tasted salty and warm.' This uses two senses: smell and taste. Adding sight: 'I saw the gooey cheese stretching,' and sound: 'I heard the crunch of crust,' would make it even more vivid.

err-paragraph-structure

Student may think a paragraph only needs a topic sentence and details, omitting a concluding sentence, or may confuse paragraph structure with essay structure.

Explain: A standard descriptive paragraph consists of three core components: a clear topic sentence, supporting relevant details (especially 5-sense derived content), and a concluding sentence that wraps up the core idea.

Example: Topic sentence: 'The bustling farmer's market overflowed with vibrant colors and enticing aromas.' Supporting details: 'Bright red apples gleamed under the morning sun, and the sweet smell of fresh honey filled the air.' Concluding sentence: 'Every visit to the market was a feast for the senses.'

err-specific-vocabulary

Students may think that adding any adjective improves the description, rather than choosing a precise noun or strong verb.

Explain: Descriptive writing uses specific nouns, strong verbs, and targeted adjectives instead of generic words. Replace vague nouns like 'thing' with precise ones like 'canvas backpack'. Replace weak verbs like 'walk' with vivid ones like 'stroll'. Avoid vague adjectives like 'nice'—use specific ones like 'sparkling'.

Example: Weak: 'He walked slowly through the park.' Vivid: 'He strolled through the park.'
