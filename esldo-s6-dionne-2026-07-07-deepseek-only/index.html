Q1. Which of the following is a complete sentence?

errorId: err-sentence-fragment | Bloom: understand | Scaffold: L1

Achievement: Knowledge and Understanding | Action: interpret | OSSD: Organize ideas coherently in writing.

Official: C2 | Node: ESLDO-C-OE02 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/esl912currb.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-1

Misconception: Student may think a dependent clause or a phrase is a complete sentence because it has a verb.

Remediation → err-sentence-fragment | Retest → err-sentence-fragment

  1. A. Because it was raining.
    This is a dependent clause. It starts with 'because' and does not express a complete thought on its own.
  2. ✓ B. The dog ran.
    Correct! 'The dog' is the subject, 'ran' is the predicate. It is a complete independent clause.
  3. C. When the bell rings.
    This is a dependent clause. It starts with 'when' and needs an independent clause to be complete.
  4. D. After the game.
    This is a phrase, not a sentence. It lacks a verb and does not express a complete thought.

Q2. What is the error in this sentence: 'I like coffee, my sister prefers tea.'?

errorId: err-compound-conjunction-missing | Bloom: analyze | Scaffold: L2

Achievement: Thinking | Action: analyze | OSSD: Organize ideas coherently in writing.

Official: C2 | Node: ESLDO-C-OE02 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/esl912currb.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-2

Misconception: Student may think a comma alone is enough to join independent clauses, creating a comma splice.

Remediation → err-compound-conjunction-missing | Retest → err-compound-conjunction-missing

  1. ✓ A. It is a run-on sentence; it needs a conjunction like 'but' or 'and'.
    Correct! Two independent clauses joined only by a comma is a comma splice. Add a coordinating conjunction (FANBOYS) to fix it.
  2. B. It is a fragment.
    Both parts have subjects and verbs, so they are not fragments. The problem is how they are joined.
  3. C. It is correct.
    In English, two independent clauses cannot be joined by just a comma; this is called a comma splice.
  4. D. The punctuation is wrong; it should be a semicolon.
    A semicolon would fix it, but the core issue is that a conjunction is missing. Usually a comma plus conjunction is the standard fix.

Q3. Is 'Because she studied hard' a complete sentence?

errorId: err-complex-dependent-alone | Bloom: understand | Scaffold: L1

Achievement: Knowledge and Understanding | Action: interpret | OSSD: Organize ideas coherently in writing.

Official: C2 | Node: ESLDO-C-OE02 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/esl912currb.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-1

Misconception: Student may think a clause with a subject and verb is automatically a complete sentence, ignoring subordinating conjunctions.

Remediation → err-complex-dependent-alone | Retest → err-complex-dependent-alone

  1. A. Yes, it has a verb 'studied'.
    Having a verb is not enough. 'Because' makes this a dependent clause; it needs an independent clause to be complete.
  2. ✓ B. No, it is a dependent clause that needs an independent clause.
    Correct! 'Because she studied hard' starts with a subordinating conjunction, so it is a dependent clause. It needs to be attached to an independent clause like 'she passed the test'.
  3. C. Yes, it expresses a complete thought.
    It seems incomplete because it leaves the reader asking 'what happened because she studied hard?'
  4. D. No, it needs a subject.
    It actually has a subject 'she'. The problem is the subordinating conjunction, not a missing subject.

Q4. What type of sentence is this: 'Although it was raining, we went outside.'?

errorId: err-compound-complex-missing-independent | Bloom: analyze | Scaffold: L2

Achievement: Thinking | Action: analyze | OSSD: Organize ideas coherently in writing.

Official: C2 | Node: ESLDO-C-OE02 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/esl912currb.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-1

Misconception: Student may think any sentence with a subordinating conjunction and two clauses is compound-complex, ignoring the number of independent clauses.

Remediation → err-compound-complex-missing-independent | Retest → err-compound-complex-missing-independent

  1. A. Simple
    This sentence has more than one clause, so it cannot be simple.
  2. B. Compound
    Compound sentences have two independent clauses joined by a coordinating conjunction. This sentence has one independent and one dependent clause.
  3. ✓ C. Complex
    Correct! 'Although it was raining' is a dependent clause, and 'we went outside' is an independent clause. That makes it a complex sentence.
  4. D. Compound-complex
    Compound-complex sentences require at least two independent clauses. This sentence has only one independent clause, so it is complex, not compound-complex.

补救模块

err-sentence-fragment

Student thinks a group of words with a verb is a complete sentence, but it lacks a subject or does not express a complete thought.

Explain: A complete sentence must have a subject (who or what the sentence is about) and a predicate (what the subject does or is). It must express a complete thought. A fragment is missing one of these parts or does not express a complete idea. For example, 'Because it was raining' is a fragment because it starts with a subordinating conjunction and leaves the reader wondering what happened.

Example: Fragment: 'Because it was raining.' Complete: 'Because it was raining, we stayed inside.' Notice the independent clause 'we stayed inside' completes the thought.

err-compound-conjunction-missing

The student thinks that a comma alone can correctly join two independent clauses, not realizing that a coordinating conjunction is required to form a proper compound sentence.

Explain: A compound sentence joins two independent clauses (each has a subject and verb and can stand alone) using a comma and a coordinating conjunction. The seven FANBOYS conjunctions are: for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so. Without a conjunction, you create a comma splice or run-on, which is incorrect.

Example: Incorrect: 'I like basketball, my friends like volleyball.' Correct: 'I like basketball, and my friends like volleyball.'

err-complex-dependent-alone

The student believes that a group of words with a subject and a verb is always a complete sentence, even when it begins with a subordinating conjunction like 'because' or 'although'. They do not understand that such clauses are dependent and must be attached to an independent clause to form a complete sentence.

Explain: A sentence must have a subject and a predicate to express a complete thought. A dependent clause has a subject and a verb but is introduced by a subordinating conjunction (e.g., because, although, since). It does not express a complete idea on its own and must be joined to an independent clause. A complex sentence has one independent clause and one dependent clause.

Example: 'Because I studied, I passed the test.' The part 'Because I studied' is a dependent clause; it cannot stand alone as a sentence. The independent clause 'I passed the test' completes the thought.

err-compound-complex-missing-independent

Student confuses a complex sentence with a compound-complex sentence, believing that any sentence with a subordinating conjunction and two clauses is compound-complex, ignoring the number of independent clauses.

Explain: A complex sentence has one independent clause and one dependent clause. A compound-complex sentence has two independent clauses and at least one dependent clause. To be compound-complex, you need at least two independent clauses (like in a compound sentence) plus a dependent clause. If there is only one independent clause, it is complex, not compound-complex.

Example: Complex: 'Because I studied, I passed the test.' (1 independent: 'I passed the test', 1 dependent: 'Because I studied'). Compound-complex: 'Although I was tired, I went for a run and I felt better.' (2 independent: 'I went for a run', 'I felt better'; 1 dependent: 'Although I was tired').
