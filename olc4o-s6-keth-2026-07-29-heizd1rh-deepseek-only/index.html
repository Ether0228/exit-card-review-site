Q1. Which sentence is appropriate for a cover letter?

errorId: err-cover-letter-tone | Bloom: understand | Scaffold: L1

Achievement: Communication | Action: interpret | OSSD: Use knowledge of writing forms, and of the connections between form, audience, a

Official: B2 | Node: OLC4O-B-OE02 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/english12curr.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-1

Misconception: Student thinks informal language like 'didn't' is acceptable.

Remediation → err-cover-letter-tone | Retest → err-cover-letter-tone

  1. A. I didn't get the job before, but I'm really good at this.
    This contains contractions ('didn't', 'I'm') and informal tone. Use formal, full forms like 'did not' and 'I am'.
  2. ✓ B. I have developed strong communication skills through my volunteer work.
    Correct. This sentence uses formal language, no contractions, and clearly states a skill.
  3. C. I think this job is awesome and I want it so bad!
    Use of 'awesome', 'so bad', and exclamation mark is informal. Avoid emotional language in cover letters.
  4. D. I am writing to apply for the position of... wait, let me check the job title again.
    Hesitating or speaking to the reader in a conversational way is not professional. Keep the tone direct and confident.

补救模块

err-cover-letter-tone

The student believes informal language, contractions, or emotional punctuation are acceptable in a cover letter.

Explain: A cover letter is a formal business document. It must use professional, objective language. Avoid contractions (e.g., don't, didn't), slang, emotional punctuation (!, ..., —), and subjective words (e.g., stupid, awesome). Instead, use full forms (do not, did not) and a confident, neutral tone.

Example: Informal: 'I didn't get the job before, but I'm really good at this.' Formal: 'Although I have not held this exact role, I have developed strong skills through related experience.'

err-cover-letter-format

Student may believe decorative fonts or wider margins are acceptable.

Explain: A cover letter must follow standard professional formatting: 1-inch margins, Times New Roman or Arial font at 10-12 pt, line spacing 1.1-1.5, double space between paragraphs, left-aligned. Consistent font throughout.

Example: Correct: 1-inch margins, Times New Roman 11 pt, 1.5 line spacing, left-aligned. Incorrect: 0.5-inch margins, Comic Sans 14 pt, 2.0 line spacing, right-aligned.

err-cover-letter-intro

The student believes that asking if the position is still open demonstrates interest, or that restating their name and address in the introduction is necessary.

Explain: A cover letter introduction should clearly state the specific position and company you are applying to, and express your interest. Do not ask if the company is still hiring (it suggests you haven't researched) and do not repeat information already in your header (your name, address, etc.).

Example: Correct: 'I am writing to apply for the Marketing Assistant position at ABC Corp, as advertised on LinkedIn.' Incorrect: 'My name is John Smith, I live at 123 Main St. Are you still hiring for the position?'

err-cover-letter-body

Student does not understand that the body of a cover letter must directly connect their skills and experiences to the specific requirements of the job, using concrete examples.

Explain: The body of your cover letter should show how your skills and experience match what the employer is looking for. Instead of listing everything you've done, choose key experiences that directly relate to the job description. Use specific examples and describe contributions you made. This demonstrates that you are a good fit.

Example: For a marketing assistant position requiring social media management, you could write: 'In my previous role as social media coordinator, I managed Instagram and Facebook accounts, increasing engagement by 40% through targeted campaigns. This experience directly prepares me to handle your company’s online platforms.'

err-cover-letter-length

Student believes cover letters can be shorter than 200 words or longer than 400 words, or that there is no length limit.

Explain: A cover letter must be 200–400 words and fit on one page. This ensures it is concise yet provides enough detail to showcase your qualifications. Employers expect a focused letter; too short may lack substance, too long may lose their interest.

Example: Too short (150 words): 'I am applying for the job. I have skills. Please hire me.' Too long (500 words): A detailed essay describing every job duty from the past 10 years. Correct length (300 words): A well-structured letter with introduction, 2 body paragraphs connecting skills to the job, and a conclusion.

err-summary-irrelevant

Student includes personal opinions or irrelevant details in a summary, not understanding that summaries should only contain main ideas and key supporting details from the source text without adding personal views or extraneous information.

Explain: A summary is a concise restatement of the main ideas and key supporting details from a source text. It must be objective and free of personal opinions, judgments, or irrelevant information. Your summary should only include what the author wrote, not your own beliefs or additional facts not in the original. The maximum length is 250 words.

Example: Original text: 'The study found that urban heat islands increase temperatures by 3-5°C. Solutions include green roofs and reflective pavements.' Inappropriate summary: 'The study found that urban heat islands increase temperatures, and I think the government should invest in solutions.' Appropriate summary: 'The study reports that urban heat islands raise temperatures by 3-5°C and suggests green roofs and reflective pavements as solutions.'

err-summary-word-count

Student believes longer summaries are more thorough or forgets the 250-word limit.

Explain: Summaries must be concise and capture only the main idea and key supporting details from the source. The maximum word count is 250 words. Exceeding this limit shows poor selection of information and may include unnecessary details.

Example: Original summary (300 words): 'The article discusses climate change, its causes like greenhouse gases, effects like rising temperatures, and solutions such as renewable energy. It also mentions the Paris Agreement, which many countries signed. The author argues that immediate action is needed. In conclusion, climate change is a serious issue that requires global cooperation...' Revised summary (248 words): 'The article discusses climate change, its causes (greenhouse gases), effects (rising temperatures), and solutions (renewable energy). It highlights the Paris Agreement and stresses the need for immediate global action.'
