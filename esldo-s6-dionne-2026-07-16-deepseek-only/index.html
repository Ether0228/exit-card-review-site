Q1. Which of the following best describes the main purpose of persuasive writing?

errorId: err-persuasive-vs-expository | Bloom: understand | Scaffold: L1

Achievement: Knowledge and Understanding | Action: interpret | OSSD: Write in a variety of forms for different purposes and audiences.

Official: C1 | Node: ESLDO-C-OE01 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/esl912currb.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-1

Misconception: Student thinks persuasive writing only provides information.

Remediation → err-persuasive-vs-expository | Retest → err-persuasive-vs-expository

  1. A. To inform readers by presenting facts and explanations without opinion.
    That describes expository writing, not persuasive. Persuasive writing aims to convince.
  2. B. To entertain readers with creative stories and vivid descriptions.
    Entertaining is not the primary goal of persuasive writing; it is to convince.
  3. ✓ C. To convince readers to agree with the author's opinion or take action.
    Correct. Persuasive writing is meant to persuade or convince.
  4. D. To describe a process step by step so readers can follow instructions.
    That is instructional writing, not persuasive.

Q2. Which of the following sentences has the most appropriate tone for persuasive writing?

errorId: err-tone-inappropriate | Bloom: analyze | Scaffold: L1

Achievement: Communication | Action: evaluate | OSSD: Write in a variety of forms for different purposes and audiences.

Official: C1 | Node: ESLDO-C-OE01 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/esl912currb.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-2

Misconception: Student may think aggressive or too casual language is appropriate.

Remediation → err-tone-inappropriate | Retest → err-tone-inappropriate

  1. A. You dumb people don't get it, but uniforms are a good idea, okay?
    This tone is disrespectful and aggressive, not appropriate for persuasive writing.
  2. B. Wearing a uniform, like, totally saves time in the morning, I guess.
    This tone is too casual and unsure. Persuasive writing should be confident and convincing.
  3. ✓ C. School uniforms should be mandatory because they reduce peer pressure and save time for students.
    Correct. This sentence is confident, respectful, and makes a clear argument.
  4. D. I think uniforms might be okay, but I'm not really sure.
    This tone is uncertain and not convincing.

Q3. A student writes: 'According to Principal Smith, wearing uniforms leads to better focus in class.' Which persuasive appeal is being used?

errorId: err-appeal-misidentification | Bloom: understand | Scaffold: L2

Achievement: Knowledge and Understanding | Action: interpret | OSSD: Write in a variety of forms for different purposes and audiences.

Official: C1 | Node: ESLDO-C-OE01 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/esl912currb.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-3

Misconception: Student may confuse authority (ethos) with logic (logos) or emotion (pathos).

Remediation → err-appeal-misidentification | Retest → err-appeal-misidentification

  1. ✓ A. Ethos – an appeal to trustworthiness and authority.
    Correct. Quoting an authority figure builds credibility, which is ethos.
  2. B. Pathos – an appeal to emotion.
    Pathos appeals to emotions like fear, happiness, or sympathy. This quote is not emotional; it uses an authority.
  3. C. Logos – an appeal to logic and evidence.
    Logos uses facts, statistics, or logical reasoning. While the principal's statement could be logical, the appeal here is to the authority of the person, not the logic itself.
  4. D. Kairos – an appeal to timing.
    Kairos is not one of the three primary appeals taught in this lesson (ethos, pathos, logos).

Q4. Read the following persuasive paragraph and match the RDF (Reason/Detail/Fact) with the correct EE (Explanation/Example). 'School uniforms should be mandatory. First, they save time in the morning. [RDF] This means students can sleep a little longer and arrive at school more refreshed.' [EE] Which pair below correctly matches the RDF and EE?

errorId: err-rdf-ee-mismatch | Bloom: apply | Scaffold: L2

Achievement: Application | Action: apply | OSSD: Organize ideas coherently in writing.

Official: C2 | Node: ESLDO-C-OE02 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/esl912currb.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-4

Misconception: Student may confuse RDF with a different part or mismatch the explanation.

Remediation → err-rdf-ee-mismatch | Retest → err-rdf-ee-mismatch

  1. A. RDF: 'School uniforms should be mandatory.' EE: 'First, they save time in the morning.'
    The RDF in this mismatch is actually the topic sentence, and the EE is the RDF. The EE should explain the RDF, not repeat it.
  2. ✓ B. RDF: 'They save time in the morning.' EE: 'Students can sleep longer and arrive more refreshed.'
    Correct. The RDF states the reason, and the EE explains how that reason works.
  3. C. RDF: 'Students can sleep longer.' EE: 'School uniforms should be mandatory.'
    The RDF should be the main point, and the EE should give explanation. Here the EE is the topic sentence.
  4. D. RDF: 'Arrive at school more refreshed.' EE: 'First, they save time.'
    The RDF should be first, and the EE should follow logically. This reverses the order but also the roles.

Q5. Read the statement: 'Imagine how much happier students would be if they didn't have to worry about fashion every morning.' Which type of appeal is this?

errorId: err-appeal-misidentification | Bloom: analyze | Scaffold: L2

Achievement: Thinking | Action: analyze | OSSD: Write in a variety of forms for different purposes and audiences.

Official: C1 | Node: ESLDO-C-OE01 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/esl912currb.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-3

Misconception: Student may think any persuasive language is logos or miss the emotional focus.

Remediation → err-appeal-misidentification | Retest → err-appeal-misidentification

  1. A. Ethos – it builds credibility through authority.
    This statement does not mention any authority; it appeals to the reader's feelings.
  2. ✓ B. Pathos – it appeals to the reader's emotions by imagining happiness.
    Correct. Pathos aims to evoke an emotional response.
  3. C. Logos – it uses logical reasoning about fashion.
    Logos uses facts and logic; this is an emotional imagination, not a logical argument.
  4. D. None – it is not a persuasive appeal.
    This is a clear example of pathos, which is a persuasive appeal.

补救模块

err-persuasive-vs-expository

The student confuses persuasive writing with expository writing, believing that persuasive writing only provides information without attempting to convince the reader.

Explain: Persuasive writing aims to convince readers to agree with the author's opinion or take action. It uses strong language, emotional guidance, and clear stances. Expository writing, on the other hand, only informs or explains without trying to persuade.

Example: Compare these two sentences: - Expository: 'Learning English provides access to global opportunities.' (just informs) - Persuasive: 'You should learn English because it will open doors to a better future!' (tries to convince)

err-tone-inappropriate

The student believes that using casual or uncertain language (e.g., 'like, totally', 'I guess') is acceptable in persuasive writing. They may also think that an aggressive or disrespectful tone shows strong conviction.

Explain: Persuasive writing must use a tone that is confident (shows certainty), respectful (does not insult or belittle), and convincing (uses strong, specific language). Avoid casual words like 'like' or 'maybe', and avoid aggressive language that attacks the reader. Instead, state your opinion directly and support it with reasons.

Example: Weak tone: 'Uniforms are, like, okay I guess.' → Confident tone: 'School uniforms are beneficial because they reduce distractions.'

err-appeal-misidentification

The student may think all persuasive appeals are based on emotion or logic, or may not recognize the specific characteristics of each appeal (ethos: credibility/authority, pathos: emotion, logos: logic/evidence).

Explain: Persuasive writing uses three main appeals: Ethos appeals to trustworthiness and authority (e.g., quoting an expert). Pathos appeals to emotion (e.g., making the reader feel happy or scared). Logos appeals to logic and evidence (e.g., using facts, statistics, or reasoning).

Example: From the lesson: 'According to Principal Smith, wearing uniforms leads to better focus in class.' is an example of ethos because it uses an authority figure. 'Imagine how much happier students would be if they didn't have to worry about fashion every morning.' is pathos because it evokes emotion. And 'Uniforms save time, as shown by a survey where 80% of students got ready faster.' would be logos.

err-rdf-ee-mismatch

The student may think that any piece of information can follow a reason, or may confuse the order of RDF and EE. They might not see that EE must directly explain or illustrate the RDF.

Explain: In persuasive writing, each reason (RDF) needs a specific explanation (EE) that tells why that reason is convincing. The EE must directly connect to the RDF, often by giving details, examples, or further reasoning.

Example: RDF: 'School uniforms save time in the morning.' EE: 'This means students can sleep a little longer and arrive at school more refreshed.' Notice how the EE explains the benefit of saving time.
