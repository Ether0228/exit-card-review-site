诊断卡

Q1. Three sleds (12.0 kg, 11.0 kg, 7.0 kg) are connected by ropes A and B and pulled east with 120 N. Rope A connects only the 7.0 kg sled to the 11.0 kg sled. Rope B connects the 11.0 kg sled to the 12.0 kg sled. Which rope has the greater tension?

errorId: err-tension-equal-assumption | Bloom: understand | Scaffold: L2

Achievement: Knowledge and Understanding | Action: interpret | OSSD: Investigate net force, acceleration, and mass in qualitative and quantitative te

Official: C2 | Node: SPH3U-C-OE02 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/2009science11_12.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-2

Misconception: Assuming all ropes in a system have equal tension regardless of pulled masses.

Remediation → err-tension-equal-assumption | Retest → err-tension-equal-assumption

  1. A. Rope A, because it pulls the smallest mass.
    Rope A pulls only the 7.0 kg sled, but rope B pulls both the 11.0 kg and 7.0 kg sleds, so rope B must support more force. Consider which masses each rope directly pulls.
  2. ✓ B. Rope B, because it pulls the combined mass of the 11.0 kg and 7.0 kg sleds.
    Correct! Rope B must pull both the middle and right sled, so it experiences greater tension.
  3. C. Both ropes have the same tension because the system is moving together.
    Although the system has the same acceleration, the tension in each rope differs because they pull different masses. The tension must supply the net force for the masses it directly accelerates.
  4. D. Rope A, because it is closest to the front.
    Position alone does not determine tension. You must consider the mass each rope pulls.

Q2. Two blocks are pushed together with 160 N applied force. The large block (32.0 kg) experiences 80.0 N friction, and the small block (8.0 kg) experiences 20.0 N friction. What is the net horizontal force on the two-block system?

errorId: err-net-force-neglect-friction | Bloom: apply | Scaffold: L2

Achievement: Application | Action: apply | OSSD: Investigate net force, acceleration, and mass in qualitative and quantitative te

Official: C2 | Node: SPH3U-C-OE02 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/2009science11_12.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-3

Misconception: Using only applied force as net force, ignoring friction.

Remediation → err-net-force-neglect-friction | Retest → err-net-force-neglect-friction

  1. A. 160 N
    You forgot to include friction. The net force is the applied force minus the total friction force.
  2. B. 80 N
    You subtracted only the friction on the large block. You need to subtract both friction forces.
  3. ✓ C. 60 N
    Correct! Net force = 160 N - (80 N + 20 N) = 60 N.
  4. D. 140 N
    You subtracted only the small block's friction. You need to subtract both friction forces.

Q3. Three sleds are connected by ropes and pulled east. Which statement about their accelerations is correct?

errorId: err-acceleration-per-object | Bloom: understand | Scaffold: L1

Achievement: Knowledge and Understanding | Action: interpret | OSSD: Investigate net force, acceleration, and mass in qualitative and quantitative te

Official: C2 | Node: SPH3U-C-OE02 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/2009science11_12.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-4

Misconception: Thinking each object has its own acceleration calculated separately.

Remediation → err-acceleration-per-object | Retest → err-acceleration-per-object

  1. A. Each sled has its own acceleration depending on its mass.
    Because the sleds are connected by taut ropes, they all move together with the same acceleration. You must treat the whole system.
  2. ✓ B. All three sleds have the same acceleration.
    Correct! The ropes keep them together, so they all accelerate at the same rate.
  3. C. The front sled accelerates more than the back sleds.
    The applied force is on the front sled, but the tension in the ropes transmits force so that all sleds accelerate together.
  4. D. Acceleration depends on the tension in each rope, so they are all different.
    While tensions differ, the acceleration of every sled must be the same because they are connected and move as one system.

Q4. In the three-sled system (12.0 kg, 11.0 kg, 7.0 kg) pulled east with 120 N, the system acceleration is 4.0 m/s^2. Rope B connects the middle sled (11.0 kg) to the front sled (12.0 kg). To calculate the tension in Rope B using F_net = ma, which mass (or masses) should you use for 'm'? Explain your reasoning and then calculate the tension.

errorId: err-mass-addition | Bloom: apply | Scaffold: L3

Achievement: Application | Action: apply | OSSD: Investigate net force, acceleration, and mass in qualitative and quantitative te

Official: C2 | Node: SPH3U-C-OE02 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/2009science11_12.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-2

Misconception: Using only the mass of the object directly attached to the rope, or using total system mass.

Remediation → err-mass-addition | Retest → err-mass-addition

    Rubric: Full credit: Student identifies that Rope B pulls both the middle and rear sleds (11.0 kg + 7.0 kg = 18.0 kg) because it must accelerate both. Then calculates T = m_total * a = 18.0 kg * 4.0 m/s^2 = 72 N. Partial credit: Correct mass choice but no calculation, or correct calculation with wrong mass.

    Sample: Rope B pulls both the 11.0 kg and 7.0 kg sleds because it is attached to the middle sled and the rear sled is attached to it. So the mass is 18.0 kg. The tension is T = (11.0 + 7.0) * 4.0 = 72 N.

    补救模块

    err-tension-equal-assumption

    You assumed that tension is the same in all ropes, but tension depends on the total mass each rope directly pulls. A rope pulling more mass must have a greater tension.

    Explain: In a system of connected objects, the tension in a rope is determined by the total mass it accelerates. For example, in the three-sled problem, Rope A pulls only the 7.0 kg sled, while Rope B pulls both the 11.0 kg and 7.0 kg sleds (total 18.0 kg). Using Newton's second law, the tension in Rope B is larger because it must accelerate a larger mass.

    Example: In the three-sled problem with 120 N applied force and masses 12.0 kg, 11.0 kg, 7.0 kg, the system acceleration is 4.0 m/s². Rope B tension = (11.0+7.0) * 4.0 = 72 N, while Rope A tension = 7.0 * 4.0 = 28 N.

    err-net-force-neglect-friction

    Students forget to include friction forces when calculating net force on an object or system.

    Explain: Net force is the vector sum of all forces acting on an object. When friction opposes motion, it reduces the applied force. Always subtract the total friction force from the applied force to get the net force. For example, in the adjacent block problem with 160 N applied and total friction 100 N (80 N on large + 20 N on small), the net force is 60 N.

    Example: Two blocks are pushed with 160 N. Large block (32.0 kg) has 80.0 N friction, small block (8.0 kg) has 20.0 N friction. Total friction = 80.0 N + 20.0 N = 100.0 N. Net force = 160 N - 100 N = 60 N.

    err-acceleration-per-object

    The student thinks that each object in a connected system can have its own separate acceleration, calculated independently using its mass and the net force on that object, ignoring that all objects move together and share the same acceleration.

    Explain: In a system of connected objects, all objects move together. Therefore, they all have the same acceleration. To find that acceleration, treat the entire set of connected objects as a single object with a combined mass. Use Newton's second law: a = F_net_total / m_total. Do not calculate accelerations separately for each object.

    Example: Three sleds (12.0 kg, 11.0 kg, 7.0 kg) are connected by ropes and pulled east with 120 N. The total mass is 30.0 kg (12.0+11.0+7.0). The net force on the system is 120 N (neglecting friction). So acceleration a = 120 N / 30.0 kg = 4.0 m/s^2. Every sled has this same acceleration.

    err-mass-addition

    Students incorrectly use only the mass of the closest object or the total system mass when computing tension, instead of adding the masses of all objects the rope directly pulls.

    Explain: When a rope pulls multiple objects, the tension must accelerate the combined mass of all objects it directly pulls. Do not use the total system mass or only the adjacent object's mass.

    Example: Three sleds (12.0 kg, 11.0 kg, 7.0 kg) are pulled east with 120 N. The system accelerates at 4.0 m/s². Rope B pulls the 11.0 kg and 7.0 kg sleds (combined 18.0 kg). Tension = 18.0 kg × 4.0 m/s² = 72 N.
