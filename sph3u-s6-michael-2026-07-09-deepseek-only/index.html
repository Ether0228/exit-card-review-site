Q1. The position-time graph below shows the motion of a car. What does the negative slope indicate about the car's motion?

errorId: err-pt-graph-slope-direction | Bloom: understand | Scaffold: L1

Achievement: Knowledge and Understanding | Action: interpret | OSSD: Demonstrate an understanding of uniform and non-uniform linear motion, in one an

Official: B3 | Node: SPH3U-B-OE03 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/2009science11_12.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-pt-graph

Misconception: Negative slope on a position-time graph is often misinterpreted as deceleration rather than motion in the negative direction.

Remediation → err-pt-graph-slope-direction | Retest → err-pt-graph-slope-direction

  1. ✓ A. The car is moving backward.
  2. B. The car is decelerating.
    A negative slope on a position-time graph indicates direction (backward), not deceleration. Deceleration would be shown by a changing slope.
  3. C. The car is moving forward but slowing down.
    A negative slope means the position is decreasing over time, indicating backward motion, not forward motion.
  4. D. The car is stationary.
    A non-zero slope indicates motion. Stationary would be a horizontal line.

Q2. What does the slope of a position-time graph represent?

Misconception: Students often think slope of position-time graph gives acceleration because it relates to change in position.

Remediation → err-slope-acceleration-confusion | Retest → err-slope-acceleration-confusion

  1. ✓ A. Velocity
  2. B. Acceleration
    The slope of a position-time graph gives velocity, not acceleration. Acceleration is the slope of a velocity-time graph.
  3. C. Displacement
    Displacement is the difference between final and initial positions, not the slope.
  4. D. Speed
    Slope gives velocity (vector), not just speed (scalar). Velocity includes direction.

补救模块

err-pt-graph-slope-direction

Student incorrectly thinks a negative slope on a position-time graph always means deceleration, when it actually indicates direction of motion (backwards).

Explain: In a position-time graph, the slope represents velocity, a vector that includes direction. Positive slope means forward motion, negative slope means backward motion. The steepness indicates speed: steeper slope = faster motion, horizontal line = stationary. The sign of the slope tells direction, not whether the object is speeding up or slowing down. Deceleration would be indicated by a change in slope (curve) or a slope approaching zero, not simply a negative slope.

Example: Consider a car whose position decreases from 10 m to 0 m over 5 seconds. The slope is (0 m - 10 m)/(5 s) = -2 m/s. This negative velocity means the car moves backward at 2 m/s. It is not necessarily decelerating; it could be moving backward at constant speed. Deceleration while moving forward would show a positive slope that decreases over time (curved downward).

err-vt-graph-area-displacement

Students often think the area under a velocity-time graph gives the total distance traveled, but it actually gives displacement. The difference is that displacement accounts for direction (sign), while distance is the sum of absolute areas.

Explain: In a velocity-time graph, the area between the curve and the time axis represents displacement. When velocity is positive, area is positive; when velocity is negative, area is negative. The net area (sum of positive and negative areas) gives displacement, not the total distance. For example, if you walk forward 10 m and then back 5 m, your displacement is 5 m but distance traveled is 15 m.

Example: Consider a velocity-time graph: from t=0 to t=5 s, v=2 m/s (positive), area = 10 m; from t=5 to t=10 s, v=-1 m/s (negative), area = -5 m. Net area = 5 m, which is displacement. Distance = 10 + 5 = 15 m.

err-at-graph-area-velocity-change

Student thinks the area under an acceleration-time graph represents the acceleration value itself or the final velocity, rather than the change in velocity.

Explain: The area under an acceleration-time graph equals the change in velocity over that time interval, not the acceleration itself (which is the y-value) nor the final velocity (which depends on initial velocity). For constant acceleration, area = acceleration × time = Δv.

Example: Consider a constant acceleration of 2 m/s² for 5 s. The area under the graph is a rectangle: area = 2 × 5 = 10 m/s. This means the velocity changes by 10 m/s. If the initial velocity is 0, the final velocity is 10 m/s, but the area only gives the change, not the final velocity itself.

err-slope-acceleration-confusion

The student confuses the slope of a position-time graph with acceleration, when it actually represents velocity.

Explain: The slope of a position-time graph represents velocity, not acceleration. Velocity is the rate of change of position, so a steeper slope means faster motion, a positive slope means forward motion, a negative slope means backward motion, and a horizontal line means zero velocity (stationary). Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity, so it is represented by the slope of a velocity-time graph, not a position-time graph.

Example: Consider a car moving forward at a constant speed of 10 m/s. The position-time graph is a straight line with a positive slope of 10 m/s. The slope indicates velocity (10 m/s), not acceleration (which is 0 since speed is constant).
