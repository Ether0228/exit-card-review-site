Q1. In Act 5 Scene 3, which is the correct order of deaths at the Capulet tomb?

errorId: err-plot-sequence | Bloom: understand | Scaffold: L1

Achievement: Knowledge and Understanding | Action: interpret | OSSD: Read and demonstrate understanding of a variety of texts for different purposes.

Official: B1 | Node: ESLDO-B-OE01 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/esl912currb.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-1

Misconception: Students think Juliet dies before Romeo or that Paris survives.

Remediation → err-plot-sequence | Retest → retest-err-plot-sequence

  1. ✓ A. Paris, then Romeo, then Juliet
    Correct. Romeo kills Paris, then drinks the poison; Juliet wakes after Romeo dies and stabs herself.
  2. B. Romeo, then Paris, then Juliet
    Paris and Romeo fight before Romeo dies, so Paris dies first.
  3. C. Juliet, then Romeo, then Paris
    Juliet is the last to die, not the first.
  4. D. Paris, then Juliet, then Romeo
    Romeo drinks the poison before Juliet wakes and kills herself.

补救模块

err-plot-sequence

Students often misremember the order of deaths in the final scene, believing Juliet dies first or that Paris survives. In fact, the correct order is Paris, then Romeo, then Juliet.

Explain: In Act 5 Scene 3, the sequence of deaths is clear. Romeo arrives at the Capulet tomb and kills Paris in a fight. Then Romeo drinks the illegal poison he bought from the apothecary and dies. Moments later, Juliet wakes from her fake death and, finding Romeo dead, stabs herself with a dagger. So the order is: Paris first, Romeo second, Juliet last.

Example: Fact from the lesson: 'Romeo kills Paris at the tomb, then drinks the poison and dies. Juliet wakes moments after Romeo dies and stabs herself.'

err-theme-single-cause

The student thinks Romeo and Juliet die because of only one cause, such as the quarantined messenger or Romeo's impulsive action. In fact, the tragedy stems from overlapping factors: intergenerational hatred, information gaps, and random misfortunes.

Explain: In Act 5, Friar Laurence's messenger is quarantined due to suspected infectious disease, so Romeo never learns of the fake death plan. Believing Juliet is dead, Romeo buys illegal poison from an apothecary. At the tomb, he kills Paris, drinks the poison, and dies. Juliet wakes moments after Romeo dies and stabs herself with a dagger. The teacher states the tragedy stems from overlapping factors: intergenerational hatred, information gaps, and random misfortunes—not just one cause. The Montague-Capulet feud, the failed message, and bad luck all combine.

Example: Chenpin Tseng's alternate ending proposal fixes only the message delivery so the couple elopes. But even if the message arrived, the feud and other risks would remain. The tragedy is not single-cause; it is the result of hatred, miscommunication, and chance.

err-vocab-apothecary

The student believes an apothecary is just a regular shopkeeper who sells general items, missing the historical role of a person who prepared and sold medicines, often illegally. In Act 5, this misunderstanding obscures why Romeo's purchase of poison is a desperate and illegal act.

Explain: In Shakespeare's time, an 'apothecary' was someone who prepared and sold medicines, similar to a pharmacist, but they often operated without strict regulations. In Act 5, Romeo finds a poverty-stricken apothecary in Mantua who illegally sells him poison. This is not a casual purchase from a general store; it is an illegal deal that shows Romeo's desperation and the tragic consequences of his impulsive choice.

Example: In Act 5 Scene 1, Romeo goes to the apothecary and asks for poison. The apothecary hesitates because selling poison is against the law, but he is poor and needs money, so he agrees. Romeo says, 'I sell thee poison; thou hast sold me none.' This exchange shows the apothecary's illegal act and Romeo's willingness to break the law for death.

err-task-missing-step

Student thinks the forum task is just proposing an alternate event and forgets the assignment has three distinct parts: identify an event, outline the whole new ending, and reply to a classmate with a reason.

Explain: The alternate ending forum task has three required steps: (1) choose one Act 5 event to change, (2) explain the adjusted full ending that follows from that change, and (3) reply to a classmate's post with a clear reason. Missing any step means the task is incomplete. Your post should clearly show all three parts so that your thinking is complete and your classmate can respond to it.

Example: Chenpin Tseng proposed changing the failed message delivery so Romeo and Juliet elope. That covers step 1 (change the failed message event) and step 2 (the new ending is an elopement that avoids more deaths). To complete step 3, Chenpin would also reply to a classmate with a clear reason, such as 'I agree with your change because it removes the information gap that caused the tragedy.'
