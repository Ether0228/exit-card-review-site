补救模块

err-vector-direction

The student treats displacement as a scalar (distance) and ignores direction when adding vectors or computing velocity.

Explain: Displacement is a vector: it has both magnitude (how far) and direction (which way). When adding displacements, you must consider direction. For example, 5 m east plus 2 m east equals 7 m east, but 5 m east plus 2 m west equals 3 m east (not 7 m). Always include a direction in your answer.

Example: A car travels 700 m west, then 100 m east. The net displacement is 600 m west (since 700 west minus 100 east = 600 west). If you simply added the magnitudes, you would get 800 m, which is the total distance, not the displacement.

err-speed-velocity-confusion

You may be confusing speed (a scalar quantity) with velocity (a vector quantity). Speed only cares about how fast an object is moving, while velocity also includes the direction of motion. When calculating average velocity, you must use displacement (change in position, with direction) rather than total distance traveled.

Explain: Speed is a scalar that measures how fast an object moves, calculated as total distance divided by time. Velocity is a vector that measures the rate of change of position, including direction, calculated as displacement divided by time. For example, if a dog runs 43 m in 28 s, its average speed is 43/28 ≈ 1.54 m/s. But if it runs 43 m east and then 43 m west back to start, its displacement is 0 m, so average velocity is 0 m/s (even though speed is positive).

Example: A car travels 100 m east in 10 s, then 100 m west in 10 s. Average speed = total distance (200 m) / total time (20 s) = 10 m/s. Average velocity = displacement (0 m) / total time (20 s) = 0 m/s. The velocity is zero because the car ended up where it started.

err-unit-inconsistency

Student fails to convert between units (e.g., km to m) before performing calculations, leading to incorrect numerical results.

Explain: Unit consistency is essential in physics calculations. You must convert all quantities to the same unit (e.g., meters, seconds) before applying formulas. For example, if distance is given in kilometers and time in seconds, convert kilometers to meters (1 km = 1000 m) before calculating speed or velocity.

Example: A cyclist rides 2 km east in 120 s. To find average velocity in m/s, first convert 2 km to 2000 m, then divide: 2000 m / 120 s = 16.7 m/s east.

err-slope-interpretation

Student may misinterpret position-time graph slope as speed rather than velocity, or fail to relate slope to displacement and time.

Explain: On a position-time graph, the slope (rise over run) equals the average velocity. Velocity is a vector, so direction matters: positive slope means motion in the positive direction, negative slope means opposite direction. Speed is the absolute value of velocity.

Example: A line from (0 s, 0 m) to (5 s, 100 m) has slope = (100 m - 0 m) / (5 s - 0 s) = 20 m/s. This is the average velocity. Since the slope is positive, direction is positive (e.g., east).
