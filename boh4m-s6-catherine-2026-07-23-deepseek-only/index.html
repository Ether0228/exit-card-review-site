    补救模块

    err-task-maintenance-confusion

    Students may think that behaviors involving interaction or support are maintenance-oriented, but task-oriented roles also involve interaction focused on task completion.

    Explain: Task-oriented roles focus on getting the job done (e.g., initiator, coordinator). Maintenance-oriented roles focus on keeping the team cohesive (e.g., encourager, harmonizer).

    Example: Initiator (task-oriented) proposes new ideas; Encourager (maintenance-oriented) praises contributions.

    err-belbin-single-role-misconception

    Students often think each team member must have a single fixed role, but the Belbin model allows individuals to be suitable for multiple roles. This misconception can lead to inefficient team structuring.

    Explain: The Belbin Team Roles Model, developed by Meredith Belbin, identifies 9 team roles categorized into thought-oriented, action-oriented, and people-oriented. An effective team requires coverage of all 9 roles, but one individual can fulfill more than one role. People often have natural tendencies toward multiple roles, and roles can be fluid depending on the situation.

    Example: For instance, a person might be strong both as a 'Plant' (creative, imaginative) and as a 'Resource Investigator' (explores opportunities). They can contribute to the team in multiple ways, not just one role.

    err-blocking-role-negative-perception

    Students may think blocking roles are always overtly negative, failing to recognize that some, like the special interest pleader, are subtle and can appear positive while still hindering group progress.

    Explain: Blocking roles are negative roles that hinder effective group operation. There are 6 types: aggressor, blocker, self-promoter, dominator, help seeker, and special interest pleader. The special interest pleader may seem positive by advocating for a cause, but it diverts the group's focus from its main task to a personal or external agenda, thus blocking progress. Recognizing subtle blocking behaviors is key to maintaining group effectiveness.

    Example: A team member constantly brings up environmental concerns during a meeting about budget allocation, shifting discussion away from the financial plan. This is the special interest pleader blocking the group's core task.
