诊断卡

Q1. In a persuasive speech, which rhetorical appeal is based most directly on facts, evidence, and reasoning?

errorId: err-logos-ethos-confusion | Bloom: understand | Scaffold: L1

Achievement: Knowledge and Understanding | Action: interpret | OSSD: Demonstrate understanding of the organizational structure and features of a vari

Official: A2 | Node: OLC4O-A-OE02 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/english12curr.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-1

Misconception: Equating ethos (credibility/authority) with logos (logical evidence and reasoning).

Remediation → err-logos-ethos-confusion | Retest → retest-err-logos-ethos-confusion

  1. A. Ethos
    Ethos builds credibility through the speaker's authority or character, not through factual reasoning.
  2. ✓ B. Logos
    Correct. Logos uses logic, facts, evidence, and reasoning to persuade.
  3. C. Pathos
    Pathos persuades through emotion, not through factual evidence.
  4. D. All of the above
    Only logos is primarily logic-based; ethos and pathos use different persuasive mechanisms.

补救模块

err-logos-ethos-confusion

The student confuses appeals to authority or character (ethos) with appeals to logic and evidence (logos), treating an expert's credibility as if it were factual reasoning.

Explain: Logos persuades through logic, facts, evidence, and reasoning, such as statistics or if-then structures. Ethos persuades through the speaker's authority, expertise, or moral character, such as a doctor endorsing a product. To tell them apart, ask: is the argument convincing because of the evidence itself (logos) or because of the person delivering it (ethos)?

Example: From the lesson: 'Smoking causes cancer, so don't smoke' is logos because it uses a factual cause-and-effect claim. 'A doctor recommends a product' is ethos because it relies on the doctor's authority, not on evidence about the product.

err-pathos-logos-misidentification

The learner confuses emotional persuasion (pathos) with logical proof (logos), treating guilt, fear, or empathy as if it were factual evidence and reasoning.

Explain: Pathos and logos both persuade, but they work differently. Pathos targets emotions such as fear, empathy, or desire, often through vivid language and personal values. Logos appeals to logic using facts, evidence, and reasoning, such as statistics, studies, or if-then structures. To tell them apart, ask: Does the statement give me a factual reason, or is it pushing an emotional button?

Example: 'Not buying insurance means betraying your family' sounds like an argument, but it works by guilt and fear, so it is pathos. In contrast, 'Smoking causes cancer, so don't smoke' gives a cause-and-effect fact, so it is logos.

err-analysis-paragraph-order

The student confuses the required content of the three analysis paragraphs, for example by placing the evaluation of overall effectiveness before identifying specific appeals.

Explain: The assignment requires a three-paragraph analysis of the documentary 'Supersize Me'. Paragraph 1 must describe specific uses of logos, ethos, and pathos in the film and their roles. Paragraph 2 must identify the most effective rhetorical appeal and justify why it is the most persuasive. Paragraph 3 must evaluate the overall effectiveness of the film as a persuasive text, with supporting reasoning.

Example: A strong response begins with Paragraph 1 naming examples such as statistics for logos, a doctor's endorsement for ethos, and emotional scenes for pathos. Paragraph 2 then argues which appeal was most effective and why. Only in Paragraph 3 does the writer make a final judgement on the film's overall persuasiveness.
