Q1. After reading the first paragraph of a story, a student predicts: "The main character will probably fail the test because he didn't study." Which statement best describes this prediction?

errorId: err-prediction-guessing-without-evidence | Bloom: apply | Scaffold: L2

Achievement: Application | Action: apply | OSSD: Demonstrate understanding of the content and meaning of informational, narrative

Official: A3 | Node: OLC4O-A-OE03 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/english12curr.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-1

Misconception: Student equates prediction with random guessing, ignoring the need for textual clues, prior experience, or cultural background.

Remediation → err-prediction-guessing-without-evidence | Retest → err-prediction-guessing-without-evidence

  1. A. It is a guess without any textual evidence.
    The prediction actually uses a text clue: "he didn't study." A strong prediction is based on evidence, not a random guess.
  2. ✓ B. It is based on the text clue that the character did not study.
    Correct! The student used the detail "didn't study" as evidence to predict failure. This is a prediction grounded in the text.
  3. C. It is a summary of what has already happened.
    A summary recaps what already happened; this statement predicts a future event (failing the test), so it is not a summary.
  4. D. It is an emotional reaction to the character's behavior.
    The prediction is based on logic (not studying leads to failing), not an emotional reaction. The student used evidence, not emotion.

Q2. In your own words, explain the difference between making a prediction and writing a summary of a text.

errorId: err-prediction-confusing-with-summary | Bloom: understand | Scaffold: L1

Achievement: Knowledge and Understanding | Action: understand | OSSD: Demonstrate understanding of the content and meaning of informational, narrative

Official: A3 | Node: OLC4O-A-OE03 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/english12curr.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-2

Misconception: Student confuses predicting with summarizing, treating a prediction as a recap of what was already read.

Remediation → err-prediction-confusing-with-summary | Retest → err-prediction-confusing-with-summary

    Rubric: A strong answer must mention that a prediction is about what will happen next, based on clues from the text, background knowledge, or experience, while a summary is a brief restatement of key points that have already occurred in the text.

    Sample: A prediction involves using clues from the text, cultural background, or prior experience to guess what will happen later in the story. A summary, on the other hand, condenses the main events or ideas that have already been presented in the text, without guessing about the future.

      Correct! The prediction ignores the cultural background. In the story's culture, bowing is a sign of respect, so the prediction is flawed.
    2. B. It relies only on personal experience without using any text clues.

      The statement is a prediction about the character's intent, not a summary of what already happened. The confusion here is about cultural interpretation, not about prediction versus summary.
    4. D. It is a random guess with no evidence.
      The student used the action of bowing as evidence, so it is not a random guess. The issue is that the evidence was misinterpreted due to cultural ignorance.

    补救模块

    err-prediction-guessing-without-evidence

    学生可能将预测等同于随意猜测，忽略了使用文化背景、经验或文本线索作为依据。

    Explain: 预测是一种阅读策略，在阅读前、阅读中和阅读后使用，以预判文本内容。预测必须基于证据，包括文化背景、过往经验和文本线索。随意猜测没有依据，不是有效的预测。

    Example: 例如，读到‘他没有学习’，预测‘他可能会不及格’是基于文本线索的合理预测。而仅凭感觉说‘他会不及格’就是猜测。

    err-prediction-confusing-with-summary

    学生可能混淆预测与总结，认为预测是复述已读内容，而实际上预测要求预判未来内容，并使用文本线索、文化背景或过往经验作为依据。

    Explain: 预测与总结不同：预测是使用文本线索、文化背景或过往经验来预判将要发生什么，焦点在未来；总结是回顾已读内容，提炼主要事件或观点，焦点在过去。

    Example: 读一个故事开头：'小明早上起晚了，匆忙出门却忘了带书包。' 预测：'小明到学校后可能会被老师批评。'（基于'忘了带书包'这一线索预判未来） 总结：'小明起晚了，匆忙出门忘了带书包。'（复述已发生事件）

    err-prediction-ignoring-cultural-background

    The student thinks personal experience is enough for prediction, failing to consider that cultural background can change the meaning of actions and events.

    Explain: When making predictions, you must consider the cultural background of the story, including rules, traditions, and beliefs. These shape how characters act and what events mean. Ignoring cultural context can lead to inaccurate predictions. Always ask: 'What does this action mean in the story's culture?'

    Example: In a story where bowing is a sign of respect, predicting that a bowing character is apologizing is wrong if you only rely on your own culture. The cultural meaning of bowing changes the prediction.
