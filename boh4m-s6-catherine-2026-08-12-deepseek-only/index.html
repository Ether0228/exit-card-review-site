Q1. Which statement best describes strategic management in an organization?

errorId: err-sm-no-guarantee | Bloom: understand | Scaffold: L1

Achievement: Knowledge and Understanding | Action: interpret | OSSD: Analyse the importance of planning to the success of an organization.

Official: D1 | Node: BOH4M-D-OE01 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/business1112currb.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-1

Misconception: Believing strategic management guarantees business success.

Remediation → err-sm-no-guarantee | Retest → err-sm-no-guarantee

  1. ✓ A. A method that helps formulate, implement, and evaluate decisions to achieve goals, but does not guarantee success.
    Correct. Strategic management is a planning method, not a promise of success.
  2. B. A one-time decision that always guarantees an organization will outperform competitors.
    Strategic management is ongoing and never a guarantee; external factors and execution matter.
  3. C. A financial audit used only to reduce costs.
    That is a narrow operational tool, not the cross-functional strategic planning approach.
  4. D. A process that works only for large corporations, not small businesses.
    Strategic management can be adapted to organizations of different sizes.

Q2. A local bakery has a strong bread product but wants to enter a nearby city where it has no customers. Using the Ansoff Matrix, this is an example of:

Official: D2 | Node: BOH4M-D-OE02 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/business1112currb.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-2

Misconception: Confusing market development with market penetration because both involve selling.

Remediation → err-ansoff-confuse-strategies | Retest → err-ansoff-confuse-strategies

  1. A. Market penetration, because the bakery is selling more bread.
    Market penetration means selling the existing product to the existing market. Here the bakery is entering a new city, a new market.
  2. ✓ B. Market development, because the existing product is being sold in a new market.
    Correct. The bread is the existing product, and the nearby city is a new market.
  3. C. Product development, because a new product is being created for the existing market.
    The bread is the existing product; the city is a new market, not a new product.
  4. D. Diversification, because the bakery is expanding into a completely new industry.
    Diversification requires a new product and a new market; this scenario keeps the same product.

    补救模块

    err-sm-no-guarantee

    Assuming strategic management guarantees business success, when it is a method with no guarantee.

    Explain: Strategic management is a cross-functional method to formulate, implement, and evaluate decisions to help organizations achieve goals; it does not guarantee success. The formula is SM = SF + SI, and implementation depends on resource allocation. Even with a solid plan, organizations operate in competitive environments such as monopoly, legal oligopoly, and hyper competition, where success is never certain.

    Example: Apple releases a new iPhone each year as a product development strategy, but it still operates in hyper-competition with many brands and temporary advantages. Even with careful strategic management, Apple cannot guarantee success because competitors constantly respond and market conditions change.

    err-ansoff-confuse-strategies

    The student confuses market penetration (existing product, existing market) with market development (existing product, new market). They focus only on the product being the same and overlook the market dimension change.

    Explain: The Ansoff Matrix uses two dimensions: products (existing/new) and markets (existing/new). Market penetration means selling the existing product in the existing market to gain a larger share (e.g., via price cuts or promotions). Market development means taking the same existing product and selling it in a new market, such as a new geographic region or a new customer segment. The key difference is the market: in penetration, the market stays the same; in development, the market is new.

    Example: A company sells a successful snack in Toronto and decides to start selling the same snack in Vancouver, where it currently has no customers. This is market development because the product is existing but the market (Vancouver) is new.

    err-ansoff-diversification-types

    Mistaking all diversification for conglomerate diversification, ignoring horizontal, vertical, and concentric types.

    Explain: Diversification means entering a new product and a new market, but it has four types. Horizontal diversification offers a new product to existing customers. Vertical diversification expands upstream or downstream in the value chain. Concentric diversification introduces a new product that is technologically similar to current products. Conglomerate diversification moves into unrelated industries. The type depends on how the new venture relates to the current business.

    Example: A construction company producing its own building materials is vertical diversification because it moves upstream into supply. A bakery selling pre-made baking dough to consumers is concentric because dough is technologically similar to baking. Apple making cars is horizontal because it targets existing customers with a new product.
