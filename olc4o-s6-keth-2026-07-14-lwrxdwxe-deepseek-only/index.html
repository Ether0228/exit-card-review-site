补救模块

err-graphic-text-timeline-allowed

Students may think timelines are permitted for the assignment, but the lesson explicitly prohibits timelines.

Explain: For Unit 1 Assignment 2, you must create an original graphic text using one of the approved formats: visual article, comparison/versus chart, map/floor plan, statistical data display, process flow chart, or structural diagram. Timelines are specifically NOT allowed. The lesson states: 'Timelines are prohibited.' Using a timeline will result in a mark of zero.

Example: A student creates a timeline showing major events in Canadian history. This format is not permitted. Instead, the student could use a flow chart to show the sequence of events or a statistical display to show data over time.

err-graphic-text-purely-decorative-visuals

The student believes visual elements in a graphic text are primarily decorative, rather than serving an equal functional role alongside factual information.

Explain: In graphic texts, visual elements are not just decorative embellishments. They carry equal functional weight as the factual information, meaning both text and visuals are essential for communicating the core message. This is because visuals can enhance understanding, speed up pattern recognition, and improve retention. For example, a bar chart in a graphic about energy usage in Ontario shows data visually, making trends easier to grasp than a table of numbers alone.

Example: Consider a graphic text about the Canadian forestry industry: it might combine a bar graph showing GDP contributions, a map of forest regions, and accompanying text. The visuals (graph, map) are not mere decoration; they convey key data and relationships that the text alone cannot achieve as efficiently.

err-graphic-text-reading-skim-all-details

Thinks that reading all details carefully is the best first step when approaching a graphic text, but the recommended strategy is to preview the structure first to map content locations.

Explain: The 4-step standard reading process for graphic texts begins with Preview: scan the overall structure, type, title, and axis units. In timed tests, spend 10-30 seconds skimming headings and markers to map content locations, then directly look up specific sections for questions. This is more efficient than reading all details first, as it helps you locate explicit facts and analyze trends quickly.

Example: Consider the Canadian forestry industry overview graphic (GDP, employment, assets). Instead of reading every number, preview the title, axis labels, and legend. Then, when asked about GDP, you immediately know where to look. This saves time and reduces cognitive load.

err-reflection-bullet-points-allowed

The student believes that bullet points are acceptable for the reflection, but the requirement is a cohesive academic paragraph or essay of at least 500 words.

Explain: The reflection component of Unit 1 Assignment 2 must be written as a cohesive academic paragraph or essay, not as a bullet-point list. It must be at least 500 words and cover personal experience, challenges, core message clarity, and understanding of visual communication. The reflection is a formal piece of writing, not a list of notes.

Example: For instance, a correct reflection might begin: 'Creating my graphic text on renewable energy sources taught me how visual elements can enhance data comprehension. I faced challenges in selecting appropriate colors to convey urgency…' This is a flowing paragraph, not a set of bullet points.

err-graphic-text-originality-clip-art

Students may think that using a pre-existing graphic from the internet or a textbook without significant original recombination is acceptable for the assignment.

Explain: While you may reuse clip art and pre-existing images, the graphic text must be originally created by you. This means you cannot simply copy a graphic from another source. You must combine clip art elements into a new, original layout that communicates your own message. The assignment specifically prohibits AI generation, copying, translation tools, or third-party assistance. Violation results in an automatic zero.

Example: A student who copies an existing infographic from a website and submits it as-is is not allowed. Another student selects clip art images of a tree, a factory, and a graph, arranges them into a new infographic about deforestation impacts, and adds original text – that meets the originality requirement.
