Q1. In the Uranus myth, which children did Uranus imprison in Tartarus?

errorId: err-myth-uranus-imprisons-all-children | Bloom: understand | Scaffold: L1

Achievement: Knowledge and Understanding | Action: interpret | OSSD: Read and demonstrate understanding of a variety of texts for different purposes.

Official: B1 | Node: ESLDO-B-OE01 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/esl912currb.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-1

Misconception: Student thinks Uranus imprisoned all his children (including Titans).

Remediation → err-myth-uranus-imprisons-all-children | Retest → err-myth-uranus-imprisons-all-children

  1. A. All his children, including the Titans
    Uranus did not imprison the Titans. He only imprisoned the Hecatoncheires and Cyclopes.
  2. ✓ B. Only the Hecatoncheires and Cyclopes
    Correct! Uranus was afraid of the Hecatoncheires and Cyclopes, so he locked them in Tartarus.
  3. C. Only the Titans
    The Titans were not imprisoned. In fact, they later rebelled against Uranus.
  4. D. Only the Hecatoncheires
    He also imprisoned the Cyclopes. Both groups were sent to Tartarus.

Q2. Which of the following best defines the word 'banish'?

Achievement: Knowledge and Understanding | Action: interpret | OSSD: Use a variety of strategies to build vocabulary.

Official: B3 | Node: ESLDO-B-OE03 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/esl912currb.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-2

Misconception: Student confuses 'banish' with 'imprison' (lock up) or 'kill' (cause death).

Remediation → err-vocab-banish-definition | Retest → err-vocab-banish-definition

  1. A. To lock someone in a prison
    That is 'imprison'. Banish means to force someone to leave a place permanently.
  2. ✓ B. To force someone to leave a place permanently
    Correct! Banish means to officially send someone away as a punishment.
  3. C. To kill someone for a crime
    That is 'execute' or 'put to death'. Banish does not mean kill.
  4. D. To scare someone into hiding
    That is not the definition. Banish is an official action, not just scaring.

    补救模块

    err-myth-uranus-imprisons-all-children

    Student thinks Uranus imprisoned all his children, but he only imprisoned the Hecatoncheires and Cyclopes. The Titans were left free and later rebelled.

    Explain: In the myth, Uranus was afraid of his children's power. He imprisoned only the Hecatoncheires (100-armed giants) and the Cyclopes (one-eyed builders) in Tartarus. The Titans, including Chronos, were not imprisoned. They remained free and later, led by Chronos, rebelled against Uranus.

    Example: Uranus imprisoned the Hecatoncheires and Cyclopes in Tartarus because he feared they would overthrow him. The Titans were not imprisoned. In fact, Chronos, a Titan, used a sickle made by Gaia to defeat Uranus.

    err-vocab-banish-definition

    Student thinks 'banish' means to imprison or kill, but it means to permanently expel someone from a place.

    Explain: The word 'banish' means to force someone to leave a place permanently, often as a punishment. It is different from 'imprison' (lock up) or 'kill' (cause death). In the Uranus myth, Uranus imprisoned his children in Tartarus; he did not banish them. Banishment would have meant sending them away forever, not locking them in a prison.

    Example: If the king banishes a criminal, the criminal must leave the kingdom and cannot return. In contrast, if the king imprisons the criminal, the criminal is kept in a jail.

    err-character-gaia-creation

    The student believes Gaia was the first being or that she created everything, but the myth begins with Chaos. Gaia emerged from Chaos and later created Uranus, but she did not create Chaos, Tartarus, or Eros.

    Explain: In the Greek myth, the first being was not Gaia but Chaos. From Chaos came Gaia (Earth), Tartarus (the underworld), and Eros (love). Later, Gaia created Uranus (the sky), but she did not create everything. She was not the origin of the universe.

    Example: The myth says: 'First there was Chaos. Then Gaia, the Earth, appeared.' So Gaia is not the very first being. She created Uranus, but Chaos, Tartarus, and Eros already existed before her creations.

    err-theme-misidentification

    Student may think any general positive or negative statement can be a theme, without checking if it is directly supported by the events of the myth.

    Explain: A theme is a core lesson or message that the story teaches. In the Uranus myth, the main themes are about fear leading to harmful choices, power needing to be fair, responsibility to oppose injustice, and family conflicts escalating. To identify a theme, ask: 'What does this story show about life or human nature?' Then check if the story's events actually support that message.

    Example: For the Uranus myth, the theme 'Fear can lead people to make harmful choices' is supported because Uranus feared his children and imprisoned them, which led to his downfall. The theme 'Love can overcome any obstacle' is not supported because love is not a major factor in the story.

    err-connection-format

    The student thinks they can write a connection as a short phrase or note, not realizing it must start with a sentence starter and be a complete sentence to clearly express the idea.

    Explain: When making a personal or world connection to the myth, you must use a sentence starter from the lesson and write a complete sentence. A complete sentence has a subject and a verb and expresses a full thought. The starter helps the reader understand the type of connection. Without a starter or with a fragment, your connection is unclear.

    Example: Bad: 'Reminds me of when I made a bad choice.' (incomplete, no starter) Good: 'This story reminds me of a time when I made a bad choice because I was afraid.' (starter + complete sentence)
