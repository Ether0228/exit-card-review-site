Q1. In the two‑box problem, the force that the large box exerts on the small box is:

errorId: err-newton3-force-equal-applied | Bloom: analyze | Scaffold: L2

Achievement: Knowledge and Understanding | Action: analyze | OSSD: Investigate net force, acceleration, and mass in qualitative and quantitative te

Official: C2 | Node: SPH3U-C-OE02 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/2009science11_12.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-1

Misconception: Students believe the force between boxes equals the applied force.

Remediation → err-newton3-force-equal-applied | Retest → err-newton3-force-equal-applied

  1. A. equal to the applied force (160 N)
    Action‑reaction pairs are between the boxes, not between the hand and the small box. The force between boxes is not the same as the applied force.
  2. B. equal to the friction force on the small box
    The small box accelerates, so the net force on it is not zero. The force from the large box must provide both the friction and the acceleration.
  3. C. equal to the mass of the small box times its acceleration
    This is the net force on the small box, not the force exerted by the large box. You must add the friction force to get the contact force.
  4. ✓ D. equal to the mass of the small box times its acceleration plus the friction force on the small box
    Correct. The large box pushes the small box, so the force must overcome friction and provide net force.

Q2. To find the acceleration of the two‑box system, you should divide the net force by:

errorId: err-accel-force-partial-mass | Bloom: apply | Scaffold: L1

Achievement: Application | Action: apply | OSSD: Investigate net force, acceleration, and mass in qualitative and quantitative te

Official: C2 | Node: SPH3U-C-OE02 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/2009science11_12.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-2

Misconception: Students use only one mass, often the smaller or the one where force is applied.

Remediation → err-accel-force-partial-mass | Retest → err-accel-force-partial-mass

  1. A. the mass of the large box (32 kg)
    The applied force accelerates the entire system, not just one box. You must use the total mass.
  2. ✓ B. the total mass of both boxes (40 kg)
    Correct. Newton's second law applies to the whole system using total mass.
  3. C. the mass of the small box (8 kg)
    The small box is part of the system, but the net force acts on the system, so total mass is needed.
  4. D. the average mass of the two boxes
    The average mass is not physically meaningful here; use total mass.

Q3. A 3 kg wooden block on a horizontal floor has μₛ = 0.50. A horizontal force of 6.8 N is applied. What is the static friction force?

Official: C2 | Node: SPH3U-C-OE02 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/2009science11_12.pdf

Misconception: Students always use μₛN even when applied force is less.

Remediation → err-static-friction-always-maximum | Retest → err-static-friction-always-maximum

  1. A. 14.7 N
    This is the maximum possible static friction, but the applied force is less, so the static friction is not at its maximum.
  2. ✓ B. 6.8 N
    Correct. Since the applied force is less than the maximum static friction, the block does not move, and static friction equals the applied force.
  3. C. 0 N
    The block is not moving, but static friction is present and balances the applied force to prevent motion.
  4. D. 9.8 N
    9.8 N is the weight, not the friction force. The maximum static friction is 14.7 N, not this value.

补救模块

err-newton3-force-equal-applied

In the two-box problem, students often think the large box pushes the small box with the same 160 N applied force, not accounting for friction and the fact that the large box also experiences friction and must accelerate itself.

Explain: Newton's third law applies to interacting pairs. In a two-box system pushed by an external force, the force between boxes is not that external force. Instead, to find the force one box exerts on another, consider the net force on that box. For example, on the small box, net force equals its mass times acceleration. That net force is the difference between the force from the large box and the friction on the small box. Therefore, the force from large on small = mass_small * a + friction_small.

Example: In the two-box problem (32 kg and 8 kg, applied 160 N, friction 80 N on large, 20 N on small, acceleration 1.5 m/s²), the force large box exerts on small box is: F_large_on_small = 8 * 1.5 + 20 = 32 N east. This is much less than the 160 N applied force.

err-accel-force-partial-mass

Students think that the acceleration of a system can be found by dividing net force by the mass of a single object, rather than the total mass of all objects in the system.

Explain: When calculating acceleration of a system of objects that move together, use Newton's second law (F_net = m_total * a). The net force is the sum of all external forces. The total mass is the sum of all individual masses. Dividing net force by only part of the mass gives a larger acceleration than actually occurs because the force must move all the mass.

Example: In the two-box problem, a worker pushes a 32.0 kg box and an 8.0 kg box (total 40.0 kg) with 160.0 N. Friction on the large box is 80.0 N, on the small box is 20.0 N. Net force = 160 - 80 - 20 = 60.0 N. Acceleration = 60.0 N / 40.0 kg = 1.5 m/s². If you incorrectly used only the 32.0 kg mass, you'd get 60.0/32.0 = 1.875 m/s², which is too high.

err-static-friction-always-maximum

You may believe that static friction is always at its maximum (μ_s * N), even when the applied force is smaller. In reality, static friction adjusts to match the applied force up to that maximum.

Explain: Static friction opposes the start of motion. Its magnitude can vary from 0 up to a maximum given by f_s_max = μ_s * N. If the applied force is less than f_s_max, the object does not move and the static friction force equals the applied force in magnitude, opposite in direction. Only when the applied force reaches or exceeds f_s_max does friction become constant at f_s_max.

Example: A 3 kg wooden block on a horizontal floor has μ_s = 0.50. The maximum static friction is f_s_max = 0.50 * 3 * 9.8 = 14.7 N. A horizontal force of 6.8 N is applied, which is less than 14.7 N. Therefore, the block does not move, and static friction is 6.8 N opposite to the applied force.
