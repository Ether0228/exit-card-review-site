Q1. In a velocity-time graph for an object with uniform acceleration, what does the slope of the line represent?

errorId: err-motion-graph-slope | Bloom: understand | Scaffold: L1

Achievement: Knowledge and Understanding | Action: interpret | OSSD: Demonstrate an understanding of uniform and non-uniform linear motion, in one an

Official: B3 | Node: SPH3U-B-OE03 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/2009science11_12.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-1

Misconception: Students often think the slope of a v-t graph gives the velocity value.

Remediation → err-motion-graph-slope | Retest → err-motion-graph-slope

  1. A. The initial velocity of the object
    The initial velocity is the y-intercept, not the slope.
  2. ✓ B. The acceleration of the object
    Correct. The slope of a v-t graph is change in velocity over time, which is acceleration.
  3. C. The displacement of the object
    Displacement is the area under the v-t graph, not the slope.
  4. D. The average velocity over the time interval
    Average velocity over an interval is total displacement divided by time, not the slope at a point.

Learning Goal: lg-2

Misconception: Students apply the five equations to any motion without checking if acceleration is constant.

Remediation → err-equation-applicability | Retest → err-equation-applicability

  1. ✓ A. Only when the acceleration is constant
    Correct. The equations are derived assuming constant acceleration.
  2. B. For any type of motion, as long as the initial velocity is zero
    Even with zero initial velocity, the acceleration must be constant.

    Knowing displacement does not guarantee constant acceleration; the equations would not be valid if acceleration changes.

Q4. If we choose the upward direction as positive, what is the acceleration of an object in free fall near the Earth's surface?

errorId: err-free-fall-sign | Bloom: apply | Scaffold: L2

Official: B3 | Node: SPH3U-B-OE03 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/2009science11_12.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-4

Misconception: Students think free fall acceleration is always +9.8 m/s² regardless of coordinate axes.

Remediation → err-free-fall-sign | Retest → err-free-fall-sign

  1. A. 9.8 m/s² upward
    Gravity always pulls downward; if upward is positive, acceleration should be negative.
  2. ✓ B. -9.8 m/s² downward
    Correct. The acceleration due to gravity is 9.8 m/s² downward, so with upward positive, the sign is negative.
  3. C. 0 m/s²
    Free fall means only gravity acts, so acceleration is nonzero (g).
  4. D. 9.8 m/s² but direction depends on the object's motion
    The acceleration due to gravity is constant in magnitude and direction regardless of motion; only the sign depends on the coordinate axis.

补救模块

err-motion-graph-slope

Confuses the slope of a velocity-time graph with velocity instead of acceleration.

Explain: In a velocity-time graph, the slope (rise/run) represents the rate of change of velocity, which is acceleration. The value of velocity at a point is read directly from the y-axis. For a straight line with uniform acceleration, the slope is constant and equals the acceleration.

Example: Consider a sports car with initial velocity 20 m/s and constant acceleration 3.2 m/s² over 5 s. The v-t graph is a straight line from (0,20) to (5,36). The slope is (36-20)/(5-0)=16/5=3.2 m/s², which is the acceleration, not the velocity. The velocity at any time is the y-value.

err-equation-applicability

Students think the five kinematic equations can be used for any motion, even when acceleration is not constant.

Explain: The five kinematic equations (v = u + at, s = ut + ½at², v² = u² + 2as, etc.) are derived from the assumption that acceleration is constant. If acceleration changes during the motion, these equations no longer apply. For example, if a car accelerates and then brakes, the acceleration is not constant over the whole trip. In such cases, each segment with constant acceleration must be treated separately or different methods (e.g., calculus) are needed.

Example: In the dart impact problem, the dart decelerates from 380 m/s to rest in 0.05 s with constant acceleration -7.04×10⁴ m/s², so the equations work. But if the acceleration varied, we could not use them.

err-instantaneous-vs-average

Confusing the area under a velocity-time graph with instantaneous velocity; thinking that the area gives velocity instead of displacement.

Explain: Instantaneous velocity is the velocity at a single moment, read directly from the y-axis of a velocity-time graph. In contrast, the area under the velocity-time graph between two times gives the displacement (change in position) over that time interval. Average velocity is total displacement divided by total time. They are different quantities.

Example: Consider a sports car with initial velocity 20 m/s and acceleration 3.2 m/s² for 5 seconds. The instantaneous velocity at t=5 s is v = 20 + (3.2)(5) = 36 m/s. The displacement over 5 s is 140 m (from the area under the v-t graph, which is a trapezoid: average velocity 28 m/s times 5 s = 140 m). The average velocity is 140 m / 5 s = 28 m/s. Notice that instantaneous velocity at t=5 s (36 m/s) is not the same as the average velocity (28 m/s).

err-free-fall-sign

Treating free fall acceleration as positive 9.8 m/s² without considering direction convention relative to chosen coordinate axis.

Explain: Free fall acceleration on Earth is always 9.8 m/s² directed downward. The sign in kinematic equations depends on your chosen coordinate system. If upward is positive, acceleration is -9.8 m/s². If downward is positive, acceleration is +9.8 m/s². Always define your positive axis first and assign the sign accordingly.

Example: Drop a ball from rest. Define upward as positive. Using v = u + at: u = 0 m/s, a = -9.8 m/s², t = 1 s → v = -9.8 m/s (downward). The negative sign confirms direction.
