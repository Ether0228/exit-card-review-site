Q1. Which of the following is a correct parenthetical citation for a book by Smith (2020)?

errorId: err-intext-parenthetical-vs-narrative | Bloom: understand | Scaffold: L1

Achievement: Knowledge and Understanding | Action: interpret | OSSD: Demonstrate the use of appropriate communication techniques related to business

Official: A2 | Node: BOH4M-A-OE02 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/business1112currb.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-1

Misconception: Students think the citation must be part of the sentence subject or misplace the year.

Remediation → err-intext-parenthetical-vs-narrative | Retest → err-intext-parenthetical-vs-narrative

  1. A. Smith (2020) argues that management is key.
    That's a narrative citation. Parenthetical places both author and year in parentheses at the end of the sentence.
  2. ✓ B. Management is key (Smith, 2020).
    Correct. Parenthetical citation includes author and year in parentheses before the period.
  3. C. Smith, 2020, argues that management is key.
    This format is not APA style. The year should be in parentheses.
  4. D. (Smith, 2020) argues that management is key.
    The parentheses should contain both author and year, but the sentence structure is incorrect. The citation should not be part of the subject.

Q2. A source has four authors: Lee, Park, Kim, and Chen (2022). The first in-text citation should be:

errorId: err-intext-et-al-misuse | Bloom: apply | Scaffold: L2

Achievement: Knowledge and Understanding | Action: apply | OSSD: Demonstrate the use of appropriate communication techniques related to business

Official: A2 | Node: BOH4M-A-OE02 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/business1112currb.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-1

Misconception: Students think all authors must be listed in first citation or misuse comma placement.

Remediation → err-intext-et-al-misuse | Retest → err-intext-et-al-misuse

  1. ✓ A. (Lee et al., 2022)
    Correct. For three or more authors, use the first author's name followed by 'et al.' from the first citation.
  2. B. (Lee, Park, Kim, & Chen, 2022)
    That format is for two authors only. For three or more, use 'et al.' even in the first citation.
  3. C. (Lee et al. 2022)
    Missing the comma before the year. APA requires a comma after 'et al.' and the year.
  4. D. (Lee, et al., 2022)
    No comma after the author's name before 'et al.' in APA style.

  1. ✓ A. (Organization, n.d.)
    Correct. When no date, use 'n.d.' for 'no date'.
  2. B. (Organization, unknown)

    There is no date, so you cannot assume a year.

补救模块

err-intext-parenthetical-vs-narrative

Student thinks the author name can be part of the sentence in a parenthetical citation, or that the year can be placed elsewhere.

Explain: In-text citations come in two forms: parenthetical and narrative. Parenthetical citations place both the author's last name and year in parentheses at the end of the sentence, before the period. Narrative citations integrate the author's name into the sentence itself, with the year in parentheses immediately after the author's name.

Example: Correct parenthetical: 'Management is key (Smith, 2020).' Correct narrative: 'Smith (2020) argues that management is key.'

err-intext-et-al-misuse

Students may think 'et al.' is used for any number of authors, or that all authors must be listed in the first citation.

Explain: In APA style, for sources with three or more authors, use only the first author's name followed by 'et al.' in every in-text citation. Do not use 'et al.' for two authors; instead, list both authors each time.

Example: Correct: (Lee et al., 2022) for a source by Lee, Park, Kim, and Chen. Incorrect: (Lee, Park, Kim, & Chen, 2022) for first citation – use 'et al.' from the start.

err-intext-nodate-forgotten

Students often omit the year or use an invented year when a source has no publication date, instead of using the APA convention 'n.d.' (no date).

Explain: In APA style, when a source does not have a publication date, you use 'n.d.' (which stands for 'no date') in place of the year in both parenthetical and narrative citations. This signals to the reader that the source lacks a publication year.

Example: For a report by the Ontario Ministry of Education with no date: Parenthetical: (Ontario Ministry of Education, n.d.) Narrative: Ontario Ministry of Education (n.d.) states...

err-quotation-length-format

Students often treat all quotations the same: they use quotation marks even for long quotes, or they don't realize the 40-word threshold triggers a different format (block quote, no quotation marks, indented).

Explain: In APA style, quotations are formatted differently based on length. Short quotations (fewer than 40 words) are enclosed in double quotation marks and included within the text. Long quotations (40 words or more) are set apart as block quotes: they start on a new line, are indented from the left margin, and do not have quotation marks. The in-text citation for a block quote goes after the final punctuation.

Example: Short quote (<40 words): According to Smith (2020), "Effective management requires clear communication" (p. 45). Long quote (≥40 words): Smith (2020) found that: Management is a complex process that involves planning, organizing, leading, and controlling. Effective managers must adapt their style to the situation and the people they work with. Communication is the key to successful management. (p. 46)

err-quotation-sic-omission

Students think they can correct errors in a quotation or omit them without marking, rather than using '[sic]' to indicate the error is in the original.

Explain: When you quote a source directly, you must reproduce it exactly as written, including any errors. To show that the error is in the original and not your own mistake, place the Latin term '[sic]' (meaning 'thus') immediately after the error in square brackets.

Example: Original: 'The managment team held a meeting.' → 'The managment [sic] team held a meeting.'

err-paraphrase-no-citation

Many students believe that because paraphrasing uses their own words, they do not need to cite the source. However, the ideas still belong to the original author and must be credited.

Explain: Paraphrasing means restating a passage in your own words while keeping the original meaning. Even though you change the wording, you still need to cite the source using an in-text citation (author, year).

Example: Original from Roose & Kang (2018): 'Technology executives faced tough questions during the hearings.' Paraphrase: During the hearings, technology executives were asked challenging questions (Roose & Kang, 2018).

err-paraphrase-too-close

Students think that changing only a few words or reordering sentences is sufficient for paraphrasing, but they must restructure entirely and use synonyms without looking at the original.

Explain: Paraphrasing means restating a passage in your own words without changing the meaning. Simply swapping a few words or altering sentence order while keeping the same structure is called 'patchwriting' and is too close to the original, risking plagiarism. You must fully rewrite the idea using your own language and sentence construction. Steps to paraphrase: read, note key concepts, write without looking, compare, cite. Tips: start at a different point, use synonyms, change sentence structure, break or combine sentences.

Example: Original: 'Effective managers communicate clearly with their teams to ensure goals are understood.'<br>Too-close: 'Effective managers talk clearly to their teams to make sure goals are understood.'<br>Good paraphrase: 'Managers who share clear messages help their teams grasp the objectives.'
