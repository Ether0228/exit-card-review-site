    补救模块

    err-pandora-box-vs-jar

    Students think Pandora opened a box because of the common phrase 'Pandora's box', but the original myth describes a jar (pithos). This is a later mistranslation.

    Explain: In the original Greek myth, Pandora opened a jar called a pithos, not a box. The phrase 'Pandora's box' is a later mistranslation by Erasmus in the 16th century who confused the Greek word for jar (pithos) with box (pyxis). The jar released all evils; only hope remained.

    Example: When we say 'Pandora's box,' we refer to the myth, but the actual object was a jar. For instance, if you see a picture of Pandora opening a small box, that's incorrect; it should be a large jar.

    err-hope-as-blessing-only

    Hope in the myth is only a positive thing; I don't see any negative interpretation.

    Explain: In the Pandora myth, hope is left inside the jar after all evils escape. While hope is often seen as a comfort that helps humans endure suffering, some scholars argue that hope can also be a form of torment—it keeps people striving for something that may never come. This ambiguity means hope is not simply a blessing; it can be both a comfort and a curse. Recognizing this complexity is important for understanding the theme 'never lose hope' as a nuanced idea.

    Example: Consider a student waiting for a college acceptance letter. Hope can be comforting because it keeps them going, but it can also be agonizing if the wait is long and the outcome uncertain. In the myth, hope remains trapped, which might mean it is incomplete or false hope that prolongs suffering.

    err-prometheus-epimetheus-swap

    Students often confuse Prometheus and Epimetheus, thinking Prometheus accepted Pandora. In reality, Epimetheus (whose name means 'hindsight') ignored his brother Prometheus's warning and accepted Pandora as his wife.

    Explain: In the myth of Pandora, Prometheus means 'foresight' and Epimetheus means 'hindsight'. Prometheus warned his brother not to accept any gifts from Zeus, because he suspected trickery. However, Epimetheus ignored the warning and accepted Pandora, who was created as a punishment. This mistake led to the release of evils into the world. The key difference is: Prometheus thinks ahead and warns; Epimetheus acts without thinking and regrets later.

    Example: Think of Prometheus as the cautious friend who says 'Be careful, this might be a trap!' and Epimetheus as the impulsive friend who says 'It looks nice, I'll take it!' and then suffers the consequences.

    err-vocab-intricate-negative

    The student mistakenly believes that 'intricate' has a negative connotation, when in fact it is a neutral word meaning complicated and detailed.

    Explain: The word 'intricate' describes something very complicated or detailed. It does not carry a positive or negative feeling by itself. For example, a complicated watch mechanism is intricate, but that doesn't make it good or bad—it just means it has many complex parts. Some learners confuse intricate with words like 'irritating' or 'tricky' which can have negative meanings, but intricate is neutral.

    Example: In the sentence 'The design on the jar was intricate,' it means the design had many fine details, not that it was ugly or bad.
