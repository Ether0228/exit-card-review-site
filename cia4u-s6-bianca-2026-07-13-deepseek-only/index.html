    补救模块

    err-economic-growth-always-good

    Economic growth always improves well-being and has no negative consequences.

    Explain: Economic growth has both benefits and costs. Benefits include higher per capita income, reduced poverty, increased employment, and more public funding for healthcare and infrastructure. However, unregulated growth can lead to environmental pollution, traffic congestion, depletion of non-renewable resources, widening income gaps, and inflation. Recognizing this tradeoff is essential for balanced policy-making.

    Example: Consider a new factory: it creates jobs (benefit) but also increases pollution and traffic (costs).

    err-unemployment-single-type

    Believes unemployment is a single phenomenon with a single cause, rather than recognizing six distinct types with different causes and solutions.

    Explain: Unemployment is not one problem; there are six different types: voluntary, seasonal, structural, frictional, cyclical, and technological. Each has a specific cause and calls for a different policy response.

    Example: Frictional unemployment occurs when workers are between jobs, while structural unemployment arises from a mismatch between workers' skills and available jobs.

    err-unemployment-measurement-confusion

    The student confuses the claimant count with the labor force survey, possibly thinking both methods rely on the same data source or that the claimant count includes all unemployed people.

    Explain: Unemployment is measured in two main ways. The claimant count simply counts the number of people receiving unemployment benefits. The labor force survey estimates unemployment by interviewing a sample of households and asking about job-seeking activity. The claimant count is a narrow measure (only those who claim benefits), while the labor force survey is broader (includes people who are jobless but not claiming benefits). Both are used to track unemployment trends.

    Example: In Canada, the claimant count might show 1.2 million people receiving EI benefits, while the labor force survey might estimate 1.5 million unemployed. The difference is because not all unemployed people are eligible for or claim benefits.
