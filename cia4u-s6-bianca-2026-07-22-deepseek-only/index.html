Q1. Which statement best describes Canada's economic system?

Official: B1 | Node: CIA4U-B-OE01 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/2015cws11and12.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-1

Misconception: Students may think Canada is a pure market economy or that 'mixed' means exactly halfway between command and market.

Remediation → err-CIA4U-B1-EconSystems | Retest → err-CIA4U-B1-EconSystems

  1. A. A pure market economy with minimal government intervention
    Canada has significant government intervention (e.g., crown corporations, public healthcare), so it is not pure market.
  2. B. A command economy where the government makes all production decisions
    Canada's economy relies heavily on private enterprise and market forces, not central planning.
  3. ✓ C. A mixed economy that leans closer to the market end of the spectrum
    Correct. Canada combines private markets with government involvement, but overall it is market-oriented.
  4. D. A mixed economy that leans closer to the command end
    While Canada has some command features, its economy is predominantly market-driven (e.g., most goods priced by supply/demand).

Q2. The three fundamental economic questions are: what to produce, how to produce, and __________.

Learning Goal: lg-1

Misconception: Students may confuse 'for whom to produce' with 'how to distribute' or omit it entirely.

Remediation → err-CIA4U-B1-EconSystems | Retest → err-CIA4U-B1-EconSystems

  1. A. why to produce
    The third question is about distribution, not purpose.
  2. B. when to produce
    Timing is not one of the three fundamental questions.
  3. ✓ C. for whom to produce
    Correct. The three questions are what, how, and for whom to produce.
  4. D. where to produce
    Location is not one of the fundamental questions.

Q3. Gross Domestic Product (GDP) is best defined as:

errorId: err-CIA4U-D1-Indicators | Bloom: remember | Scaffold: L1

Achievement: Knowledge and Understanding | Action: recall | OSSD: Explain how economists measure and represent standards of living and distributio

Official: D1 | Node: CIA4U-D-OE01 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/2015cws11and12.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-2

Misconception: Students may confuse GDP with total national wealth or income of all citizens.

Remediation → err-CIA4U-D1-Indicators | Retest → err-CIA4U-D1-Indicators

  1. ✓ A. The total value of all goods and services produced by a country in one year
    Correct. GDP measures the market value of all final goods and services produced within a country's borders.
  2. B. The total income earned by all residents of a country
    That is closer to Gross National Income (GNI). GDP measures production, not income.
  3. C. The total value of all exports minus imports
    That is net exports, a component of GDP, not GDP itself.
  4. D. The total wealth accumulated by a country over its history
    GDP is a flow measure for a specific year, not a stock of wealth.

    补救模块

    err-CIA4U-B1-EconSystems

    Thinks Canada is a pure market economy or that the three questions include 'why' or 'when' instead of 'for whom'.

    Explain: Economic systems range from command (government decides) to market (consumers decide). Canada is a mixed economy, closer to the market end because most production decisions are made by private firms responding to consumer demand, but the government also provides essential services through crown corporations. The three fundamental economic questions are: What to produce? How to produce? For whom to produce? Every economy must answer these, but the answers differ based on the system.

    Example: In Canada, 'what to produce' is largely driven by consumer preferences (market), but the government decides on public goods like healthcare. 'How to produce' is chosen by firms to minimize costs, subject to regulations. 'For whom to produce' depends on income, though programs like public education redistribute. In contrast, China's command economy has the state answer all three questions.

    err-CIA4U-D1-Indicators

    Student confuses GDP with other economic measures (e.g., wealth or exports) or misunderstands that foreign exchange rate indicates relative currency value.

    Explain: Gross Domestic Product (GDP) is the total value of all goods and services produced by a country in one year. Foreign exchange rate is the relative value of one country's currency against another.

    Example: For instance, Canada's GDP includes everything from lumber production to haircuts. If the exchange rate is 1.25 CAD/USD, it means 1 US dollar buys 1.25 Canadian dollars.
