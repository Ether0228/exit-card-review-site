Q1. Why did Icarus fall into the sea and drown?

errorId: err-myth-plot-icarus-low | Bloom: understand | Scaffold: L1

Achievement: Knowledge and Understanding | Action: interpret | OSSD: Read and demonstrate understanding of a variety of texts for different purposes.

Official: B1 | Node: ESLDO-B-OE01 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/esl912currb.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-1

Misconception: Student thinks Icarus flew too low or that the wings broke for another reason.

Remediation → err-myth-plot-icarus-low | Retest → err-myth-plot-icarus-low

  1. A. He flew too low and the seawater weighed down his wings.
    Actually, Daedalus warned Icarus not to fly too low, but the fall was because he flew too high.
  2. ✓ B. He flew too high and the sun melted the wax on his wings.
    Correct! Icarus ignored his father's warning, flew too close to the sun, and the wax melted.
  3. C. His wings were poorly made and broke apart.
    Daedalus made strong wings. They broke only because the wax melted from the sun's heat.
  4. D. He was pushed by a gust of wind.
    No, the story says he fell because the wax melted, not because of wind.

Q2. Why was Daedalus banished from Athens?

Learning Goal: lg-2

Misconception: Student confuses banishment (Athens) with imprisonment in Crete.

Remediation → err-myth-cause-banishment | Retest → err-myth-cause-banishment

  1. A. He built the Labyrinth for King Minos.
    Building the Labyrinth happened in Crete, after his banishment.
  2. ✓ B. He tried to kill his nephew Perdix out of jealousy.
    Correct! Daedalus was jealous of Perdix's talent and pushed him off a cliff, so he was banished.
  3. C. He helped Queen Pasiphae hide the Minotaur.
    That happened later in Crete, not the reason for his banishment from Athens.
  4. D. He stole a precious artifact from the Athenian temple.
    No, the myth does not mention this. His crime was against Perdix.

    补救模块

    err-myth-plot-icarus-low

    The student believes Icarus fell because he flew too low and the seawater weighed down his wings. However, in the myth, Icarus flew too high, causing the sun to melt the wax on his wings, leading to his fall.

    Explain: Icarus fell because he ignored his father's warning and flew too high. The heat from the sun melted the wax holding his wings together. He did not fly too low; in fact, Daedalus warned him not to fly too low either, but the fatal mistake was flying too high.

    Example: In the story, Daedalus builds wings from feathers and wax. He tells Icarus to fly at a medium height. Icarus becomes overconfident and flies higher. The wax melts, his wings fall apart, and he plummets into the sea.

    err-myth-cause-banishment

    Confusing the reason for Daedalus's banishment from Athens (jealousy and attempted murder of Perdix) with the reason for his imprisonment in Crete (hiding Pasiphae's secret).

    Explain: Daedalus was banished from Athens because he tried to kill his nephew Perdix out of jealousy. Later, in Crete, he was imprisoned by King Minos for helping Queen Pasiphae hide the Minotaur's secret. These are two separate events: banishment from Athens, imprisonment in Crete.

    Example: Think of it like being expelled from one school for fighting (banishment) and then being grounded at a new school for breaking a rule (imprisonment). The reasons are different.

    err-myth-character-role

    Student confuses the roles of characters in the Daedalus and Icarus myth, such as thinking Daedalus is a king or Icarus is the inventor.

    Explain: In the myth of Daedalus and Icarus, each character has a specific role. Daedalus is an inventor and craftsman, not a king. He is the father of Icarus and uncle of Perdix. Icarus is Daedalus's son, not an inventor. King Minos is the ruler of Crete, not a craftsman. Understanding these roles helps you follow the story.

    Example: Daedalus built the Labyrinth for King Minos. He also made wings for himself and Icarus to escape. Icarus flew too close to the sun and fell. King Minos did not build anything; he gave orders.

    err-myth-theme-single

    The student believes a myth can only have one correct theme, but the teacher explained that multiple valid themes can be supported by text evidence.

    Explain: A theme is a central message or lesson in a story. Myths often contain multiple themes because they can be interpreted in different ways. For example, the myth of Daedalus and Icarus teaches about heeding warnings, avoiding overconfidence, and respecting experience. All of these are valid as long as you can support them with evidence from the text.

    Example: One student might say the theme is 'listen to your elders' because Daedalus warned Icarus. Another student might say the theme is 'pride comes before a fall' because Icarus's overconfidence led to his death. Both are correct if they use evidence.

    err-vocab-labyrinth-meaning

    A labyrinth is the same as a simple maze that you can find your way out of.

    Explain: A labyrinth is different from a simple maze. In Greek mythology, the Labyrinth built by Daedalus had only one entrance and no exit. It was a confusing, winding structure designed to trap the Minotaur forever. People could easily get lost inside and never find their way out. Modern mazes often have multiple paths and exits, but the original Labyrinth was a single path that twisted and turned, leading to the center with no escape.

    Example: Think of the Labyrinth in the story: King Minos used it to imprison the Minotaur. No one who entered could escape except Theseus, who used a ball of thread to retrace his steps. This shows its unique design.
