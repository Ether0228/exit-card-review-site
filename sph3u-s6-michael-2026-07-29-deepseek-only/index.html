Q1. A box is sliding to the right along a rough floor. What is the direction of the kinetic friction force acting on the box?

errorId: err-friction-direction | Bloom: understand | Scaffold: L1

Achievement: Knowledge and Understanding | Action: interpret | OSSD: Demonstrate an understanding of the relationship between changes in velocity and

Official: C3 | Node: SPH3U-C-OE03 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/2009science11_12.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-1

Misconception: Students may think friction always opposes acceleration or the applied force direction.

Remediation → err-friction-direction | Retest → err-friction-direction

  1. A. To the right, in the same direction as motion
    Friction opposes motion, so it cannot be in the same direction as the box's velocity.
  2. ✓ B. To the left, opposite to the direction of motion
    Correct! Kinetic friction always acts opposite to the relative motion of the surfaces.
  3. C. Upward, because friction is normal to the surface
    Friction acts parallel to the surface, not perpendicular. Normal force is perpendicular.
  4. D. It depends on the acceleration of the box
    Direction of kinetic friction does not depend on acceleration; it depends on relative motion.

Q2. A person carries a suitcase horizontally at constant speed across a flat floor. Which statement describes the work done by the person on the suitcase?

errorId: err-work-sign | Bloom: understand | Scaffold: L1

Achievement: Knowledge and Understanding | Action: interpret | OSSD: Demonstrate an understanding of work, efficiency, power, gravitational potential

Official: D3 | Node: SPH3U-D-OE03 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/2009science11_12.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-2

Misconception: Students often think if force is applied, work must be done, ignoring the angle.

Remediation → err-work-sign | Retest → err-work-sign

  1. A. Positive work, because the person applies force to keep it moving
    The force is vertical to support the weight, but displacement is horizontal. Since the angle is 90°, cos(90°)=0, so no work is done.
  2. B. Negative work, because the suitcase resists motion
    Negative work occurs when force opposes displacement (angle 180°). Here force is perpendicular, so work is zero.
  3. ✓ C. Zero work, because the force is perpendicular to displacement
    Correct! Since the force is vertical and displacement horizontal, the angle is 90° and work is zero.
  4. D. Cannot determine without knowing the mass and speed
    Work depends on force, displacement, and angle, not mass or speed directly. The angle alone determines zero work here.

Q3. A student pushes a box with a constant force of 50 N at an angle of 30° above the horizontal. The box moves 8.0 m horizontally. How much work does the student do?

errorId: err-work-formula | Bloom: apply | Scaffold: L2

Achievement: Application | Action: apply | OSSD: Demonstrate an understanding of work, efficiency, power, gravitational potential

Official: D3 | Node: SPH3U-D-OE03 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/2009science11_12.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-2

Misconception: Students may forget to use cosine or use the angle from horizontal incorrectly (e.g., use sin or omit cos).

Remediation → err-work-formula | Retest → err-work-formula

  1. A. 400 J
    You likely used W = F·d without cosine. The force is not parallel to displacement, so you must multiply by cos(30°).
  2. ✓ B. 346 J
    Correct! W = 50 N × 8.0 m × cos(30°) = 400 × 0.866 ≈ 346 J.
  3. C. 200 J
    You might have used sin(30°) instead of cos or halved incorrectly. Remember to use cosine of the angle between force and displacement.
  4. D. 0 J
    Work is not zero because the horizontal component of force contributes to displacement. Only perpendicular components yield zero work.

    补救模块

    err-friction-direction

    Students may think friction always acts opposite to acceleration rather than opposite to velocity, or apply kinetic friction direction incorrectly for static cases.

    Explain: Kinetic friction always opposes the relative motion between two surfaces in contact. This means it acts opposite to the direction in which an object is sliding, not necessarily opposite to its acceleration. For example, if a box is sliding to the right, kinetic friction acts to the left. This is true even if the box is slowing down (acceleration left) or speeding up (acceleration right) – friction always points opposite to velocity.

    Example: Imagine you push a box to the right. As it slides, your hand applies a force to the right, but the floor exerts kinetic friction to the left. If you stop pushing, the box continues sliding right while friction still pulls left until it stops.

    err-work-sign

    Students may confuse the conditions for positive, negative, and zero work, e.g., thinking work is always positive if force is applied, or not recognizing that the angle between force and displacement determines the sign.

    Explain: Work is defined as W = F·cosθ·d, where θ is the angle between the force and displacement vectors. The sign of work depends on θ: positive when 0° ≤ θ < 90° (force aids motion), zero when θ = 90° (force perpendicular), negative when 180° (force opposes motion). Key examples: lifting a box (positive work), friction slowing a box (negative work), carrying a suitcase horizontally (zero work because force is vertical, displacement horizontal).

    Example: Consider a curling stone pushed with a 50 N force over 8 m on frictionless ice. The force is in the direction of displacement, so θ = 0° and work is positive: W = 50 × 8 × cos(0°) = 400 J. If friction acts opposite (180°), work is negative: e.g., W_friction = -F_friction × d. For zero work, imagine pushing against a wall with no displacement: d = 0, so W = 0 regardless of force.

    err-work-formula

    Student may think work is simply force times distance regardless of direction, or may confuse which angle to use in the cosine factor.

    Explain: The work formula is W = F·cosθ·d, where θ is the angle between the force vector and the displacement vector. The cosine factor extracts the component of force that is parallel to the displacement. Only this component does work. A common mistake is to use the angle from horizontal incorrectly or to use sin instead of cos. Always identify the two vectors and measure the angle between them.

    Example: If you push a box with a force of 50 N at an angle of 30° above the horizontal and the box moves 8.0 m horizontally, the work you do is calculated using the component of force in the direction of motion: F_parallel = 50 N × cos(30°) = 43.3 N. Then work = 43.3 N × 8.0 m = 346 J. If you had simply used F = 50 N, you would get 400 J, which is incorrect.
