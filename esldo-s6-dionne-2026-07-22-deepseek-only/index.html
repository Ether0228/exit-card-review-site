    补救模块

    err-prometheus-epimetheus-confusion

    Students may confuse Prometheus and Epimetheus, mixing their roles and gifts.

    Explain: Prometheus is a Titan with foresight who created humans from clay. Epimetheus is his brother, an afterthinker who distributed all survival gifts to animals first, leaving humans defenseless.

    Example: Prometheus thought ahead to help humans by stealing fire, while Epimetheus acted without thinking, giving away all resources and later accepting Pandora, which released troubles.

    err-pandora-purpose

    You thought Pandora was created for a positive purpose, like being a companion or reward, but she was actually a deliberate trap designed by Zeus to punish humans.

    Explain: Zeus created Pandora as a punishment after Prometheus stole fire for humans. She was the first mortal woman, crafted to be beautiful and irresistible, but she carried a jar (often called a box) containing all the troubles of the world. Zeus intended her as a trap for Epimetheus, who would accept her impulsively and unleash suffering upon humanity.

    Example: Just as a hacker might send a tempting email with a virus attached, Zeus sent Pandora as a 'gift' that actually brought harm. Epimetheus, ignoring Prometheus's warning, accepted her, and when she opened the jar, disease, sorrow, and other evils escaped into the world.

    err-zeus-fire-ban-reason

    Students think Zeus banned fire because it was dangerous for humans or out of general cruelty, rather than as a deliberate act to maintain his power and punish Prometheus for his defiance.

    Explain: Zeus banned fire for humans not because he was cruel or because fire was dangerous, but because he wanted to keep humans weak and dependent on the gods. Fire represented knowledge, technology, and civilization. By denying fire, Zeus ensured humans could not advance. Additionally, the ban was a direct punishment for Prometheus, who had created humans and stole fire from Olympus to help them. Zeus's action was about control and retaliation.

    Example: Consider a ruler who forbids citizens from learning to read. The ruler does not hate reading itself; rather, literacy would empower citizens to question authority. Similarly, Zeus banned fire to prevent humans from gaining power and to punish Prometheus for his rebellion.

    err-hercules-rescue-timing

    Students may think Prometheus was freed by Hercules immediately after the punishment, missing the role of the prophecy and his mother's disclosure.

    Explain: Prometheus's rescue did not happen right after his punishment. For many years, he was chained to a mountain while an eagle ate his liver daily. Hercules freed him much later, but only after Prometheus's mother revealed a secret prophecy about Zeus losing power. This prophecy was valuable to Zeus, so he allowed Hercules to rescue Prometheus in exchange for the information.

    Example: Imagine a prisoner who only gets released after his family gives the captor a secret code. That's similar to how Prometheus's mother shared a prophecy with Hercules that helped Zeus, leading to Prometheus's freedom.

    err-theme-identification

    Students may identify a superficial theme rather than the deeper contrast between foresight and afterthought or the consequence of rebellion.

    Explain: A theme is the central message or lesson of a story. In the Prometheus myth, a core theme is the contrast between foresight (planning ahead) and afterthought (acting without thinking). Prometheus shows foresight by helping humans and accepting punishment, while Epimetheus acts impulsively, leading to disasters. Another theme is the consequences of rebellion against authority: Prometheus defies Zeus to help humanity but suffers greatly.

    Example: For instance, when Epimetheus accepts Pandora without questioning, his lack of foresight releases troubles into the world. Meanwhile, Prometheus's foresight leads him to steal fire despite knowing the severe punishment.
