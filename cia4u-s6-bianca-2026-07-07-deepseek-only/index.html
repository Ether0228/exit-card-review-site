Q1. Scarcity in economics refers to:

errorId: err-scarcity-needs-vs-wants | Bloom: understand | Scaffold: L1

Achievement: Knowledge and Understanding | Action: interpret | OSSD: Demonstrate an understanding of the significance of the concept of scarcity and

Official: B1 | Node: CIA4U-B-OE01 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/2015cws11and12.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-1

Misconception: Students may think scarcity means shortage or poverty rather than the economic condition of unlimited wants vs limited resources.

Remediation → err-scarcity-needs-vs-wants | Retest → err-scarcity-needs-vs-wants

  1. A. A shortage of goods due to high prices
    A shortage is a temporary mismatch of demand and supply, not the fundamental condition of scarcity.
  2. ✓ B. Limited resources relative to unlimited human wants
    Correct. Scarcity is the core economic problem: our wants exceed what limited resources can produce.
  3. C. Poverty and lack of money
    Poverty is about low income, not scarcity. Scarcity applies to everyone, even the wealthy.
  4. D. When demand exceeds supply temporarily
    That describes a market disequilibrium, not the enduring concept of scarcity.

Q2. Xiao Qiang gives up a salary of $40,000 per year to open a shoe store. Which of the following is the opportunity cost of opening the store?

Official: B1 | Node: CIA4U-B-OE01 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/2015cws11and12.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-1

Misconception: Students may think only explicit monetary payments count as cost, ignoring the forgone alternative.

Remediation → err-opportunity-cost-money | Retest → err-opportunity-cost-money

  1. ✓ A. The $40,000 salary he gave up
    Correct. The opportunity cost is the next best alternative foregone — in this case, the salary.
  2. B. The rent for the store
    Rent is an explicit monetary cost, not the opportunity cost. Opportunity cost is the value of the next best alternative.
  3. C. The profit from the shoe store
    Profit is a return, not a cost. Opportunity cost is what you give up, not what you gain.
  4. D. All business expenses
    Business expenses are explicit costs, but opportunity cost specifically refers to the value of the next best alternative.

Learning Goal: lg-2

Misconception: Students may think a point inside the PPC is efficient or unattainable.

Remediation → err-ppc-points-misplaced | Retest → err-ppc-points-misplaced

  1. A. Efficient use of all resources
    Efficient points lie on the curve, not inside. Inside means some resources are idle.
  2. B. Unattainable production with current resources
    Unattainable points lie outside the curve. Inside is attainable but inefficient.
  3. ✓ C. Inefficient use of resources (underutilization)
    Correct. Points inside the PPC indicate the economy is not using all its resources fully.
  4. D. The maximum possible output
    Maximum output is achieved on the curve, not inside.

Remediation → err-ppc-shift-vs-rotation | Retest → err-ppc-shift-vs-rotation

  1. A. Shift outward for both goods equally
    A parallel shift requires an overall increase in resources or productivity in both sectors. Here, only wheat productivity improves.
  2. ✓ B. Rotate outward along the wheat axis
    Correct. The maximum wheat output increases while corn max stays the same, so the curve rotates on the corn axis.
  3. C. Rotate outward along the corn axis
    Rotation along the corn axis would mean corn productivity increased, not wheat.
  4. D. Not change
    Technology change expands production possibilities, so the PPC must shift outward in some way.

    补救模块

    err-opportunity-cost-money

    Student thinks opportunity cost is only monetary cost, rather than the value of the next best alternative foregone.

    Explain: Opportunity cost is the value of the next best alternative you give up when making a choice. It is not always money; it can be time, leisure, or any benefit you forgo. Every choice involves a trade-off because resources are scarce.

    Example: Xiao Qiang gave up a $40,000 salary to open a shoe store. The opportunity cost of opening the store is the $40,000 salary he could have earned, not the rent or other expenses. The rent is an explicit cost, but the opportunity cost is the forgone alternative.

    err-ppc-points-misplaced

    Students may confuse points inside, on, and outside the PPC, e.g., thinking a point inside is unattainable or a point outside is efficient.

    Explain: The Production Possibility Curve (PPC) shows maximum combinations of two goods that can be produced with fixed resources and technology. Points on the curve are efficient (full resource use). Points inside the curve are inefficient (resources underutilized). Points outside the curve are unattainable with current resources and technology.

    Example: On a PPC for corn and wheat: point A on the curve means you produce efficiently; point B inside means you produce less than possible (e.g., some workers idle); point C outside is impossible right now (e.g., you'd need more land or better seeds).

    err-ppc-shift-vs-rotation

    Students may not distinguish between a parallel shift (overall resource increase) and a rotation (productivity improvement in one good).

    Explain: A Production Possibility Curve (PPC) can change in two ways: a shift or a rotation. A parallel shift outward occurs when the economy gains more resources (e.g., more land, labor, capital) or overall technology improves for both goods. A rotation occurs when productivity improves for only one good, expanding that good's maximum output while the other good's maximum stays the same. For example, if wheat productivity doubles due to new technology, the PPC rotates outward along the wheat axis; corn maximum remains unchanged. This illustrates that the PPC is not static and changes differently based on the source of growth.

    Example: Using the wheat and corn example, if a new farming technique doubles wheat output per acre but doesn't affect corn, the PPC rotates outward on the wheat axis. The new point shows more wheat can be produced at any given corn level, but the maximum corn is unchanged.

    err-scarcity-needs-vs-wants

    Students often think scarcity means shortage, poverty, or temporary high prices, rather than the fundamental economic condition of unlimited wants vs limited resources.

    Explain: Scarcity is the basic economic problem: our wants (for goods, services, time, etc.) are unlimited, but the resources (land, labor, capital, entrepreneurship) to fulfill them are limited. This forces us to make choices. Scarcity is not the same as poverty—even wealthy people face scarcity because they cannot have everything they want, and time is scarce for everyone. It also applies to non-physical things like time and services.

    Example: Consider Xiao Qiang, who must decide between opening a shoe store or keeping his job. He has limited time and money. Even if he were rich, he would still face scarcity of time—he cannot work two full-time jobs. Similarly, a student has only 24 hours a day to study, sleep, and socialize. That is scarcity.
