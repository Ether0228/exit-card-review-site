  1. A. Democratic leadership, because it values everyone's input.
    Democratic leadership requires discussion time, which the team doesn't have under a tight deadline, and inexperienced members may not contribute effectively.
  2. ✓ B. Autocratic leadership, because quick decisions and clear guidance are needed.
    Correct! Autocratic leadership is effective when time is short and the team lacks experience.
  3. C. Laissez-faire leadership, because the team can learn on their own.

    Autocratic leadership is specifically designed for such situations; it provides structure and speed.

    补救模块

    err-autocratic-always-ineffective

    Student believes autocratic leadership is always ineffective, ignoring its necessity under time constraints or with inexperienced teams.

    Explain: Autocratic leadership is not always ineffective. It works best when time is limited, team members lack experience, or the team is unfamiliar with each other. In such situations, the leader makes quick decisions and provides clear guidance, which is more efficient than seeking input from inexperienced or unacquainted team members. The key is to match the style to the situation.

    Example: In the North Star launch decision case study (a Canadian enterprise in Markham, Ontario), the leader needed to make a fast decision to meet a tight deadline with a new, inexperienced team. Autocratic leadership was effective because it provided immediate direction and avoided time-consuming group discussions.

    err-laissez-faire-no-leadership

    You think laissez-faire means the leader does nothing, so it is not real leadership. But laissez-faire is a deliberate style that works when the team is highly skilled and self-motivated.

    Explain: Laissez-faire is a hands-off leadership style where the leader gives minimal instructions and only steps in when the team asks for help. It is NOT the absence of leadership – the leader still sets goals, provides resources, and remains accountable. This style works best with a team that is highly motivated, expert, and familiar with working together. For example, a senior engineering team that has collaborated on many projects thrives with laissez-faire because they need autonomy, not constant oversight. The leader's role is to support, not direct. When overused or used with unprepared teams, it causes role ambiguity and stress.

    Example: Scenario: A team of experienced software developers who have worked together for years. Their manager rarely checks in and only provides guidance when the team asks. The team feels empowered and produces innovative solutions. This is laissez-faire leadership in action – it works because the team is highly skilled and self-driven.

    err-democratic-always-effective

    Democratic leadership is often seen as fair and inclusive, so students assume it works in every situation. However, it requires sufficient discussion time, clear common goals, and willing participants. When the team is unmotivated, lacks expertise, or is in high conflict, democratic leadership can lead to slow decisions, indecision, or poor outcomes. Recognizing these limitations is key to choosing the right style.

    Explain: Democratic leadership prioritizes equality and collaboration: the leader collects full team input before making final decisions. This style is effective only when there is enough time for discussion, the team shares clear common goals, and members are willing to contribute. It fails when the team is unmotivated (members won't engage), lacks competence (can't provide useful input), or faces high conflict (disagreements stall decisions). Pros include higher acceptance of decisions and preparing members for promotion. Cons are long decision cycles and the need for coordination.

    Example: Imagine a project team that is deeply divided and unfocused. Using democratic leadership, the leader calls a meeting to gather everyone's opinion. Instead of constructive discussion, members argue, and no consensus emerges. The decision is delayed, and the project falls behind. An autocratic leader would have made a swift decision to keep things moving.

    err-confuse-autocratic-democratic

    Student confuses the roles of the leader in autocratic and democratic leadership, for example, thinking that an autocratic leader still collects team input or that a democratic leader decides alone.

    Explain: Autocratic and democratic leadership differ in how decisions are made. In autocratic leadership, the leader holds full decision-making authority, issues direct orders, and rejects team input. In democratic leadership, the leader prioritizes equality and collaboration, collects full team input before making final decisions, and values every member's voice equally. The key difference: autocratic = leader decides alone; democratic = leader gathers input then decides.

    Example: Consider a manager facing a tight deadline: an autocratic leader would quickly assign tasks without discussion, while a democratic leader would call a meeting to gather ideas first—even if time is short.
