补救模块

err-graphic-text-pure-text-counts

The student believes that any text document, even one without any visual elements, can be considered a graphic text. This is incorrect because a graphic text must combine both visual imagery and textual content.

Explain: A graphic text is a type of text that combines visual imagery (like pictures, diagrams, maps) with written text. Pure text without any visuals does not qualify. The visual and textual elements work together to convey information more effectively. Examples include maps, infographics, flowcharts, and diagrams. Remember: if there is no image, it's not a graphic text.

Example: A floor plan of a building with labeled rooms is a graphic text because it uses visual layout (the floor plan) and text (labels). In contrast, a 500-word magazine article with no images is just a plain text, not a graphic text.

err-flowchart-decision-is-process

Student confuses the decision diamond symbol with the process rectangle.

Explain: In a flowchart, different symbols represent different types of steps. A rectangle (process) represents an action or operation, like 'calculate total' or 'print receipt'. A diamond (decision) represents a point where a yes/no question is asked, leading to different branches. For example, 'Is the payment valid?' would be a diamond, with 'Yes' and 'No' arrows leading to different rectangles.

Example: Flowchart: Start (rounded rectangle) → 'Enter password' (parallelogram) → 'Is password correct?' (diamond) → Yes: 'Access granted' (rectangle) → End (rounded rectangle); No: 'Show error' (rectangle) → End.

err-news-report-headline-too-long

Student believes there is no word limit for the headline or confuses the headline limit with the lead paragraph's 35-word limit.

Explain: According to the Unit 1 Assignment 1 requirements, the headline must be no more than 5 words. This is a strict limit to ensure headlines are concise and attention-grabbing. The lead paragraph has its own 35-word limit, which is separate.

Example: Correct headline (5 words): 'City Opens New Downtown Park'. Incorrect (7 words): 'City Opens New Downtown Park to Public'.

err-news-report-lead-too-long

The student thinks the lead paragraph can exceed 35 words, possibly confusing it with other limits or ignoring the requirement entirely.

Explain: In the Unit 1 Assignment 1 news report, the lead paragraph must be a maximum of 35 words. This is a strict requirement to keep the lead concise and engaging. The lead introduces the story and must capture the reader's attention quickly.

Example: Correct lead (34 words): 'Mayor Jane Smith announced a new community garden project at City Hall yesterday, aiming to transform vacant lots into green spaces for local residents.'

err-machine-translation-allowed

You think it's okay to use tools like Google Translate to write your assignments in English, as long as the meaning is clear.

Explain: In this course, all written work must be written by you in original English. Machine translation—using any tool to translate text from another language into English—is strictly prohibited. This rule ensures that the work reflects your own English skills, which is the purpose of the OLC4O course: to develop your literacy in English. Even if spelling or grammar have errors, that is fine; what matters is that the words are your own.

Example: If you write a paragraph in your first language and then use Google Translate to convert it to English, that is machine translation and is not allowed. Instead, write directly in English, even if it's simple or has mistakes. Your instructor wants to see your current English ability, not a machine's.
