    补救模块

    err-act3-plot-sequencing

    The student does not firmly track the cause-and-effect sequence of Act 3. They may remember that Romeo dies or that Juliet dies and assume those events happen in Act 3, or they may think Romeo kills Tybalt before Mercutio is killed.

    Explain: In Act 3, the events must happen in a certain order. First, in Act 3 Scene 1, Tybalt kills Mercutio. Then Romeo, in rage, kills Tybalt. As a result, the Prince banishes Romeo from Verona. After that, in Scenes 2-4, Juliet learns about Tybalt's death and Romeo's exile, and her father arranges her marriage to Paris. Finally, in Scene 5, Romeo and Juliet spend their last night together, then Juliet refuses to marry Paris and decides to seek help from Friar Lawrence. Events like Romeo's death or Juliet's death happen much later, so they are not part of Act 3.

    Example: If a student writes 'Romeo dies after being exiled in Act 3,' that is wrong because Romeo's death occurs in the final act. The Act 3 ending is Romeo leaving for exile, not dying.

    err-oxymoron-foreshadow-confusion

    The student knows the two terms but not their functions. They may think any strong or emotional quote is foreshadowing, or any phrase with contrast is an oxymoron, without checking whether it hints at a future event or pairs opposite words.

    Explain: Foreshadowing hints at something that will happen later in the story. An oxymoron puts two opposite words together to show a conflict, especially an inner emotional conflict. In Act 3, Juliet says 'If all else fail, myself have the power to die,' which foreshadows the tragic ending because she is hinting she may kill herself. Later in the same speech, she calls Romeo a 'beautiful tyrant' and a 'dove-feathered raven.' These are oxymorons because they combine opposite ideas to show she loves him and is angry at him at the same time. To tell them apart, ask: Does the line predict a future event? Then it is foreshadowing. Does it create a phrase from two opposite words? Then it is an oxymoron.

    Example: Foreshadowing: 'If all else fail, myself have the power to die' (hints at Juliet's death). Oxymoron: 'beautiful tyrant' (beautiful + tyrant are opposites).

    err-assignment-paragraph-structure

    A short paragraph is just a personal opinion; it does not need text evidence or a clear structure. The student may not know that Assignment 1 Part A requires a topic sentence, supporting details with text evidence, and a concluding sentence.

    Explain: For Assignment 1 Part A, you must write ONE paragraph analyzing whether a character's decision was good or bad. Use a clear structure: (1) Topic sentence that names the character and states your judgment. (2) Supporting details that give an event from Act 3 and explain how it supports your judgment. (3) A concluding sentence that restates your main point in new words. Every body sentence should connect to the character's decision. The assignment also asks for text evidence, so include a specific Act 3 event, such as Romeo kills Tybalt after Tybalt kills Mercutio, or Juliet refuses the Paris marriage and resolves to seek help from Friar Lawrence.

    Example: Topic sentence: In Act 3, Romeo's decision to kill Tybalt is bad because it leads to his exile. Supporting detail: After Tybalt kills Mercutio, Romeo kills Tybalt in rage, and the Prince then banishes Romeo from Verona. Explanation: This shows that Romeo's rash action separates him from Juliet and makes their situation worse. Concluding sentence: Therefore, Romeo's rage is a turning point that destroys his chance to stay in Verona with Juliet.
