Misconception: Confusing simile with metaphor, e.g., thinking all comparisons without 'like' are similes.

Remediation → err-simile-metaphor | Retest → err-simile-metaphor

  1. A. The school became a bridge to his future.
    This is a metaphor because it directly compares the school to a bridge without using 'like' or 'as'.
  2. ✓ B. The sunlight poured like warm honey.
    Correct! 'Like' makes this a simile.
  3. C. The classroom was a quiet forest.
    This is a metaphor. Similes use 'like' or 'as', which this does not.
  4. D. Her voice was music.
    This is a metaphor because it does not use 'like' or 'as'.

      补救模块

      err-simile-metaphor

      Student confuses simile and metaphor, thinking they are the same or using 'like' incorrectly.

      Explain: Similes and metaphors are both comparisons, but similes use 'like' or 'as', while metaphors directly state that one thing is another.

      Example: Simile: 'The sunlight pours like warm honey.' Metaphor: 'The school became a bridge to his future.'

      err-sensory-detail

      Thinking only sight matters or ignoring other senses

      Explain: A descriptive essay uses sensory details to paint a picture for the reader. Sensory details engage the five senses: sight, sound, smell, taste, and touch. Using only sight limits your description; including other senses makes it vivid and immersive.

      Example: Instead of 'The kitchen was messy,' try: 'Dirty dishes piled in the sink (sight), the hum of the refrigerator (sound), and the sharp smell of vinegar (smell) filled the air.'

      err-thesis

      Thinking the thesis is only the subject or topic, not the dominant impression or main claim.

      Explain: In a descriptive essay, the thesis statement does more than name the topic—it states the dominant impression, which is the overall feeling or mood you want the reader to experience. For example, instead of 'I will describe my school,' a strong thesis might be 'My school felt like a second home, full of warmth and energy.' The dominant impression is 'second home' with 'warmth and energy.'

      Example: Model essay 'Alex's First Impressions of His New School' uses a thesis that gives a clear dominant impression: 'The school was a place of new beginnings, both exciting and intimidating.'

      err-structure

      The student thinks a descriptive essay has no fixed structure or confuses the function of each paragraph and the steps of the writing process.

      Explain: A descriptive essay follows a clear five-paragraph structure: introduction, three body paragraphs, and conclusion. Each part has a specific job. The writing process has four stages: pre-writing (select subject, observe, draft thesis), drafting (organize body paragraphs with sensory details), revision (check alignment, details, structure), and editing (grammar, punctuation, spelling). Ignoring this structure leads to a disorganized essay.

      Example: In 'Alex's First Impressions of His New School', the introduction presents the thesis, each body paragraph describes a different sense impression, and the conclusion summarizes the dominant impression. Pre-writing helped Alex observe details; drafting arranged them; revision ensured clarity; editing fixed errors.

      err-dominant-impression

      Not having a clear dominant impression or mixing multiple impressions

      Explain: A descriptive essay aims to create a single, clear dominant impression—the overall feeling or mood the writer wants the reader to experience. This impression should be consistent throughout the essay, guiding the choice of sensory details and figurative language. The thesis statement explicitly states this dominant impression.

      Example: In the model essay 'Alex's First Impressions of His New School', the dominant impression is one of excitement and opportunity. Every sensory detail—the bright hallways, the hum of activity—supports this feeling.
