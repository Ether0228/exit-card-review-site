Q1. Who wrote Romeo and Juliet and when was it first performed?

errorId: err-nd-romeo-juliet-background | Bloom: remember | Scaffold: L1

Achievement: Knowledge and Understanding | Action: interpret | OSSD: Read and demonstrate understanding of a variety of texts for different purposes.

Official: B1 | Node: ESLDO-B-OE01 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/esl912currb.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-1

Misconception: Students may confuse the author with another Elizabethan playwright or the premier date with a later performance.

Remediation → err-nd-romeo-juliet-background | Retest → err-nd-romeo-juliet-background

  1. ✓ A. William Shakespeare, 1597
    Correct! Shakespeare wrote this tragedy early in his career, and it was first performed in 1597.
  2. B. Christopher Marlowe, 1595
    Not quite. The author is William Shakespeare; Marlowe was a different playwright of the same period.
  3. C. William Shakespeare, 1601
    Shakespeare wrote it earlier – the first performance was in 1597, not 1601.
  4. D. Ben Jonson, 1597
    Ben Jonson wrote other plays, but Romeo and Juliet is definitely by William Shakespeare.

Q2. What is the central conflict in Romeo and Juliet?

errorId: err-nd-core-conflict | Bloom: understand | Scaffold: L1

Achievement: Knowledge and Understanding | Action: interpret | OSSD: Read and demonstrate understanding of a variety of texts for different purposes.

Official: B1 | Node: ESLDO-B-OE01 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/esl912currb.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-1

Misconception: Students may think the conflict is between individual characters (e.g., Romeo vs. Tybalt) rather than families.

Remediation → err-nd-core-conflict | Retest → err-nd-core-conflict

  1. A. A war between the cities of Verona and Mantua
    The play is not about a war between cities; it's about a personal feud inside Verona.
  2. ✓ B. A long-standing feud between the Capulet and Montague families
    Yes! The entire story is built on the hatred between the two noble families.
  3. C. Romeo's argument with his father about marriage
    Romeo's father is not a major source of conflict; the family feud is the driving force.
  4. D. A rivalry between two schools in Verona
    There are no schools involved; it's a deadly family rivalry.

Q4. According to the lesson, what is the main theme of Romeo and Juliet?

  1. A. Young love always overcomes every obstacle
    The play actually shows how love is destroyed by hatred, not how it conquers all.
  2. ✓ B. Meaningless inter-group hatred can lead to irreversible tragedy
    Exactly! The lesson emphasized that Shakespeare uses the lovers' deaths to show the terrible cost of the family feud.

    While friendship is present (Mercutio, Nurse), the central theme is about hatred and its consequences.

    补救模块

    err-nd-romeo-juliet-background

    The student may think Romeo and Juliet was written by another Elizabethan playwright (e.g., Christopher Marlowe or Ben Jonson) or first performed in a later year (e.g., 1601).

    Explain: Romeo and Juliet is a tragedy written by William Shakespeare early in his career. It was first performed in 1597 and is set in Verona, Italy, in the 1590s. Knowing this background helps you connect the play to Shakespeare's time and setting.

    Example: In the lesson, the play was introduced as 'Romeo and Juliet, a tragedy by William Shakespeare, first performed in 1597.' Shakespeare's plays often used Italian settings and themes of family conflict.

    err-nd-core-conflict

    The student may think the central conflict is something other than the feud between the Capulet and Montague families, such as a war between cities, a personal argument, or a rivalry not involving entire households, and may not realize the feud is long-standing, origin unknown, and extends to servants.

    Explain: In Romeo and Juliet, the main conflict is not just a quarrel between two individuals. It is a deep-rooted feud between the Capulet and Montague families in Verona, Italy. The origin of this feud is unknown, but it is so strong that it even extends to the servants of both households. This family hatred drives the whole plot: it forces Romeo and Juliet to marry in secret, causes street brawls, leads to Mercutio's death, gets Romeo exiled, and ultimately ends in a failed fake death plan and a double suicide. The lesson emphasizes that this inter-group hatred has a destructive cost, which is the core theme.

    Example: When Tybalt, a Capulet, kills Mercutio, who is a friend of Romeo, the fight happens because of the family feud. Even though Romeo has just secretly married Juliet, a Capulet, he cannot escape the hatred between the families. That single act of violence leads to Romeo being exiled, which sets the tragedy in motion. The feud makes the lovers' world impossible, and it shows how the conflict affects everyone, from the noble families down to their servants.

    err-nd-plot-summary

    The student recalls pieces of the story but not the order or cause-and-effect links between them, especially how street brawls, Mercutio's death, Romeo's exile, the failed fake death plan, and the double suicide connect.

    Explain: Romeo and Juliet are star-crossed lovers from two feuding families. They marry in secret. Street brawls break out, leading to Mercutio's death and Romeo's exile. Later, a fake death plan fails, and the play ends in the double suicide of Romeo and Juliet. The plot moves from love to violence to tragedy because of the hatred between the Capulet and Montague families.

    Example: Think about the chain: Romeo and Juliet secretly marry → street brawls lead to Mercutio's death → Romeo is exiled → the fake death plan fails → Romeo and Juliet both die. Each event causes the next one.

    err-nd-theme

    The student sees Romeo and Juliet mainly as a teenage love story and overlooks the play's central message about the destructive consequences of inter-group hatred.

    Explain: In Romeo and Juliet, the most important theme is the irreversible destructive cost of meaningless inter-group hatred. Though Romeo and Juliet fall in love, their families—the Capulets and Montagues—are locked in a deep feud. That hatred leads to street brawls, Mercutio's death, Romeo's exile, a failed fake-death plan, and finally the double suicide of the lovers. Shakespeare is not saying love conquers all; he is showing that hatred destroys young love and causes tragedy.

    Example: Chenpin shared that the story conveys inter-group hatred destroys young love and impulsive decisions lead to danger. For example, the audience knows Juliet is only asleep, but Romeo doesn't—the feud and missed message cause both to die. The lesson's core fact is the theme: reflection on the irreversible destructive cost of meaningless inter-group hatred, not just a teenage love story.

    err-nd-literary-terms

    The student may confuse soliloquy, aside, and dramatic irony because they all involve audience knowledge, or mix up the definitions of monologue, comic relief, and other terms.

    Explain: In Romeo and Juliet, Shakespeare uses five key dramatic terms. A monologue is a long speech by one character to others on stage. A soliloquy is a speech by a character alone on stage, revealing inner thoughts. An aside is a short remark heard only by the audience, not by other characters. Comic relief is a humorous moment that lightens a serious scene. Dramatic irony occurs when the audience knows something important that a character does not.

    Example: In Act 5, the audience knows Juliet is only asleep, but Romeo believes she is dead—this is dramatic irony. Romeo speaking alone about his feelings is a soliloquy. Friar Laurence's long speech about plants is a monologue. The Nurse's jokes after the fight provide comic relief.

    err-nd-assignment-requirements

    The student thinks the assignment is simply to write any ending, and does not remember the exact constraints: only Act 5 may be rewritten, Romeo and Juliet and one extra character must appear, at least 5 dialogue lines are needed, one literary device must be used, the length must be 300–500 words, and 3 supporting questions must be answered.

    Explain: In this assignment you rewrite only Act 5 of Romeo and Juliet. You may choose a happy ending, an alternative tragedy, or a twist ending. Your rewrite must include Romeo, Juliet, and at least one extra character. You must have at least 5 lines of dialogue and use at least one literary device: symbolism, dramatic irony, or foreshadowing. The whole rewrite must be 300–500 words. After the rewrite, you must answer 3 supporting questions about theme, moral lesson, or character behaviour, using 2–3 sentences each. Submit your assignment as a single file to Dropbox; extensions are not allowed without serious circumstances.

    Example: For a happy ending, Friar Laurence's message reaches Romeo in time. He arrives at the tomb just as Juliet wakes. They embrace, and Romeo tells the Prince that the feud must end. The rewrite includes Romeo, Juliet, Friar Laurence, and 8 lines of dialogue. It uses dramatic irony: the audience knows Juliet's 'death' is fake, but Romeo does not. The word count is 450. After the rewrite, the student answers 3 questions: one about how the theme changes, one about the moral lesson, and one about Friar Laurence's decisions, each in 2–3 sentences.
