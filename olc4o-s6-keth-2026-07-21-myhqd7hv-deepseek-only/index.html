Q1. A magazine article about electric cars uses technical terms like 'regenerative braking' and 'lithium-ion battery cycle life'. Which of the following is the most likely intended audience?

    Technical terms suggest the magazine is not written for a general audience; specific knowledge is assumed.
  2. ✓ B. Mechanics and engineers, because the article uses automotive jargon
    Correct. The specialized vocabulary and topic point to readers with technical background in cars or batteries.

  4. D. Environmental activists, because the topic is green technology
    Environmental activists might read it, but the technical focus on battery chemistry suggests a more engineering-oriented audience.

Q2. A newspaper editorial argues that a new highway should be built to reduce traffic, but also provides data on construction costs and environmental impact. Which purpose(s) does this text most likely serve?

Official: B2 | Node: OLC4O-B-OE02 | Source: https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/english12curr.pdf

Learning Goal: lg-2

Misconception: Student thinks a text can have only one purpose, missing that editorial can both persuade and inform.

Remediation → err-purpose-single | Retest → err-purpose-single

  1. A. Only to inform, because it includes data
    The text includes an argument (should be built), so it also aims to persuade, not just inform.
  2. B. Only to persuade, because it argues for the highway
    While it persuades, the inclusion of factual data shows it also informs; it serves both purposes.
  3. ✓ C. Both to inform and to persuade
    Correct. The editorial gives information (costs, impact) while advocating a position, so it has mixed purposes.
  4. D. To analyze, because it weighs pros and cons
    Analyzing involves balanced examination; this text clearly takes a side, making it more persuasive than analytical.

Q3. When analyzing a news report from a foreign country about a local protest, which of the following context elements is MOST important to consider for understanding the text's reliability?

  1. A. The reporter's personal opinion about the protest
    Personal opinion is relevant but not the primary context element; more important is how the news is framed by cultural or political factors.

    Correct. Cultural and political context helps explain biases, assumptions, and what is emphasized or omitted.

  4. D. The date the article was published
    Date is relevant for timeliness but does not directly address reliability; cultural factors are deeper.

补救模块

err-audience-universal

Students may mistakenly think there is a single universal audience for any text, ignoring the need to identify specific target readers.

Explain: No text is written for everyone. Every non-fiction text targets a specific audience based on its purpose and context. A magazine article with technical terms like 'regenerative braking' and 'lithium-ion battery cycle life' is not meant for the general public but for readers with technical knowledge, such as mechanics or engineers. Identifying the audience helps you choose appropriate language, examples, and length in your own writing.

Example: Consider an article about electric cars using terms like 'regenerative braking'. If you assume it's for everyone, you miss that the jargon points to an audience of engineers or enthusiasts. A children's book on the same topic would use simpler words and pictures, showing that audience determines content.

err-purpose-single

Students may think every text serves only one purpose, ignoring that many texts combine informing, persuading, and analyzing.

Explain: The lesson categorizes writing purposes into three types: to inform, to persuade, and to analyze. However, many non-fiction texts serve mixed purposes. For example, a newspaper editorial that argues for building a new highway (persuade) also provides data on construction costs and environmental impact (inform). Recognizing mixed purposes helps you understand the text more deeply and respond appropriately.

Example: Consider an editorial: 'We must build the new highway to reduce traffic. Construction costs are estimated at $50 million and the environmental impact includes noise pollution.' This text both persuades (argues for action) and informs (provides facts).

err-context-neglect

Students think context is just the topic or setting, ignoring deeper factors like cultural background, professional circles, and text legitimacy.

Explain: Context analysis goes beyond the topic. It includes checking the text's legitimacy (is it from a credible source?), time required for the task (when was it written and for what deadline?), professional circles of the reader (what specialized knowledge do they have?), and cultural background (what values or assumptions shape the text?). These factors affect how the text should be interpreted and what inferences are valid.

Example: A news report about a protest in a foreign country may use words like 'riot' or 'demonstration'. The cultural context (e.g., media freedom, government stance) influences which word is chosen and what bias exists. Ignoring this could lead to a misinterpretation of the event's severity.
